History,from below.

The great changes in history are usually credited to kings, presidents and parliaments. This atlas tells the stories of the weavers, strikers, mothers, students and farm labourers who actually made them happen — and of what it cost them.

Hover over a point on the map for a summary, or click to read the full story. Every story follows the same structure, and every account of what happened is fully referenced.

The stories

Why "from below"?

Someone always has to stand in the field, walk off the job, or refuse to move.

"History from below" is an approach associated with historians such as E.P. Thompson, who set out to rescue ordinary people from what he called "the enormous condescension of posterity". Rather than beginning with those at the top, it asks who was in the crowd, what they earned, what they feared and hoped for, and how they organised.

Each story here includes famous names, but it also tries to bring forward the people who did the unglamorous work: collecting signatures, running strike committees, carrying banners and facing arrest. That is where change has usually come from.

How each story works

Every event follows the same seven-part structure, so that they can be read and compared side by side:

  1. What is the background to this event?
  2. Who were the key people?
  3. What happened?  — fully referenced
  4. What happened next?
  5. Why does it matter?
  6. What other events are like this?
  7. Where can I find out more?
