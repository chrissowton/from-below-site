Reject Finance Bill: Kenya's Gen Z Protests

Young Kenyans organised online against a bill that would have taxed bread and sanitary pads. They forced the president to withdraw it, but at least 60 people were killed in 2024, and dozens more when protests returned in 2025.

When

  • Whether anyone will be convicted for the killings of protesters

This is recent history. Accounts, casualty figures and outcomes may change as more evidence emerges.

01

What is the background to this event?

By 2024, Kenya was struggling with heavy public debt, and the government planned to raise more revenue through taxes.1

  • The Finance Bill 2024 proposed, among other measures, 16% VAT on ordinary bread and an "eco levy" on imported goods including sanitary towels and nappies.1
  • It also proposed letting the tax authority access people's financial records, including mobile-money statements, without a court order.1
  • Protests against the 2023 Finance Bill, led by the opposition politician Raila Odinga, had already left people dead.1
  • Many young Kenyans faced high living costs and few jobs, and saw politicians as wasteful and corrupt.13

03

What happened?

From May 2024, young people campaigned against the bill online.1

  • They shared explainers, translated the bill into local languages, and posted MPs' phone numbers so that people could message them directly. The hashtags #RejectFinanceBill2024 and #OccupyParliament spread widely.1
  • Street protests began in Nairobi on 18 June and spread to many counties. Parliament removed some clauses but went on to pass the bill.1
  • On 25 June 2024, protesters broke into the Parliament buildings and set part of the complex on fire. Police opened fire, and people were killed.1
  • President Ruto called the events "treasonous" and said the protests had been "hijacked by dangerous people". The army was deployed to support the police.1
  • On 26 June, Ruto announced that he would not sign the bill. Protests continued, with many now calling for his resignation.13
  • On 11 July 2024, Ruto dismissed almost his entire cabinet. He later brought several members of Raila Odinga's opposition party into government.1

The Kenya National Commission on Human Rights recorded at least 60 deaths during the protests of June and July 2024. It also documented 74 people forcibly disappeared between June and November, 26 of whom were still missing in November 2024, and 25 cases of injuries to security officers caused by protesters.2

In June 2025, the death of Albert Ojwang in police custody set off new protests.4 On the anniversary, 25 June 2025, the KNCHR recorded 19 deaths.5 On Saba Saba Day, 7 July 2025, which marks the 1990 movement for multiparty democracy, it recorded 38 deaths by 11 July.6 Police gave much lower figures.10

04

What happened next?

  • The bill: the Finance Bill 2024 never became law.13
  • Abductions: human rights groups reported a wave of abductions and enforced disappearances of government critics in the months after the protests. President Ruto denied government involvement.211
  • 2025: new protests in June and July 2025 were again met with lethal force. On 25 June 2025, the communications regulator ordered broadcasters to stop live coverage of the protests.7
  • Justice: inquests and prosecutions have moved slowly. In the inquest into Rex Masai's death, an investigator told the court in 2026 that the officer who fired the fatal shot had not been identified.8
05

What do others say?

At the time

Protesters saw the bill as unfair to the poor and the government as unaccountable. The president called the storming of Parliament "treasonous" and said criminals had hijacked peaceful protests.1

Debates since

  • Leaderless protest: the movement's lack of leaders made it hard to arrest or co-opt, but also hard to negotiate with or to turn into lasting political change.1
  • Violence: human rights bodies blame most deaths on police use of live ammunition. The authorities point to arson, looting and attacks on officers, and the KNCHR also documented injuries to police.23
  • Counting the dead: figures differ between the KNCHR, rights groups and the police, and some cases of the missing remain unresolved.210

08

Why does it matter?

The Gen Z protests showed how a generation with no single leader or party could use social media to understand a complicated law, organise across the country, and force a government to back down. They also showed the high price of protest when police use lethal force and few are held to account.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did a finance bill provoke such strong anger among young people?
  2. How did social media change the way the protests were organised?
  3. Why do casualty figures from human rights bodies and the police differ so much?
  4. Was the storming of Parliament a turning point for the movement, or a setback?

What other events are like this?

  • 2024The July Uprising in Bangladesh — A student protest over government job quotas grew into a nationwide uprising. The crackdown killed as many as 1,400 people, and on 5 August 2024, Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina fled the country after fifteen years in power.
  • 2025Nepal's Gen Z Uprising — When the government banned 26 social media platforms, young Nepalis angry at corruption and 'nepo kids' took to the streets. Police shot protesters dead, the parliament was burned, and the prime minister resigned within two days.
  • 1952The Mau Mau Uprising — An armed rebellion over land and freedom was met with mass detention and torture. Decades later, the British government paid compensation to survivors.
