Nicaragua's April Uprising

Pensioners and students protesting against cuts to social security were attacked by police and pro-government groups. The protests grew into a national uprising against Daniel Ortega — and the crackdown killed hundreds.

When

Where

01

What is the background to this event?

Daniel Ortega, a leader of the Sandinista revolution that overthrew the Somoza dictatorship in 1979, had returned to power as president in 2007.2

  • Critics accused him of weakening term limits and of building a corrupt electoral system. Protests had begun in 2014 against plans for an interoceanic canal.1
  • On 16 April 2018, demonstrators in Managua protested against the government's slow response to forest fires in the Indio Maíz nature reserve.1
  • On 18 April, the government announced social security reforms that raised payroll taxes and cut pensions by 5%.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Protests by pensioners, students and other activists broke out in Managua and six other cities. Police and paramilitary groups attacked them.13

  • Within five days, at least 26 people had been killed, including the journalist Ángel Gahona. On 22 April, Ortega cancelled the reforms.1
  • But the killings turned the protests into a movement demanding Ortega's resignation. Students occupied universities and built barricades.3
  • By 26 April, at least 63 people had been killed, mostly by gunshot.1 The Catholic bishops called for an end to police violence and mediated a national dialogue; the government said the opposition's agenda amounted to a coup.13
  • On 30 May, Nicaragua's Mothers' Day, the Mothers of April led a march in memory of the dead. It was attacked, and over a dozen people were killed. The bishops suspended the dialogue.3
  • Protests and arrests continued into 2019. Political prisoners were held in La Modelo prison; some were released in June 2019.1

Estimates of the total number killed vary widely, from 325 to 568 by April 2019.3

04

What happened next?

  • Exile: the crackdown and the restriction of civil liberties led to waves of emigration, with more than 30,000 Nicaraguans seeking asylum in Costa Rica.2
  • Repression: rallies were restricted, and many critics, opposition leaders and journalists fled the country in 2021.12
05

What do others say?

At the time

The protesters called for justice for the dead and for Ortega to go. The government, and allies such as Venezuela's president, called the protests a 'terrorist and coup' plot.3

Debates since

  • Who was responsible? The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights sent a delegation, and investigators reported that most victims died from accurate shots to the head, neck and chest; the government blamed the opposition for the violence.3
  • Violence: estimates of deaths range from 325 to 568 by April 2019, most of them protesters.3

How is it remembered?

  • The Mothers of April Movement led the march of 30 May 2018, Nicaragua's Mothers' Day, in memory of the dead.3
08

Why does it matter?

The April Uprising was the largest challenge to Daniel Ortega's government, and its crushing marked Nicaragua's turn to closed authoritarian rule — under a leader who had himself been a revolutionary against a dictatorship.12
