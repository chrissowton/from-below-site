Armenia's Velvet Revolution

When Serzh Sargsyan tried to stay in power by moving from president to prime minister, an opposition MP began a walk across Armenia. Weeks of peaceful protest and street blockades forced Sargsyan to resign.

When
April – May 2018
Where
Republic Square, Yerevan, and across Armenia

01

What is the background to this event?

Serzh Sargsyan was president of Armenia from 2008 to 2018. His Republican Party had been in power since 1999.1

  • A 2015 referendum moved power from the president to the prime minister.
  • When his second term as president ended, Sargsyan was nominated as prime minister — a move critics saw as a way to rule for longer.1
  • Many Armenians were angry about corruption, poverty and emigration.

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 31 March 2018, Nikol Pashinyan began a walk from Gyumri to Yerevan, calling on people to "Reject Serzh".1

  • Protests grew in Yerevan in April. Students walked out of classes, and protesters blocked streets and government buildings.1
  • On 17 April, parliament elected Sargsyan prime minister anyway. The protests grew larger.1
  • Protesters used decentralised tactics, blocking roads with dancing, music and parked cars.
  • On 22 April, Pashinyan was arrested after a televised meeting with Sargsyan, and held overnight.1
  • On 23 April, Pashinyan was released, and Sargsyan resigned, saying: "Nikol Pashinyan was right."1
  • After a failed first vote, parliament elected Pashinyan prime minister on 8 May.1

04

What happened next?

  • Elections: Pashinyan's alliance won a landslide in the December 2018 election.
  • War: in 2020, Armenia lost a war with Azerbaijan over Nagorno-Karabakh. In 2023, Azerbaijan took the region, and almost all its Armenian population fled.
  • Pashinyan survived protests demanding his resignation, and won another election in 2021.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government warned of chaos; protesters insisted on non-violence. Russia, Armenia's ally, stayed neutral.1

Debates since

  • Peaceful: there were scuffles and arrests, and police used stun grenades on one day, but no one was killed.2
  • After the revolution: supporters credit Pashinyan with reducing corruption; critics blame him for the defeats of 2020 and 2023.

How is it remembered?

  • The slogan "Reject Serzh" became a symbol of the movement.
08

Why does it matter?

The Velvet Revolution was a rare example in the former Soviet Union of a long-ruling elite removed by peaceful protest without a split in the security forces.

  • Its tactics — walking, blocking streets, keeping it festive — kept it non-violent.

What other events are like this?

  • 19899 April 1989: The Tbilisi Massacre — Thousands of Georgians gathered peacefully in the heart of Tbilisi to demand independence. Before dawn, Soviet soldiers attacked them with spades and gas, killing 21 people, most of them young women.
  • 2014The Burkinabè Uprising — When Blaise Compaoré tried to change the constitution to stay in power after 27 years, hundreds of thousands took to the streets. Within three days, the parliament was on fire and the president had fled.
  • 2020The Belarusian Protests of 2020 — When Alexander Lukashenko claimed 80 per cent of the vote after 26 years in power, Belarusians came out in the largest protests in their history. Three women led the challenge. Tens of thousands were arrested.
