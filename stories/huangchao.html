The Huang Chao Rebellion

A salt smuggler who had failed the imperial examinations led an army of the poor across China and captured the Tang capital. The rebellion was crushed, but the Tang dynasty never recovered.

When
874 – 884
Where
Chang'an (Xi'an), China
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

By the 870s, the Tang dynasty was weakened by regional warlords, heavy taxes, drought and famine.1

  • The state controlled the trade in salt and taxed it heavily, so salt smuggling was widespread.1
  • Huang Chao came from a family of salt traders and had repeatedly failed the imperial examinations.1
  • In 874–875, a rebellion broke out under Wang Xianzhi, another salt smuggler, and Huang Chao joined it.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Huang Chao

    Salt smuggler and rebel leader who proclaimed himself emperor of Qi.

  • Wang Xianzhi

    The first leader of the rebellion, killed in 878.

  • Li Keyong

    A Shatuo Turk general whose cavalry defeated the rebels for the Tang.

  • Peasants and the poor

    Who swelled the rebel armies.

03

What happened?

After Wang Xianzhi was killed in 878, Huang Chao led the rebel army on a long march across China.1

  • In 879, the rebels took the southern port of Guangzhou.1
  • They turned north and, in early 881, captured Chang'an, the Tang capital. The emperor fled.1
  • Huang Chao declared himself emperor of a new Qi dynasty.1
  • The Tang court recruited Li Keyong, whose forces drove the rebels from Chang'an in 883.1
  • Huang Chao died in 884 — killed by his own nephew, according to the main accounts.1
04

What happened next?

  • The fall of the Tang: the dynasty survived only in name, and ended in 907.
  • Division: China split into the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms.
05

Why does it matter?

The Huang Chao rebellion shows how poverty, taxation and a closed elite could drive a revolt that broke an empire.

  • It is also a story of terrible violence against civilians.
  • After the rebellion the Tang dynasty survived only in name, and it ended in 907.
  • China split into the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms, a half-century of division.
  • A poem about chrysanthemums attributed to Huang Chao is still widely known in China.

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

Official histories, written by the victors, portray Huang Chao as a bandit.2

Debates since

  • Peasant revolution? Twentieth-century Chinese Marxist historians celebrated him as a peasant leader; others stress his violence and ambition.2
  • Violence on all sides: Arab and Persian sources say the rebels massacred many tens of thousands of foreign merchants at Guangzhou (the figures are probably exaggerated).1 Rebels killed members of the elite in Chang'an, and Tang and allied forces plundered and killed in turn.
