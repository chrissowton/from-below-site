Timișoara and the Romanian Revolution

It began with parishioners protecting their pastor from eviction in Timișoara. Within ten days, the most repressive regime in Eastern Europe had fallen — the only one in 1989 to fall in blood.

When
16 – 25 December 1989
Where
Timișoara and Bucharest, Romania
Facts checked
2026-11-15

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Nicolae Ceaușescu had ruled Romania since 1965. His secret police, the Securitate, was among the most feared in the Eastern Bloc.1

  • To repay foreign debts, Ceaușescu imposed severe austerity in the 1980s, causing shortages of food, heating and electricity.1
  • By December 1989, communist governments had fallen in Poland, Hungary, East Germany, Czechoslovakia and Bulgaria.
  • In Timișoara, the authorities tried to evict László Tőkés, a pastor of the Hungarian Reformed Church who had criticised the regime.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 16 December 1989, parishioners and supporters formed a human chain around László Tőkés's home in Timișoara. The protest grew into an anti-government demonstration.1

  • On 17 December, soldiers and security forces fired on protesters in Timișoara, killing and wounding many.13
  • Protests continued, and by 20 December the army had withdrawn and Timișoara was declared a free city.3
  • On 21 December, Ceaușescu addressed a mass rally in Bucharest. The crowd began to boo, and the broadcast was cut.1
  • Protests broke out in Bucharest. After the defence minister died, the army changed sides.1
  • On 22 December, crowds stormed the party headquarters, and the Ceaușescus fled by helicopter. They were captured, tried by a hastily assembled military court and executed on 25 December.1
  • For several days, confused fighting continued between soldiers, civilians and alleged Securitate "terrorists".1

Over a thousand people were killed in the revolution, most of them after Ceaușescu fell.1

04

What happened next?

  • The new government: the National Salvation Front, led by former communists, won elections in 1990.
  • Mineriads: in June 1990, miners brought to Bucharest by the government violently broke up opposition protests.
  • Trials: in 2019, Ion Iliescu and others were charged with crimes against humanity over the deaths after 22 December; the case has dragged on.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The new government blamed the deaths after 22 December on Securitate "terrorists" loyal to Ceaușescu.1

Debates since

  • Revolution or coup? Some argue that the people's revolution was hijacked by second-rank communists who used it to take power.3
  • The "terrorists": no evidence has been found of an organised Securitate counter-attack; many deaths may have come from confusion and friendly fire.1
  • The trial: the Ceaușescus' summary trial and execution have been widely criticised, even by those who opposed them.
06

The evidence

  • Television footage, including the broadcast of 21 December.
  • Investigations by Romanian prosecutors.

07

How is it remembered?

  • Timișoara calls itself the first free city of Romania; a memorial museum tells the story.
08

Why does it matter?

The revolution showed that even the most repressive regime could fall when ordinary people lost their fear.

  • It began with an act of solidarity across ethnic lines.
  • Its violent and confused ending still divides Romanians.

What other events are like this?

  • 1968Prague 1968: Resisting the Tanks — When half a million Warsaw Pact troops invaded to crush 'socialism with a human face', Czechs and Slovaks resisted without weapons — removing road signs, broadcasting from secret radios and arguing with soldiers in the streets.
  • 1989The Baltic Way — Around two million people joined hands in a human chain 675 kilometres long, across Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, to demand freedom from the Soviet Union.
  • 1956The Hungarian Revolution of 1956 — Students, workers and soldiers rose against Hungary's Soviet-backed dictatorship and pulled down Stalin's statue. For twelve days they won — until Soviet tanks returned.
