The Satnami Revolt

A community of farmers and artisans, many of them low caste, who rejected caste and idol worship rose against the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb. They took a town and defeated local forces before a large army crushed them.

When
1672
Where
Narnaul, Haryana, India

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The Satnami movement was founded by Bir Bhan in Narnaul in 1657–58.1

  • Satnamis rejected caste and idol worship, and repeated the name of God, "Satnam".1
  • Many were farmers, artisans and traders of low caste; the movement is connected to Dalits.1
  • They shaved their heads and wore white.1
  • Under Aurangzeb, Mughal taxes and pressure on non-Muslims were increasing.
02

Who were the key people?

  • Bir Bhan

    Founder of the Satnami movement.

  • The Satnamis of Narnaul

    Farmers and artisans who rose up.

  • Aurangzeb

    The Mughal emperor, who sent an army against them.

03

What happened?

In 1672, according to Mughal accounts, a quarrel between a Satnami farmer and a Mughal soldier escalated into a revolt.12

  • The Satnamis defeated local Mughal forces and took control of Narnaul.
  • The revolt spread through the surrounding countryside, and alarmed the court at Delhi.
  • Aurangzeb sent a large army with artillery. In a fierce battle, the Satnamis were defeated and thousands were killed.2

Sources

04

What happened next?

  • Survival: the Satnami tradition survived, and a later Satnami movement was founded by Ghasi Das in Chhattisgarh in 1820.1
  • Unrest: peasant and religious revolts against the Mughals grew in the following decades.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Mughal chroniclers described the Satnamis as low and rebellious; the movement's own records are few.2

Debates since

  • Religion or class? Historians such as Irfan Habib see an agrarian revolt against heavy taxation; others stress religious persecution.2
  • Violence: the revolt involved fighting on both sides; casualty figures come from Mughal sources.2
06

The evidence

  • Mughal chronicles, including the Maasir-i-Alamgiri.
07

How is it remembered?

  • Satnami communities remember the revolt as part of their history of resistance.
08

Why does it matter?

The Satnami revolt was a rare rising by low-caste peasants against one of the world's greatest empires.

  • It shows the link between religious equality and resistance to oppression.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did the Satnamis reject caste?
  2. Why did a quarrel turn into a revolt?
  3. Why was the emperor so alarmed?
  4. How have later Dalit movements remembered the Satnamis?

What other events are like this?

  • 1450Kabir, the Weaver Poet — A weaver from Varanasi mocked the priests and mullahs alike and sang that God belonged to no caste or religion. Threatened by both Hindus and Muslims in his lifetime, he was claimed by both after his death.
  • 1710Banda Singh Bahadur and Land to the Tillers — A Sikh commander raised an army of peasants against Mughal rule in Punjab. He abolished the great landlords' estates and gave the land to those who farmed it — before he was captured and tortured to death in Delhi.
  • 1075The Kaivarta Revolt — Led by a chieftain named Divya, the Kaivartas — a community of fishermen, boatmen and farmers treated as low caste — rose against the Pala king of Bengal, killed him and ruled their own state for about half a century.
