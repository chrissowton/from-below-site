The Jiu Valley Miners' Strike

35,000 coal miners stopped work and demanded that Ceaușescu himself come to hear them. He came, promised everything — and then took his revenge.

When
1 – 3 August 1977
Where
Lupeni, Jiu Valley, Romania
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1977, Romania was ruled by Nicolae Ceaușescu's communist regime.

  • A new law ended disability pensions for miners and raised their retirement age from 50 to 55.1
  • Miners also faced long hours, unpaid overtime, Sunday working, low pay and poor living conditions.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 1 August 1977, 35,000 of the Jiu Valley's 90,000 miners stopped work at Lupeni and nearby mines.1

  • They drew up a list of 17 demands and insisted that Ceaușescu come in person.1
  • When officials came from Bucharest, the miners booed them and would not let them go.1
  • On 3 August, Ceaușescu came to Lupeni, and in front of thousands of miners promised to meet their demands and that there would be no reprisals.1
04

What happened next?

  • Revenge: the Securitate (secret police) moved in. Fifteen strikers were jailed, more than 300 were internally deported, and almost 4,000 were moved or fired.1
  • Leaders: the main strike leaders disappeared within weeks.1
05

Why does it matter?

The Jiu Valley strike was the largest protest against Romania's communist regime before 1989.1

  • It showed workers challenging a "workers' state".
  • The secret police moved in: fifteen strikers were jailed, and almost 4,000 were moved or fired.1
  • The main strike leaders disappeared within weeks.1

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

The regime kept the strike out of the press; news reached the outside world slowly.3

Debates since

  • Myths: some stories about the strike, such as claims of mass killings, are disputed or seen as legend.1
  • Violence: some miners roughed up local party officials before the strike; the regime's reprisals, by the Securitate, were systematic.1
How do we know?The evidence, and a source from the time
  • Securitate files opened after 1989.3
  • Testimony of strike leaders such as Dobre.1

Ceaușescu visits Lupeni in November 1977, three months after the strike. In public, the regime showed the leader among loyal miners; in private, strikers were moved, watched and punished.

Reading it

Ceaușescu visits Lupeni three months after the strike, surrounded by loyal miners. This is the public face of the regime's response. In private, strikers were moved, watched and punished. What the miners really thought had to wait until after 1989 to be told.

How is it remembered?Memorials, anniversaries and arguments over memory
  • The strike is remembered in Romania as an early act of resistance to Ceaușescu.
