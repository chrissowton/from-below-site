People Power: Bobi Wine and Uganda's 2021 Election

A popular singer became the voice of young Ugandans against a president in power since 1986. His arrest in November 2020 set off protests in which around 100 people were reported killed, and the election was held under an internet shutdown.

When
2018 – January 2021
Where
Kampala and towns across Uganda

01

What is the background to this event?

Yoweri Museveni had been president of Uganda since 1986.2

  • Bobi Wine, a popular musician, was elected to parliament in 2017.1
  • In 2018 he campaigned for opposition candidates who won several by-elections, and popularised the slogan 'People Power, Our Power'.1
  • During a by-election in Arua in August 2018, after stones were allegedly thrown at the president's convoy, Bobi Wine said police had shot at his car and killed his driver. Museveni blamed Bobi Wine for the incident.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Bobi Wine stood for president in the election of January 2021.1

  • During the campaign, several of his aides, bodyguards and members of his team were arrested, jailed and killed.2
  • On 18 November 2020, he was arrested for allegedly breaking COVID-19 rules. The arrest set off protests in which, according to reports, about 100 people died and more than 500 were injured.2
  • On 13 January 2021, the day before the vote, the government ordered a complete internet shutdown, after Facebook closed accounts it said were linked to the government.2
  • Museveni was declared the winner with about 58% of the vote to Bobi Wine's 35%. Bobi Wine called it the most fraudulent election in Uganda's history, and security forces surrounded his home.2

04

What happened next?

  • Arrests: Bobi Wine was arrested again on his return to Uganda in October 2023.1
  • 2026: he stood again in January 2026. Museveni was declared the winner with about 72% in an election that Human Rights Watch said was marred by abuses, with thousands of opposition supporters detained and the internet shut down again.3
05

What do others say?

At the time

Bobi Wine's supporters saw a fight for freedom against a dictatorship; the government accused the opposition of violence and the internet companies of taking sides.2

Debates since

  • Elections without change? Uganda holds regular elections, but no second round has ever taken place and critics say the electoral process is controlled by the president.3
  • Violence: the death toll of the November 2020 protests is disputed; the figure in reports cited by Wikipedia is about 100.2
06

The evidence

  • Bobi Wine's own videos and livestreams, press reports, and the Peabody Award-winning documentary Bobi Wine: The People's President.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • The slogan 'People Power, Our Power' became the rallying cry of Bobi Wine's movement.1
08

Why does it matter?

Bobi Wine's movement showed how a popular musician could mobilise a generation born after Museveni came to power — and how a long-ruling government could use arrests, force and internet shutdowns to hold on.12

Questions to think about

  1. Why did a musician become the leader of an opposition movement?
  2. Why might young Ugandans in particular support People Power?
  3. Why did the government shut down the internet?
  4. How can a government stay in power for decades while holding elections?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 2011Uganda's Walk to Work Protests — With fuel and food prices soaring, Ugandans were asked to protest in the simplest way possible: by walking to work. The government answered with tear gas, arrests and live bullets.
  • 2024Reject Finance Bill: Kenya's Gen Z Protests — Young Kenyans organised online against a bill that would have taxed bread and sanitary pads. They forced the president to withdraw it, but at least 60 people were killed in 2024, and dozens more when protests returned in 2025.
  • 2016#ThisFlag — A pastor posted a video with the national flag around his neck, asking what its colours meant when the country was broken. It went viral, and on 6 July 2016 Zimbabweans stayed at home in a national shutdown.
