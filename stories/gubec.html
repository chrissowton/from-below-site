The Croatian–Slovene Peasant Revolt

Serfs in Croatia and Slovenia rose against a cruel baron and planned a world without nobles, with peasant officials answerable only to the emperor. They were crushed in twelve days.

When
28 January – 15 February 1573
Where
Stubičko polje, Hrvatsko Zagorje, Croatia
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the late sixteenth century, the threat of Ottoman raids strained the economy, and feudal lords kept increasing their demands on peasants.1

  • In Croatian Zagorje, Baron Ferenc Tahy treated his serfs cruelly and fought with neighbouring barons over land.1
  • When complaints to the emperor went unanswered, the peasants planned a revolt with peasants in Styria and Carniola and with poorer townspeople.1

03

What happened?

On 28 January 1573, the revolt broke out at the same time in Carniola, Styria and Croatia.1

  • The rebels wanted to replace the nobility with peasant officials answerable to the emperor, and to abolish feudal holdings and obligations to the Church.1
  • A peasant government was formed, and plans were drawn up to abolish provincial borders, open roads for trade and allow peasant self-rule.1
  • Each household sent one man to the rebel army.1
  • The nobles raised armies, and on 5 and 6 February rebel forces were defeated at Krško and near Samobor.1
  • On 9 February, at Stubičko polje, Gubec and 10,000 peasants were defeated after a four-hour battle.1

04

What happened next?

  • Reprisals: about 3,000 peasants died in the final battle, and many captives were hanged or maimed.1
  • Gubec was publicly tortured and executed on 15 February; Gregorić was captured and executed in 1574.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The nobility saw the revolt as a threat to the whole social order.1

Debates since

  • Leader? Gubec is remembered as the leader, but he was one of several members of the peasant government.1
  • Violence: the rebels attacked castles and manors; the nobles' reprisals were brutal.1
  • Memory: socialist Yugoslavia celebrated the revolt as an early class struggle.2

Johann Weikhard von Valvasor, 1689

An engraving of the 1573 revolt from Valvasor's great history of Carniola, published more than a century later. Valvasor was a nobleman, and his book became one of the best-known early accounts of the revolt.

07

How is it remembered?

  • The revolt has inspired writers including August Šenoa and Miroslav Krleža, the sculptor Antun Augustinčić, and the first Croatian rock opera, Gubec-beg (1975).1
  • A museum and monument stand at Gornja Stubica.
08

Why does it matter?

The revolt had one of the most radical programmes of any peasant rising in Europe.

  • Gubec became a legendary figure in Croatia and Slovenia.

Questions to think about

  • 1525The German Peasants' War and the Twelve Articles — Tens of thousands of peasants rose against their lords, armed with the new ideas of the Reformation and a printed manifesto of rights. It was Europe's largest popular uprising before the French Revolution.
  • 1670Stenka Razin's Rising — A Cossack raider gathered runaway serfs and the poor of the Volga into an army against the nobles and officials of the tsar. He took city after city before being defeated, betrayed and executed in Red Square.
