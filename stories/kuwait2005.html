The Campaign for Women's Votes in Kuwait

For more than forty years, Kuwaiti women campaigned for the vote — through petitions, a strike, mock ballots and demonstrations outside parliament. In May 2005, they won.

When

Where

01

What is the background to this event?

After independence in 1961, Kuwait's new parliament limited the vote to men over 21 whose families had lived in Kuwait since before 1920.1

  • Women from the first university graduates founded the Women's Cultural and Social Society in 1963.1
  • Bills to give women the vote were introduced in 1963, 1985 and 1986, but defeated.1
  • During the Iraqi occupation of 1990–91, women played a major role in resistance and in keeping society running.1

03

What happened?

In 1996, 500 women stopped work for an hour to demand the vote. Demonstrations continued in the years that followed.1

  • In May 1999, the emir issued a decree giving women the right to vote and stand for office. Parliament overturned it six months later.1
  • In the 2003 election, women set up mock ballot boxes, allowing hundreds of women to cast symbolic votes.1
  • In March 2005, about 1,000 people surrounded the parliament.1
  • On 16 May 2005, parliament passed a law granting women the right to vote and stand for election, by 37 votes to 21.1

04

What happened next?

  • The first women MPs: in May 2009, four women — Rola Dashti, Massouma al-Mubarak, Aseel al-Awadhi and Salwa al-Jassar — were elected to parliament.1
  • Setbacks: few women have been elected since, and in 2024 the emir suspended parliament.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Opponents argued that women's suffrage was contrary to tradition or religion; supporters argued it was required by the constitution and by justice.2

Debates since

  • From above or below? Some see the reform as driven by the ruling family and Western pressure; others credit decades of women's campaigning.2
  • Non-violence: the campaign was entirely peaceful.

07

How is it remembered?

  • 16 May is remembered by Kuwaiti women's groups as the day they won the vote.
08

Why does it matter?

Kuwait was the first state in the Gulf with an elected parliament, and women's long campaign there influenced the region.

  • It shows that the vote can be won through patient, peaceful pressure.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did it take more than forty years for Kuwaiti women to win the vote?
  2. Why did parliament overturn the emir's decree in 1999?
  3. Why were mock ballots an effective form of protest?
  4. What does the vote mean when parliament itself can be suspended?
