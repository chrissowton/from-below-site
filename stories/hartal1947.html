The vote & democracy

The All-Malaya Hartal

Malay radicals, Chinese and Indian groups united behind a 'People's Constitution' for an independent, democratic Malaya with equal citizenship for all. On one day, they shut down the country.

When
20 October 1947
Where
Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Singapore and across Malaya

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

After the Second World War, Britain planned a new constitution for Malaya. Its first plan, the Malayan Union, offered equal citizenship to all residents, but was opposed by many Malays and abandoned.1

  • Britain then negotiated in secret with the Malay rulers and the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO) to create a Federation of Malaya.1
  • The new proposals gave the Malay rulers greater power, restricted citizenship for non-Malays, and offered no road map to independence.1
  • Singapore was to remain a separate colony.
02

Who were the key people?

  • Tan Cheng Lock

    A Malacca Chinese leader who chaired the All-Malaya Council of Joint Action (AMCJA).

  • Dr Burhanuddin al-Helmy and Ahmad Boestamam

    Leaders of the Malay Nationalist Party and its radical youth wing, API, who formed the Malay coalition PUTERA.

  • Gerald de Cruz and Philip Hoalim

    Leaders of the Malayan Democratic Union, part of the AMCJA.

  • Workers, traders and shopkeepers

    Who closed their shops and stayed away from work.

03

What happened?

In December 1946, a range of organisations formed the All-Malaya Council of Joint Action (AMCJA), including the Malayan Democratic Union, the Malay Nationalist Party and the Malayan Indian Congress.1 Its principles were:1

  • a united Malaya including Singapore;
  • an elected central government and elected state councils;
  • equal citizenship for all who made Malaya their permanent home.

The AMCJA joined with PUTERA, a coalition of radical Malay groups, and in 1947 they published the "People's Constitutional Proposals" for an independent, democratic Malaya, with Malay as the national language and equal citizenship.

When the British ignored them, the coalition called a nationwide hartal — a general stoppage of work and business — on 20 October 1947. Across Malaya and Singapore, shops closed, workers stayed home and much of the economy came to a halt. It was one of the largest peaceful protests in Malayan history.

04

What happened next?

  • The Federation: the British went ahead with the Federation of Malaya in February 1948, ignoring the People's Constitution.
  • The Emergency: in June 1948, the British declared a state of emergency after the Malayan Communist Party began an armed revolt. Many left-wing organisations were banned, and their leaders arrested, including Ahmad Boestamam.
  • Independence: Malaya became independent in 1957 under a constitution negotiated by the UMNO-led Alliance, closer to the Federation model.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The British and UMNO argued that the Federation protected the Malays and the rulers. The AMCJA–PUTERA coalition argued for equal citizenship and democracy.

Historians' debates

  • Communist influence: the British claimed the coalition was a communist front; historians show that it was a broad alliance in which communists were one element.
  • A lost alternative? Some historians see the People's Constitution as a lost path to a less racially divided Malaysia; others note its limited support among many Malays.
  • The Emergency: the violence of the Emergency, including atrocities on both sides, overshadowed the peaceful movement of 1947.

07

How is it remembered?

  • The People's Constitution was republished in 2005, and activists have marked its anniversaries.
08

Why does it matter?

The hartal of 1947 showed that a multiracial, democratic alternative existed in the struggle for Malayan independence. Its memory has been revived by those who seek a more inclusive Malaysia.

  • It shows the power of peaceful, united action across ethnic lines.
  • It raises questions about which voices shaped decolonisation.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did the AMCJA insist on equal citizenship?
  2. Why was a hartal an effective form of protest?
  3. Why did the British ignore the People's Constitution?
  4. How might Malaysia be different if the People's Constitution had been adopted?

What other events are like this?

  • 1955The Hock Lee Bus Strike — Bus workers sacked for joining a union went on strike, and Chinese-school students came to support them. A night of rioting left four people dead and became a defining moment in Singapore's history.
  • 1930The Salt March — A 24-day march to the sea to make salt in defiance of British law set off a campaign of civil disobedience across India.
  • 1950Positive Action and Ghana's Independence — A campaign of strikes and boycotts landed Kwame Nkrumah in prison — from where he won an election and led the first sub-Saharan African colony to independence.
