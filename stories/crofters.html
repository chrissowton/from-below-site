The Crofters' War

Highland crofters, cleared from their ancestors' land to make way for sheep and deer, refused rent, occupied land and fought the police. Their land league won them the right not to be evicted.

When
1882 – 1886
Where
The Braes and Glendale, Isle of Skye, Scotland
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

After 1746, many Highland people were cleared from their ancestral lands in the Highland Clearances.1

  • Landlords turned most of the land into sheep farms and deer forests.1
  • Those who stayed were crammed onto small crofts, with no security, at the mercy of their landlords.1
  • Many had no croft at all, and lived as cottars and squatters on others' land.1
  • The potato famine had hit the Highlands hard.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In the early 1880s, crofters on Skye began rent strikes and land raids, demanding secure tenure and more land.1

  • In April 1882, at the Braes, crofters fought police sent from Glasgow to serve eviction notices — the "Battle of the Braes".
  • In 1883, the government set up the Napier Commission, but its 1884 report fell far short of the crofters' demands.1
  • Protests spread across the Highlands, and the Highland Land League grew to thousands of members.1
  • The League used rent strikes, boycotts and land occupations.1
  • In 1885, crofters' MPs were elected for four Highland seats.1

04

What happened next?

  • The Crofters Act: the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886 gave crofters security of tenure and fair rents.1
  • Land raids continued into the twentieth century.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Landlords called the crofters lawbreakers; the League's Gaelic slogan said the people are mightier than a lord.1

Debates since

  • Crofters' history: historians such as James Hunter rewrote Highland history from the crofters' point of view.2
  • Violence: crofters fought police with stones and sticks; the state sent police, troops and marines to Skye; there were injuries but few or no deaths.2

How is it remembered?

  • A monument at Glendale on Skye honours the Land Leaguers.
08

Why does it matter?

The Crofters' War won protection from eviction for Highland families, and inspired later land reform in Scotland.12

What other events are like this?

  • 1879The Irish Land War and the First Boycott — Irish tenant farmers organised against evictions and high rents — and gave the world a new word, 'boycott'.
  • 1839The Rebecca Riots — At night, Welsh farmers with blackened faces and women's clothes smashed the toll-gates that taxed every journey to market. They called themselves 'Rebecca and her daughters'.
  • 1549Kett's Rebellion — Villagers tearing down a landlord's fences asked a farmer to join them. He pulled down his own fences and led 16,000 people to camp outside Norwich, where they ran their own affairs for six weeks.
