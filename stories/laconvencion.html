The Peasant Unions of La Convención

Quechua tenant farmers in a coffee-growing valley organised unions, refused unpaid labour and took over the landlords' estates. Their struggle forced Peru's first land reform.

When
1958 – 1963
Where
Quillabamba and the La Convención valley, Cusco, Peru
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the La Convención valley near Cusco, large estates (haciendas) grew coffee, tea and sugar.

  • Quechua tenant farmers had to work unpaid for the landlords in return for small plots of land.
  • Landlords were notorious for abuse of indigenous workers.1
  • In 1958, the Trotskyist agronomy student Hugo Blanco moved to the valley as a subtenant farmer.1

03

What happened?

From 1958, with the help of students, peasant unions organised the tenants against the landlords.1

  • Hugo Blanco built a bridge between Spanish-speaking activists and Quechua-speaking farmers.1
  • Between 1961 and 1963, peasants occupied landlords' lands, and organised to defend them.1
  • In the Chaupimayo area, they set up their own government, courts, defence and schools, and passed their own land reform.1
  • In 1963, Blanco was captured and sentenced to 25 years in prison.1

04

What happened next?

  • Land reform: the government passed a land reform for the valley, and peasants gained much of the land they had occupied.
  • National reform: Peru's sweeping land reform of 1969 followed.
  • Blanco: chosen as Amnesty International's prisoner of the year in 1968, he was freed and deported in 1971.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Landlords and the government saw a communist insurrection; the peasants saw a fight for land and dignity.1

Debates since

  • Leaders and members: historians stress that the movement was built by peasants themselves, not only by Blanco.2
  • Violence: the movement formed armed self-defence groups; a police officer was killed in a clash that Blanco said was self-defence, and the claim against him is disputed; police killed peasants during the repression.1

The evidence

07

How is it remembered?

  • Thousands attended Hugo Blanco's wake in Lima in 2023.
08

Why does it matter?

La Convención showed that indigenous peasants could organise and win back land.

  • Its slogan, "Land or death", spread across Latin America.

What other events are like this?

  • 1780The Rebellion of Túpac Amaru II — An Indigenous leader and his wife, Micaela Bastidas, led the largest rebellion in Spain's American empire, demanding an end to forced labour and abuse.
  • 1990The Indigenous Uprising of 1990 — Indigenous communities blocked roads, occupied a church and shut down Ecuador for a week, demanding land, bilingual education and recognition of a 'plurinational' state. It changed the country.
  • 1911Zapata and the Plan of Ayala — The peasant revolutionary Emiliano Zapata declared that the land, woods and waters taken from Mexico's villages must be returned — a demand that shaped the Mexican Revolution.
