The Defence of Geok Tepe

Teke Turkmen families defended their mud-walled fortress against the Russian Empire. After a long siege, the walls were blown open and thousands were killed — completing Russia's conquest of Central Asia.

When
1879 – January 1881
Where
Gökdepe, Akhal oasis, Turkmenistan
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the nineteenth century, the Russian Empire expanded into Central Asia.1

  • The Teke Turkmen lived in the Akhal oasis, farming land watered by streams from the Kopet Dag mountains.1
  • In 1879, the Teke defeated a Russian expedition at Geok Tepe.1
  • Russia prepared a new campaign under General Mikhail Skobelev.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

By the end of 1880, Skobelev had about 4,000 infantry, 750 cavalry, artillery and machine guns outside Geok Tepe.1

  • About 40,000 Teke were thought to be in the area, many inside the fortress with mud walls 5.5 metres thick.1
  • In January 1881, the Russians cut off the water supply and besieged the fortress.1
  • When the fortress fell, the Russians killed all the Turkmen men who had not escaped, but spared about 5,000 women and children.1
  • Skobelev's own report said 6,500 bodies were buried inside the fortress, and 8,000 people, of both sexes, were killed in the pursuit.1

04

What happened next?

  • Conquest: the battle gave Russia control of most of present-day Turkmenistan and nearly completed its conquest of Central Asia.1
  • Remembrance: the resistance is a source of national pride in Turkmenistan.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Russia presented the conquest as bringing order and ending slave raiding; the Teke fought to keep their independence.2

Debates since

  • Massacre: Skobelev's own figures show mass killing of civilians after the fortress fell.1
  • Violence on both sides: the Teke had raided Persia for captives — the Russians freed about 600 Persian slaves in the fortress; Russian forces killed thousands of Turkmen, including women.1
06

The evidence

  • Skobelev's official report.1
  • Accounts by foreign observers and Turkmen oral tradition.2

07

How is it remembered?

  • Turkmenistan marks a national day of remembrance for the defenders of Geok Tepe, and a large mosque stands at Gökdepe.
08

Why does it matter?

Geok Tepe was the last great stand of the Turkmen against Russian conquest.

  • It shows the mass violence of imperial expansion in Central Asia.

Questions to think about
