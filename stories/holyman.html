The Holy Man's Rebellion

In the highlands of southern Laos and north-east Siam, followers of 'holy men' rose against French and Siamese taxes and forced labour. In Laos, the resistance lasted for more than thirty years.

When
1901 – 1936
Where
Bolaven Plateau, Laos

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

At the end of the nineteenth century, France took control of Laos, and Siam (Thailand) reorganised its north-eastern provinces.1

  • Siam abolished slavery and replaced tribute with a tax on every able-bodied man.1
  • On the Bolaven Plateau, the French imposed chiefs to collect taxes and forced labour for road building.1
  • The highland Lao Theung peoples had long been despised and exploited by lowland rulers.1
  • The Phu Mi Bun ("holy man") movement promised a coming age of justice.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Ong Keo

    Holy man whom the rebels wanted to make king; killed by the French in 1910.

  • Ong Kommadam

    Leader who carried on the resistance until his death in 1936.

  • Ong Man

    Rebel leader in Siam in 1901–02.

  • Lao Theung highland peoples

    Who made up much of the movement.

03

What happened?

In March 1901, followers of the Phu Mi Bun movement began an armed rebellion against French Indochina and Siam.1

  • In Siam, rebels raided the town of Khemmarat, killing two officials and burning and looting the town.1
  • In April 1902, Siamese troops ambushed a rebel column outside Ubon, killing 300 and capturing 400.1
  • The rising was crushed in Siam by 1902, but continued in Laos.1
  • In 1910, Ong Keo was killed by a French official who had hidden a pistol under his hat during peace negotiations.1
  • Resistance continued on the Bolaven Plateau until it was finally suppressed in January 1936.1

04

What happened next?

  • Memory: the Lao communist movement later celebrated the highland rebels as forerunners of its own struggle.2
  • Independence: Laos became independent from France in 1953.
05

What do others say?

At the time

French and Siamese officials saw sorcerers and bandits; the rebels saw a fight for justice and a new age.1

Debates since

  • Religion or resistance? Historians such as Geoffrey Gunn stress the economic grievances behind the millenarian beliefs.2
  • Violence on all sides: rebels killed officials and, in 1905, massacred 41 Loven villagers; Siamese and French forces killed hundreds of rebels.1
06

The evidence

  • French colonial archives and Siamese reports.2
07

How is it remembered?

  • Ong Kommadam is honoured in Laos as a hero of resistance.
08

Why does it matter?

The rebellion was one of the longest anti-colonial movements in Southeast Asia.

  • It was led by marginalised highland peoples against both colonial and local rulers.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did forced labour and new taxes cause revolt?
  2. Why did the rebels follow 'holy men'?
  3. Why did the rebellion last so much longer in Laos than in Siam?
  4. How should we judge the killing of Ong Keo during negotiations?
