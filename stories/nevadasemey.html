Peace & anti-war

Nevada–Semipalatinsk

After 40 years of Soviet nuclear tests near their homes, Kazakhs formed a mass movement led by a poet. Within two years, the test site was closed forever.

When
1989 – 1991
Where
Semey (Semipalatinsk), Kazakhstan
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Between 1949 and 1989, the Soviet Union carried out 456 nuclear tests at the Semipalatinsk Test Site in north-east Kazakhstan.1

  • The United Nations believes about a million people were exposed to radiation.1
  • Rates of birth defects and cancer around Semey are far higher than in the rest of the country.1

03

What happened?

In 1989, Olzhas Suleimenov founded the Nevada–Semipalatinsk movement.1

  • It was one of the first major anti-nuclear movements in the Soviet Union.1
  • Its name showed solidarity with protesters against the US Nevada Test Site.1
  • Thousands joined its protests and campaigns.1

04

What happened next?

  • Closure: the test site was closed in 1991.1
  • Disarmament: after independence, Kazakhstan gave up the nuclear weapons left on its soil.2
  • Recognition: UNESCO has recognised the movement's role in raising awareness of nuclear dangers.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Soviet authorities had long kept the effects of testing secret; the movement demanded truth and an end to tests.1

Debates since

  • Its legacy: Kazakhstan's government has made anti-nuclear policy part of its national identity; some argue the grassroots movement's role is sometimes overshadowed.2
  • Violence: the movement was peaceful; the harm came from decades of testing by the state.1
06

The evidence

  • Medical and environmental studies of the Semipalatinsk region.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • 29 August, the date of the first Soviet test and of the site's closure, is the UN International Day against Nuclear Tests.
08

Why does it matter?

Nevada–Semipalatinsk was a citizens' movement that shut down a nuclear test site.

  • It linked people harmed by nuclear testing across the world.
