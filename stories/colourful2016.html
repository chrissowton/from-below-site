The Colourful Revolution

When the president pardoned politicians accused in a mass phone-tapping scandal, protesters armed themselves with paintballs and paint, splashing government buildings and statues in every colour of the rainbow.

When
April – June 2016
Where
Skopje and other towns, North Macedonia
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Nikola Gruevski was prime minister of Macedonia from 2006 to 2016. His government became increasingly authoritarian.1

  • In 2015, the opposition leader Zoran Zaev released recordings suggesting that the government had illegally wiretapped about 20,000 people.21
  • Large protests followed, and the European Union and United States brokered an agreement in 2015 under which Gruevski resigned and a special prosecutor was appointed.2
  • Gruevski's "Skopje 2014" project had filled the capital with expensive statues and neoclassical buildings.1

03

What happened?

In April 2016, President Gjorge Ivanov halted the investigation of Gruevski and dozens of other politicians involved in the wiretapping scandal.2

  • Protests began in Skopje, organised by the movement Protestiram and supported by opposition parties.2
  • Protesters threw paint at government buildings and the statues of Skopje 2014, giving the movement its name.
  • The protests spread to Bitola, Kumanovo, Tetovo, Veles and other towns. The government's supporters held counter-rallies.2
  • The European Union and United States criticised the pardons.2
  • In June 2016, Ivanov withdrew the pardons, and the planned elections were postponed.
04

What happened next?

  • Elections: after elections in December 2016, Zoran Zaev formed a government in 2017.
  • Storming of parliament: in April 2017, government supporters stormed parliament and attacked opposition MPs, including Zaev.
  • Gruevski was sentenced to two years for corruption in 2018, and fled to Hungary.1
  • Prespa: the new government signed an agreement with Greece, and the country became North Macedonia in 2019.
05

Why does it matter?

The Colourful Revolution showed how creative, largely non-violent protest could challenge an entrenched government.

  • It combined anger at corruption with a critique of nationalist monuments.
  • The former prime minister was sentenced for corruption in 2018 and fled to Hungary.1
  • The new government signed an agreement with Greece, and the country became North Macedonia in 2019.

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

The government said the protests were a foreign-backed attempt at a "Ukraine scenario"; Russia called the opposition a tool of foreign powers.2

Debates since

  • Damage: critics saw the paint attacks as vandalism; supporters saw them as a symbolic, non-violent response to corruption.
  • Violence: the 2016 protests were mainly peaceful; the most serious violence came in 2017, when government supporters attacked MPs.
