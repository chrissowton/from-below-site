May 68: Students, Workers and the Largest Strike in French History

It began with a student protest and police batons at the Sorbonne. Within ten days, about ten million workers — two-thirds of France's workforce — were on strike, and President de Gaulle briefly fled the country.

When
3 May – 23 June 1968
Where
The Sorbonne and the Latin Quarter, Paris, France

01

What is the background to this event?

France had grown rapidly after the Second World War, but inequality and frustration were rising.1

  • Universities were overcrowded and rigid, and many young people rejected conservative social rules.1
  • Workers were unhappy with low wages and poor conditions.1
  • President Charles de Gaulle's government seemed outdated to many.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Student protests began at Nanterre University in March 1968.1

  • After police intervened, Nanterre was closed on 2 May, and the protests moved to the Sorbonne.1
  • On 6 May, police violently dispersed students at the Sorbonne; students built barricades, and on the night of 10 May there were street battles.1
  • By 13 May, the protests had become a general strike. About 10 million workers — two-thirds of the workforce — stopped work.1
  • The government, employers and unions negotiated the Grenelle agreements, raising the minimum wage by 35%, but many workers rejected them.3
  • On 29 May, de Gaulle briefly left for a French base in West Germany; he returned, dissolved parliament and called elections.1
04

What happened next?

  • Elections: in the elections of 23 June, de Gaulle's party won a large victory.1
  • Long-term change: de Gaulle resigned the next year, and May 68 shaped French politics, workplaces and culture for decades.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government warned of revolution and disorder; protesters spoke of freedom and changing everyday life.1

Debates since

  • Revolution or reform? Some see a failed revolution; others see lasting cultural and social change.1
  • Violence on both sides: police used batons and tear gas against students; protesters fought police on the barricades. On 24 May, a police inspector in Lyon was killed by a lorry sent into police lines, and a man was stabbed to death in Paris.3 In June, a school student, Gilles Tautin, drowned while fleeing a charge by gendarmes at Flins, and two workers died when riot police broke up the strike at the Peugeot factory in Sochaux — one shot, one falling from a wall after a grenade blast.4

07

How is it remembered?

  • May 68 is still argued over in France, and its posters and slogans are famous worldwide.
08

Why does it matter?

May 68 showed how a student protest could spark a general strike — and became a symbol of the worldwide revolts of 1968.
