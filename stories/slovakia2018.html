For Ján and Martina: Slovakia's Protests after a Journalist's Murder

An investigative journalist and his fiancée were shot dead in their home. Tens of thousands of Slovaks took to the streets in the largest protests since 1989, and within three weeks the prime minister had resigned.

01

What is the background to this event?

Ján Kuciak, 27, was an investigative reporter for the news website Aktuality.sk. He wrote about tax fraud by businessmen with links to top politicians, including people close to the governing Smer party.1

  • In 2017, the businessman Marian Kočner phoned Kuciak, swore at him and threatened to dig up damaging information on him and his family. Kuciak complained to the police, but they did not speak to him for over a month.1
  • When he was killed, Kuciak was working on a story about Italian businessmen with links to the 'Ndrangheta mafia, and their connections to senior officials.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 21 February 2018, Ján Kuciak and Martina Kušnírová were shot dead in their home in Veľká Mača. Kuciak was the first journalist murdered in independent Slovakia.1

  • On 2 March, up to 25,000 people gathered in Bratislava to remember the couple.1
  • On 9 March, protests were held in 48 Slovak towns and 17 cities abroad. About 60,000 marched in Bratislava — the largest demonstration since the Velvet Revolution of 1989.1
  • Universities, teachers, artists and non-governmental organisations backed the protests.1
  • On 12 March, Interior Minister Robert Kaliňák resigned. On 15 March, Prime Minister Robert Fico and his whole cabinet resigned, and a new government was formed under Peter Pellegrini of the same party.1
  • Two days later, more than 65,000 people protested in Bratislava, demanding early elections.1
04

What happened next?

  • The killers convicted: the former soldier Miroslav Marček admitted shooting the couple and was sentenced to 25 years; his cousin, a former policeman, was also sentenced to 25 years; the man who arranged the killing was sentenced to 15 years.1
  • Who ordered it? Prosecutors accused Marian Kočner of ordering the murder. He was acquitted of the murder charges in 2020; the case has since been sent back for retrial.1
  • Pressure continued: protesters demanded the removal of the police chief; in April 2018 the new interior minister resigned rather than dismiss him.1
05

Why does it matter?

The murder of a young journalist brought Slovaks onto the streets in numbers not seen since 1989. It showed how investigative journalism and public anger together could force a government out.1

  • The man who shot the couple and his accomplice were each sentenced to 25 years.1
  • The businessman accused of ordering the murder was acquitted in 2020, and the case was sent back for retrial.1
  • Marches for Ján and Martina were also held in Czech cities.1

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

The government replaced ministers and then the prime minister, but kept the same governing parties; protesters marched under slogans such as "Early elections".1

Debates since

  • Justice: the gunman and accomplices were convicted, but the question of who ordered the murder has gone through several trials.1
  • Violence: the protests were peaceful.1
