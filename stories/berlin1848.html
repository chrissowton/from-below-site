The Berlin Barricades of March 1848

When news of the revolutions in Paris and Vienna reached Berlin, crowds demanded freedom from the Prussian king. Shots were fired, barricades went up, and after thirteen hours of fighting the army withdrew from the city.

When
18 – 19 March 1848
Where
Alexanderplatz, Berlin, Germany

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1848, Germany was divided into many states, the largest being Austria and Prussia.

  • People faced heavy taxes and political censorship, and called for a united, democratic Germany.1
  • In March 1848, news of the revolution in Paris inspired demonstrations across the German states.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In mid-March, the army charged a crowd returning from a meeting in Berlin's Tiergarten, killing one person and injuring many.1

  • On 18 March, a large demonstration took place. After two shots were fired, people feared the 20,000 soldiers in the city would be used against them.1
  • They built barricades, and fighting lasted thirteen hours until the troops were ordered to withdraw, leaving hundreds dead.1
  • The king promised reforms and approved the arming of citizens.1

04

What happened next?

  • Frankfurt Parliament: an elected parliament met in Frankfurt and in December 1848 declared equal rights for all citizens before the law.1
  • Defeat: the King of Prussia refused the crown offered by the parliament, and the revolution was defeated by force in 1849.1
  • Exile: many democrats — the "Forty-Eighters" — went into exile, many of them to the United States.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Conservatives saw rebellion and disorder; democrats saw the people defending themselves against the army.1

Debates since

  • A failed revolution? Some historians stress failure; others point to the ideas and rights it established.1
  • Violence: the dead of 18 March were mostly civilians killed by the army; armed citizens also fought and killed soldiers.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • Those killed on 18 March, the "March fallen", are buried in Berlin's Friedrichshain, and a square by the Brandenburg Gate is named "Platz des 18. März".
08

Why does it matter?

The Berlin barricades were the high point of the German democratic movement of 1848.

  • The Frankfurt Parliament's list of basic rights influenced later German constitutions.
