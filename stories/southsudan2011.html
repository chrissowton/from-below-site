The vote & democracy

South Sudan's Independence Vote

After decades of war, the people of southern Sudan queued for hours to vote on their future. Nearly 99% chose independence, and the world's newest country was born — before falling into war again.

When
9 – 15 January 2011
Where
Juba, South Sudan

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Southern Sudan, mostly Christian and traditionalist, had long been marginalised by governments in Khartoum.

  • Two long civil wars (1955–72 and 1983–2005) killed millions.
  • In 2005, the Comprehensive Peace Agreement between Khartoum and the Sudan People's Liberation Movement (SPLM) promised a referendum on independence.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Voters of southern Sudan

    Including refugees and the diaspora.

  • Salva Kiir Mayardit

    SPLM leader, who became South Sudan's first president.

  • The referendum commission

    Which ran the vote.

03

What happened?

From 9 to 15 January 2011, southern Sudanese voted on whether to stay part of Sudan or become independent.1

  • Turnout had to reach 60% for the vote to count; it was far above that.1
  • On 7 February, the results showed 98.83% in favour of independence.1
  • A planned referendum in the disputed Abyei area was postponed.1
  • On 9 July 2011, South Sudan became independent.1

04

What happened next?

  • New war: in December 2013, a civil war broke out within South Sudan between forces loyal to the president and the vice-president, killing hundreds of thousands.
  • Abyei: the status of Abyei remains disputed.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The vote was widely praised as peaceful and credible; some ballots were set aside in counties where turnout exceeded 100%, but the result was not in doubt.1

Debates since

  • From below? The referendum came from an agreement between armed movements and governments, but it was ordinary people who queued to vote.2
  • Violence: the wars before the vote involved atrocities by all sides; the civil war after independence brought killings by both government and rebel forces.2
06

The evidence

  • Official results and reports by election observers.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • 9 July is South Sudan's Independence Day.
08

Why does it matter?

The referendum showed the determination of a people to decide their own future after decades of war.

  • Its aftermath shows that independence alone does not bring peace or justice.

Questions to think about
