Kett's Rebellion

Villagers tearing down a landlord's fences asked a farmer to join them. He pulled down his own fences and led 16,000 people to camp outside Norwich, where they ran their own affairs for six weeks.

When
July – August 1549
Where
Mousehold Heath, Norwich, England

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the 1540s, English farming was in crisis, and there was unrest across the country.1

  • The main grievance was enclosure: landlords fencing common land for their own use, leaving peasants nowhere to graze their animals.1
  • Some landlords forced tenants off farms to turn arable land into sheep pasture for the wool trade.1
  • Inflation, unemployment, rising rents and falling wages added to the hardship.1
  • The Lord Protector, the Duke of Somerset, had issued a proclamation against illegal enclosures, so many rioters believed they were acting lawfully.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Robert Kett

    A yeoman farmer of Wymondham, about 57, who led the rebellion; hanged in 1549.

  • William Kett

    His elder brother, who joined the rebellion.

  • The Marquess of Northampton

    Commander of the first royal army, defeated at Norwich.

  • John Dudley, Earl of Warwick

    Commander of the army that crushed the rebels.

03

What happened?

On 8 July 1549, after a local feast, people from Wymondham began tearing down fences around nearby villages.1

  • A landowner, Sir John Flowerdew, bribed them to attack Robert Kett's fences instead. Kett listened to their grievances, helped tear down his own fences, and agreed to lead them.1
  • On 12 July, some 16,000 rebels set up camp on Mousehold Heath outside Norwich.1
  • Kett held court under the "Oak of Reformation", where the camp was organised and complaints were heard.
  • The rebels stormed Norwich on 29 July, and on 1 August defeated a royal army under the Marquess of Northampton.1
  • On 27 August, they were defeated by the Earl of Warwick at the Battle of Dussindale.1

04

What happened next?

  • Executions: about 3,000 rebels were killed at Dussindale, and dozens more were hanged in Norwich in the days that followed.1
  • Kett: captured the night after the battle, he was tried for treason in London and hanged from the walls of Norwich Castle on 7 December 1549.1
  • Politics: the risings of 1549 helped bring down the Duke of Somerset.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government condemned the rebels as traitors; the rebels saw themselves as loyal subjects enforcing the king's laws against greedy landlords.2

Debates since

  • Class war? Historians such as Andy Wood stress the rebels' language of "commonwealth" and their hostility to the gentry.2
  • Violence: there is only one attested case of the rebels killing in cold blood (a captured Italian mercenary); about 3,000 rebels died at Dussindale against some 250 of Warwick's men.1
06

The evidence

  • The rebels' list of 29 demands.
  • Alexander Neville's hostile account of 1575 and Nicholas Sotherton's account.2
07

How is it remembered?

  • In 1949, 400 years after the rebellion, a plaque was placed on Norwich Castle honouring Kett.1
  • Campaigners still mark the anniversary of his death.1
08

Why does it matter?

Kett's Rebellion was the most serious of the 1549 risings against enclosure, and a key moment in the long struggle over common land in England.

  • The rebels' camp showed ordinary people organising themselves.

Questions to think about

  1. Why was enclosure such a threat to ordinary people?
  2. Why did a fairly wealthy farmer join the rebels?
  3. Why did the rebels think the government might be on their side?
  4. Why has Norwich come to honour Kett?

What other events are like this?

  • 1649The Diggers of St George's Hill — In the year the king was executed, a small group of poor men and women began to dig and plant common land, declaring the earth 'a common treasury for all'.
  • 1381The Peasants' Revolt — Tens of thousands of villagers and townspeople marched on London to demand an end to serfdom. For a few days, the government of England was at their mercy.
  • 1514György Dózsa's Peasant War — Peasants called up for a crusade against the Ottomans turned their weapons on the nobles instead. Led by a soldier and radical friars, they demanded the end of the nobility and the sharing of land.
