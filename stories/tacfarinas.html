Tacfarinas and the Musulamii against Rome

A Berber deserter from the Roman army led the herders of North Africa in a seven-year war against Rome. He fought to keep the grazing lands that the empire was turning into wheat fields to feed the city of Rome.

When
17 CE – 24
Where
Thagaste (Souk Ahras) and the Numidian plateau, Algeria and Tunisia
Facts checked
2026-11-16

01

What is the background to this event?

By the first century AD, Rome ruled the province of Africa (roughly modern Tunisia and eastern Algeria), a vital source of grain for the city of Rome.1

  • Under Augustus, Rome took over the traditional summer grazing grounds of the Musulamii, a Berber herding people.1
  • Tacfarinas was a Numidian Berber from Thagaste (now Souk Ahras, Algeria) who had served in, and deserted from, the Roman army.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

From 17 CE, Tacfarinas won the support of the Musulamii and a changing coalition of other Berber peoples.1

  • He trained part of his forces in Roman style, while his mobile horsemen raided across the province.1
  • The raids disrupted grain production, which threatened unrest in Rome itself.1
  • He sent envoys to Tiberius asking for land in return for peace. The emperor was furious that a commoner dared to bargain with him.1
  • Rome sent an extra legion. Three Roman governors were awarded triumphal honours for victories over him, but he kept escaping into the desert.1
  • In 24 CE, after a lucky break in Roman intelligence, his camp was surprised and he was killed.1

04

What happened next?

  • Land: after the war, Rome registered the whole Tunisian plateau for land tax and turned it mainly to wheat.1
  • Exclusion: the Musulamii were probably shut out of their summer pastures for good, and pushed into poorer mountain and desert land.1
  • Mauretania: the neighbouring kingdom was annexed by Rome in 44 CE.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Tacitus, the only detailed source, presents Tacfarinas as a bandit chief, while using the war to show the weaknesses of Tiberius.12

Debates since

  • How serious? The historian C. R. Whittaker suggested Tacitus exaggerated the threat; others point to the extra legion and repeated triumphs as signs of a major war.1
  • Violence: his raids hit farms and settlements in the province; each Roman triumph implied the killing of thousands of his followers.1
06

The evidence

  • Tacitus's Annals.2
  • Roman inscriptions and the later land survey of the region.
07

How is it remembered?

  • In Algeria and among Amazigh (Berber) activists, Tacfarinas is remembered as an early hero of resistance.
08

Why does it matter?

Tacfarinas's war is one of the earliest recorded struggles in Africa against colonial land seizure.

  • It was a conflict between herders and an empire that wanted the land for export crops.

What other events are like this?

  • 147 BCEViriathus and the Lusitanian War — After a Roman governor massacred Lusitanians who had laid down their arms, one survivor — a shepherd turned warrior — led eight years of resistance to Rome. Rome could not defeat him in battle, so it paid his own men to kill him.
  • 60 CEBoudica's Revolt — When Roman officials seized the kingdom of the Iceni, flogged its queen and raped her daughters, Boudica led the peoples of eastern Britain in a revolt that burned three Roman towns — before ending in a massacre.
  • 1921Abd el-Krim and the Republic of the Rif — Berber mountain tribes inflicted one of the worst defeats on a European army in colonial history and founded a republic. Spain and France needed hundreds of thousands of troops — and poison gas — to crush it.
