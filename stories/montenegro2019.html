'Resist!' — Montenegro's Anti-Corruption Protests

A video of a businessman handing an envelope of cash to the mayor of Podgorica brought thousands into the streets every week. The protests did not bring down the government — but its 30-year rule ended at the ballot box the next year.

When
February – June 2019
Where
Podgorica, Montenegro
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Milo Đukanović and his Democratic Party of Socialists (DPS) had dominated Montenegro since the introduction of multi-party politics in 1990.1

  • In 2015, an investigative journalists' network named Đukanović "Person of the Year in Organized Crime"; he denied most claims about his wealth.1
  • In January 2019, a video from 2016 surfaced showing the businessman Duško Knežević apparently handing the mayor of Podgorica an envelope that Knežević said contained $97,000 for the DPS.1
  • Knežević, who fled to London, said he had funded the party in this way for 25 years.1

03

What happened?

In February 2019, the civic group "97,000 – Resist!" began weekly protests in Podgorica, named after the sum in the envelope.1

  • Protesters demanded the resignation of the president, the prime minister and other officials.1
  • The protests were supported by opposition parties, but organised by civil society.1
  • They took place at the same time as anti-government protests in neighbouring Serbia.1
  • The protests faded by mid-2019 without achieving their demands.1

04

What happened next?

  • Church protests: in late 2019 and 2020, mass protests by the Serbian Orthodox Church against a new religion law also challenged the government.
  • Defeat: in August 2020, the DPS lost its parliamentary majority for the first time in three decades.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government dismissed the protests as opposition politics; the organisers insisted they were a civic, non-party movement.1

Debates since

  • Effect: some argue the protests weakened the DPS and prepared the way for its defeat in 2020; others credit the church protests or other factors.
  • Non-violence: the protests were peaceful.
06

The evidence

  • The Knežević videos and documents.

07

How is it remembered?

  • The slogan "Resist!" became a reference point for civic activism in Montenegro.
08

Why does it matter?

The protests show both the power and the limits of civic movements against entrenched elites.
