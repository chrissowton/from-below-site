The May Fourth Movement

When the peace conference at Versailles handed German territory in China to Japan, Beijing students took to the streets. Workers and merchants joined them in strikes, and China refused to sign the treaty.

When
4 May – June 1919
Where
Beijing, China
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

After the fall of the Qing in 1911, China was dominated by warlords, and the Beijing government could do little against foreign power.1

  • In 1919, the Paris Peace Conference allowed Japan to keep the former German territories in Shandong.1
  • A "New Culture Movement" was already challenging Confucian traditions.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 4 May 1919, students gathered in front of Tiananmen to protest at the government's weak response.1

  • They demanded the resignation of three officials accused of collaborating with Japan, burned their homes and beat some of their servants.1
  • Students were arrested, jailed and beaten.1
  • Student strikes spread across China; in June, nearly 1,000 students were arrested in Beijing.1
  • In Shanghai, merchants and workers held a general strike.1
  • On 12 June, the government dismissed the three officials, and China's delegates in Paris refused to sign the Treaty of Versailles.1

04

What happened next?

  • New politics: many leaders of the next fifty years emerged from the movement, including founders of the Chinese Communist Party.1
  • Culture: the movement pushed for vernacular writing, science and democracy.2
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government saw disorder; the students saw patriotism.1

Debates since

  • Who owns it? Both the Communist Party and liberals claim the movement's legacy.2
  • Violence: students burned officials' homes and beat servants; the government arrested and beat students.1

Why does it matter?

The May Fourth Movement turned a student protest into a national movement, and shaped China's twentieth century.12

What other events are like this?

  • 1919The March First Movement — Koreans read out a declaration of independence and poured into the streets in peaceful protests against Japanese rule. Up to two million people took part, and thousands were killed.
  • 1989Tiananmen Square, 1989 — Students and workers occupied the heart of Beijing for seven weeks, calling for freedom of speech and an end to corruption, until the army cleared the streets with tanks and gunfire.
  • 1919Egypt's 1919 Revolution — When Britain exiled the nationalist leader Saad Zaghlul, Egyptians of every class and religion rose up in strikes and demonstrations — and women marched in public for the first time.
