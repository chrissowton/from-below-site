The vote & democracy

Malawi's Vote for Democracy

After 30 years under a 'President for Life', Malawi's Catholic bishops broke the silence with a pastoral letter. Strikes, protests and an exile's return followed, and in 1993 Malawians voted to end one-party rule.

When
1992 – 1993
Where
Lilongwe and Blantyre, Malawi

01

What is the background to this event?

Malawi had been ruled by Hastings Kamuzu Banda and the Malawi Congress Party since independence in 1964.1

  • Banda became President for Life in 1971, and the MCP was the only legal party.1
  • Opponents were imprisoned, exiled or killed.

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 8 March 1992, Malawi's Catholic bishops issued a pastoral letter criticising human rights abuses, breaking years of silence.1

  • In April 1992, the exiled union leader Chakufwa Chihana returned and called for a referendum; he was arrested and sentenced to two years' hard labour.1
  • Protests and strikes followed, along with international pressure.1
  • Banda agreed to a referendum, and opposition groups organised campaigns despite limited access to the media.1
  • On 14 June 1993, over 64% voted for multi-party democracy.1
04

What happened next?

  • Elections: in 1994, Banda and the MCP were defeated in multi-party elections.1
  • Security: the army dismantled the regime's secret police.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The MCP claimed multi-party politics would bring tribal and religious conflict.1

Debates since

  • From below or from outside? Some of Banda's advisers blamed Western donors; others stress the courage of churches, unions and ordinary voters.1
  • Violence: the transition was largely peaceful, but security forces killed protesters during riots in Blantyre and Lilongwe in May 1992.
06

The evidence

  • The bishops' pastoral letter of 1992.1
  • Referendum results and observers' reports.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • The pastoral letter is remembered in Malawi as the spark of the democratic transition.
08

Why does it matter?

Malawi's churches, unions and citizens ended thirty years of dictatorship without civil war.
