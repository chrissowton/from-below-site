Electric Yerevan

When electricity prices rose again, thousands of young Armenians sat down in the main avenue of Yerevan under the slogan 'No to Robbery'. Police water cannon only made the protest bigger, and the price rise was reversed.

When
June – July 2015
Where
Baghramyan Avenue, Yerevan, Armenia

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In 2015, nearly half of Armenians lived below the poverty line, and unemployment was high.1

  • Smaller protests had already won victories against rises in minibus fares and a compulsory pension scheme.1
  • In June 2015, the regulator approved a 17% rise in electricity prices — the third rise in a few years.1
  • The electricity network was owned by a Russian company.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • 'No to Robbery' activists

    Mostly young people who organised the protest.

  • Residents of Yerevan, Gyumri and other cities

    Who joined in.

03

What happened?

On 22 June 2015, protesters marched on Baghramyan Avenue towards the Presidential Palace and began a sit-in.1

  • Early on 23 June, police cleared the avenue with water cannon and detained about 200 people, many of them journalists.1
  • Around 25 people were taken to hospital, and the police action triggered a much larger wave of protest.1
  • Protests spread to Gyumri, Vanadzor and other cities.1
  • Thousands held the avenue for days, with music and dancing.

Sources

  1. Wikipedia, 'Electric Yerevan', on the background, the price rise, the protests, the police response and the outcome. Link ↗
04

What happened next?

  • Price reversed: the price rise was reversed, and the Russian-owned company sold the network.1
  • 2018: the experience of Electric Yerevan fed into the Velvet Revolution of 2018.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Some commentators compared it to Ukraine's Maidan; organisers insisted it was about prices, not geopolitics.1

Debates since

  • Its legacy: many see it as a rehearsal for the 2018 revolution.
  • Violence: the protesters were peaceful; police used water cannon and made mass arrests.1
06

The evidence

  • Photographs and videos shared by protesters.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • Baghramyan Avenue became a site of protest again in 2018.
08

Why does it matter?

Electric Yerevan showed that a non-partisan protest over a basic cost of living could win.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did an electricity price rise cause such anger?
  2. Why did the police action make the protest bigger?
  3. Why did protesters insist the protest was not political or anti-Russian?
  4. How did Electric Yerevan prepare the way for 2018?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 2018Armenia's Velvet Revolution — When Serzh Sargsyan tried to stay in power by moving from president to prime minister, an opposition MP began a walk across Armenia. Weeks of peaceful protest and street blockades forced Sargsyan to resign.
  • 1989The Caracazo — When a new government raised fuel and bus fares overnight, the poor of Caracas rose in anger. The army was sent into the hillside barrios, and hundreds — perhaps thousands — were killed.
  • 2003The Rose Revolution — After a rigged election, Georgians filled the streets for three weeks. Protesters carrying roses entered parliament, and the president resigned without a shot being fired.
