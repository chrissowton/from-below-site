Palermo Rises: The First Revolution of 1848

On 12 January 1848, the people of Palermo rose against their Bourbon king. It was the first of the revolutions that swept Europe that year, and Sicily governed itself for sixteen months under one of the most democratic constitutions of its time.

When

Where

01

What is the background to this event?

Sicily had been joined to Naples in 1815 to form the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, ruled by the Bourbon king Ferdinand II.1

  • The king had abolished the island's constitution, and many Sicilians resented rule from Naples.1
  • The island was poor, and the police regime was harsh.1
  • Three earlier revolts against Bourbon rule had broken out in Sicily since 1800.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 12 January 1848, a popular uprising broke out in Palermo against Ferdinand II.1

  • It became the first of the revolutions that swept Europe in 1848.12
  • The rebels set up a provisional government and then an independent Kingdom of Sicily.1
  • Its constitution of 1848 was advanced for its time in democratic terms, and proposed a confederation of Italian states.1
  • The insurgents sang a song in Sicilian about "the twelfth of January" and freedom.1

04

What happened next?

  • Reconquest: the revolutionary government never controlled the fortified city of Messina. Bourbon forces bombarded Messina, and their soldiers looted, killed and raped civilians there.1
  • End: the Bourbon army retook the whole island by 15 May 1849, and Settimo went into exile in Malta.1
  • Unification: Bourbon rule ended when Giuseppe Garibaldi's expedition landed in 1860.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The king saw a rebellion against lawful rule; Sicilians saw the recovery of their own constitution and freedom.1

Debates since

  • Whose revolution? The uprising began in the streets, but Sicilian nobles soon took a leading role in the new government.1
  • Violence on both sides: the revolution was fought with arms; the Bourbon reconquest, especially at Messina, involved the bombardment of civilians and atrocities by soldiers.1

How is it remembered?

  • The revolution is remembered as a step towards Italian unification.1
08

Why does it matter?

Palermo shows that the great European revolutions of 1848 began not in Paris but on the edge of Europe.
