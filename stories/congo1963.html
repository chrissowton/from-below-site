Workers & unions

The Trois Glorieuses

When President Fulbert Youlou tried to impose a one-party state and jailed union leaders, workers and young people filled the streets of Brazzaville. In three days — the 'Three Glorious Days' — he was forced to resign.

When
13 – 15 August 1963
Where
Brazzaville, Republic of the Congo
Facts checked
2026-11-16

01

What is the background to this event?

The Republic of the Congo became independent from France on 15 August 1960, with Abbé Fulbert Youlou, a former Catholic priest, as president.1

  • By 1963, the economy had not improved, while the government spent lavishly.1
  • Youlou was seen as authoritarian and corrupt.1
  • In August 1963, he moved to impose a single party and imprisoned trade union leaders.1

03

What happened?

On 13 August 1963, the unions called a general strike and demonstrations in Brazzaville.2

  • Crowds demanded the release of the union leaders and the president's resignation.
  • According to many accounts, crowds attacked the prison and freed detained union leaders.
  • French troops in the city did not intervene, and President de Gaulle did not support Youlou.2
  • On 15 August — the third anniversary of independence — Youlou resigned under pressure from the unions and the army.2

Some people were killed during the three days, though accounts give different figures.

04

What happened next?

  • A new government: Alphonse Massamba-Débat became president and moved towards socialism and China.2
  • One party after all: in 1964 the National Movement of the Revolution became the sole party.
  • Military rule: in 1968, Marien Ngouabi took power in a coup and founded a Marxist–Leninist state.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Youlou blamed communists; his opponents saw a popular revolution against corruption.1

Debates since

  • Revolution or coup? Some historians stress the role of the army and politicians in Youlou's fall.
  • Violence: crowds attacked prisons and property; deaths were reported, but figures are uncertain.

07

How is it remembered?

  • In the Congo, 13–15 August was celebrated as a national holiday for many years.
08

Why does it matter?

The Trois Glorieuses was one of the first times in independent Africa that a popular uprising, led by trade unions, brought down a president.

  • Ironically, it led to another one-party state.

09

What other events are like this?

  • 1947The Dakar–Niger Railway Strike — African railway workers in French West Africa held out for more than five months to win equal treatment with their European colleagues.
  • 2007Guinea's General Strike of 2007 — When Guinea's ailing president freed two men accused of corruption, the trade unions called a general strike. Weeks of shutdown and protest, met with deadly force and martial law, forced him to share power.
  • 1990Benin's National Conference — Strikes by unpaid teachers, students and civil servants forced Benin's military ruler to call a national conference. The delegates declared themselves sovereign, took power peacefully — and inspired democratic movements across Africa.
