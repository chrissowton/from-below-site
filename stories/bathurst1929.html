Edward Francis Small and the Bathurst Strike

A teacher and journalist founded the Gambia's first trade union and led a three-week strike. The Bathurst Trade Union was the first legally registered union in Africa — and the beginning of Gambian politics.

When
1929
Where
Bathurst (now Banjul), the Gambia
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The Gambia was a small British colony, based on the trade in groundnuts. Wages were low and Africans had little say in government.

  • Edward Francis Small, born in Bathurst in 1891, was educated in Freetown and worked as a teacher and Methodist mission worker.1
  • He founded the Gambia's first nationalist newspaper, organised evening classes for villagers, and founded a farmers' co-operative in 1917.1
  • He was active in the National Congress of British West Africa, which called for African representation.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In 1929, Small founded the Bathurst Trade Union, building on the Carpenters' and Shipwrights' Society.3

  • Other craft associations joined in October 1929.3
  • In the autumn of 1929, the union led a three-week strike.3
  • The union received support from British labour and anti-imperialist groups.3
  • By April 1930, its membership had grown to about 1,000.3
04

What happened next?

  • Division: in 1932–33, the union split over leadership, with political and ethnic tensions; Small formed the Gambia Labour Union in 1935.3
  • Politics: Small founded the Gambia's first political party and became the first citizen elected to its legislature.1
  • Independence: the Gambia became independent in 1965.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Colonial officials and conservative elites in Bathurst were uneasy about Small's activism.3

Debates since

  • Firsts: historians debate which African union was first, but the BTU is often credited as the first to be legally registered.3
  • Non-violence: the strike was peaceful.

The evidence

07

How is it remembered?

  • Small is honoured in the Gambia as a national hero; a hospital in Banjul is named after him.
08

Why does it matter?

The Bathurst Trade Union was the first legally registered trade union in Africa.3

  • Small linked workers' rights to the struggle for African self-government.

What other events are like this?

  • 1947The Dakar–Niger Railway Strike — African railway workers in French West Africa held out for more than five months to win equal treatment with their European colleagues.
  • 1935The Copperbelt Strike of 1935 — When the colonial government raised the tax on African mineworkers, they walked out at three of the Copperbelt's great mines. Police shot six people dead — but the strike is remembered as the birth of Zambian nationalism.
  • 1898The Hut Tax War — When the British imposed a tax on every house in the new Sierra Leone Protectorate, the Temne leader Bai Bureh led a guerrilla war against them, while a separate Mende rising killed traders and missionaries. Both were crushed.
