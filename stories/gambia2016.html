Solo Sandeng and the Fall of Yahya Jammeh

An opposition activist led a small protest for electoral reform and died in custody the next day. His death set off protests, mass arrests — and, eight months later, the defeat of a ruler who had held power for 22 years.

When
April 2016 – January 2017
Where
Serekunda and Banjul, the Gambia
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

Yahya Jammeh had taken power in a bloodless coup in 1994.4

  • Protests were not tolerated, and the National Intelligence Agency was later accused of torturing protesters.23
  • In the Gambia, voters cast their ballot by dropping a marble into a drum marked for their candidate.2

In their own words

03

What happened?

In April 2016, Solo Sandeng led a demonstration demanding electoral reform. He was arrested, tortured, and died in custody on 14 April.15

  • The head of the National Intelligence Agency was later charged with his murder.3
  • Ousainou Darboe, leader of Sandeng's party, led protests demanding Sandeng's body, "dead or alive". He and others were arrested and jailed.5
  • With Darboe in prison, opposition parties united behind Adama Barrow as their joint candidate in "Coalition 2016".5
  • Jammeh warned that "in this country we don't allow demonstrations". Messaging apps were blocked before the election, and internet access was cut on polling day.2
  • On 1 December 2016, Barrow won 43.3% of the vote against Jammeh's 39.6%. Jammeh at first conceded.2

04

What happened next?

  • Prisoners freed: a few days after the election, Darboe and 18 other opposition members were released.5
  • Jammeh refuses to go: on 9 December, Jammeh announced on television that he rejected the result.6
  • Regional pressure: the West African bloc ECOWAS assembled troops from Senegal, Nigeria and Ghana. Barrow was sworn in at the Gambian embassy in Dakar on 19 January 2017; ECOWAS troops entered the country without resistance, and on 21 January Jammeh left for exile in Equatorial Guinea.6
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government treated protests as illegal; the opposition demanded justice for Sandeng and electoral reform.25

Debates since

  • From below or from outside? Gambians voted Jammeh out, but it took the threat of regional military force to make him leave.6
  • Violence: Sandeng and at least one other opposition member died after torture in custody in April 2016, according to the charges against the intelligence chief.3
06

The evidence

  • Election results, the testimony of former detainees, and the charges brought against intelligence officials after Jammeh's fall.23
07

How is it remembered?

  • Sandeng's death is remembered as the spark of the change of government in 2016.5
08

Why does it matter?

Solo Sandeng's death turned fear into anger. An opposition that had been divided united — and a ruler who had held power for 22 years was voted out, then forced out with the help of neighbouring states.156

Questions to think about

  1. Why did one activist's death have such a large effect?
  2. Why did the opposition unite behind a candidate with no political experience?
  3. Why do you think Jammeh first conceded, then changed his mind?
  4. Was it right for neighbouring countries to use the threat of force to make Jammeh leave?
