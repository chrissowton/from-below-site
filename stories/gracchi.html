The Gracchi and the Land of Rome

Tiberius Gracchus proposed giving public land held by the rich to poor citizens. Senators beat him to death with clubs. Ten years later his brother Gaius tried again — and was killed too.

When
133 – 121 BC
Where
Rome

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

By the second century BC, Rome had conquered much of the Mediterranean. Wealthy families had taken over large areas of public land (ager publicus) and worked it with enslaved captives.1

  • Small farmers, who also served as soldiers, were losing their land.1
  • Tiberius Sempronius Gracchus came from one of Rome's leading families; his mother Cornelia was the daughter of Scipio Africanus.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Tiberius Gracchus

    Tribune of the plebs in 133 BC, who proposed the land law.

  • Gaius Gracchus

    His younger brother, tribune in 123–122 BC.

  • Cornelia

    Their mother, admired by Romans for educating her sons.

  • Marcus Octavius

    A tribune who vetoed the land law and was deposed.

  • Scipio Nasica

    The senator who led the attack on Tiberius.

03

What happened?

In 133 BC, Tiberius, as tribune of the plebs, proposed a law to limit how much public land one person could hold and to distribute the rest to poor citizens.1

  • When another tribune, Octavius, vetoed it, Tiberius had him removed from office — something never done before.1
  • A commission was set up to distribute the land.1
  • Tiberius also stood for a second term as tribune, which his enemies said was illegal.1
  • In a riot started by his enemies, Tiberius and many supporters — about 300, according to ancient writers — were clubbed to death, and their bodies thrown into the Tiber.1
  • In 123–122 BC, Gaius Gracchus proposed wider reforms, including subsidised grain and new colonies. In 121 BC he too was killed, with thousands of supporters.

04

What happened next?

  • Land distributed: the land commission distributed more than 3,000 square kilometres of land over the following years.1
  • Political violence: Tiberius's death is traditionally seen as the beginning of the Roman Republic's decline.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The senators who killed Tiberius claimed he wanted to make himself king; his supporters saw him as a defender of the poor.2

Debates since

  • Motives: historians debate whether the Gracchi were idealists, ambitious aristocrats, or both.1
  • Violence: the brothers broke political conventions, but the killings were carried out by their opponents.1
  • Who benefited? The land went mostly to Roman citizens, not to Rome's Italian allies, who remained excluded.
06

The evidence

  • Plutarch and Appian.2
  • Boundary stones set up by the land commission.
07

How is it remembered?

  • François-Noël Babeuf, a radical of the French Revolution, took the name "Gracchus" in their honour.
08

Why does it matter?

The Gracchi raised questions that recur throughout history: who owns the land, and what happens when the rich block reform?

  • Their deaths showed that political disputes could now be settled by violence.

Questions to think about

  1. Why was land so important in Rome?
  2. Were the Gracchi reformers or rule-breakers?
  3. Why did the senate respond with violence?
  4. Why have later radicals looked to the Gracchi as heroes?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 494 BCThe First Secession of the Plebs — Crushed by debt and denied any say in government, the common people of Rome — who made up most of its army — simply walked out of the city. It was perhaps the world's first general strike.
  • 1649The Diggers of St George's Hill — In the year the king was executed, a small group of poor men and women began to dig and plant common land, declaring the earth 'a common treasury for all'.
  • 1879The Irish Land War and the First Boycott — Irish tenant farmers organised against evictions and high rents — and gave the world a new word, 'boycott'.
