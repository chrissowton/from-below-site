The Belgian General Strike of 1893

When parliament blocked votes for working men, 200,000 Belgian workers walked out — in what may have been the first true general strike in Europe. Troops killed strikers, but the vote was won.

When
12 – 18 April 1893
Where
The Borinage and Brussels, Belgium
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the early 1890s, only wealthy men could vote in Belgium.

  • The new Belgian Labour Party (POB–BWP) campaigned for the vote for all men.1
  • On 11 April 1893, Catholic and Liberal politicians joined to block a proposal to give the vote to all men over 25.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • The miners of the Borinage

    Who pushed the party into calling the strike.

  • Emile Vandervelde

    Leader of the Belgian Labour Party.

  • Auguste Beernaert

    Catholic prime minister.

03

What happened?

On the evening of 11 April 1893, the Labour Party called a general strike.1

  • According to the historian Henri Pirenne, the strike was only called under pressure from the miners of the Borinage.1
  • About 200,000 workers joined, more than the party leaders expected.1
  • The government, fearing revolution, sent the army against the strikers.1
  • Security forces killed between 13 and 20 strikers.1
  • After six days, parliament gave way.1
04

What happened next?

  • Reform: parliament gave the vote to all men, multiplying the electorate tenfold — but gave extra votes to the wealthy and educated, and excluded women.1
  • Socialists in parliament: the 1894 election brought socialist deputies into parliament for the first time.1
  • More strikes: further general strikes for equal voting followed in 1902 and 1913.1
05

Why does it matter?

According to historian Carl Strikwerda, it was the first true general strike in Europe.1

  • It showed workers using their collective power to win political rights.
  • Parliament gave the vote to all men, multiplying the electorate tenfold — though the wealthy got extra votes and women were excluded.1
  • Socialist deputies entered parliament for the first time in 1894.1

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

Conservatives feared revolution; socialists saw a peaceful use of workers' strength.1

Debates since

  • The first general strike? Strikwerda calls it the first true general strike in Europe; others point to earlier examples.12
  • Violence: some strikers clashed with troops; the army killed between 13 and 20 strikers.1
How do we know?The evidence, and a source from the time
  • Illustrated newspapers and Eugène Laermans's paintings of the strike.

Primary source: Painting

What is it?
Painting
Made by, and when
Eugène Laermans, 1893

A Belgian painter's picture of the general strike, made in the same year. Laermans painted rural and working people with sympathy.

Reading it

Laermans painted working people with sympathy, and he painted this in the year of the strike. It is a picture of mood rather than fact — sombre, massive, determined. An artist on the strikers' side is not a neutral witness, but art can capture how a moment felt in a way a newspaper report cannot.

How is it remembered?Memorials, anniversaries and arguments over memory
  • The strike is remembered as a founding moment of the Belgian labour movement.
