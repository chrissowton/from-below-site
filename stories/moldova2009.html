The Chișinău Protests of 2009

A journalist's call on social media brought thousands of young people into the streets after a disputed election. A peaceful protest turned into a riot in which parliament was set on fire — and police then beat and tortured hundreds.

When
6 – 7 April 2009
Where
Chișinău, Moldova
Facts checked
2026-11-16

01

What is the background to this event?

The Party of Communists of the Republic of Moldova had governed since 2001.1

  • On 5 April 2009, it won about 50 per cent of the vote in parliamentary elections — 60 seats, one short of the number needed to choose the president alone.1
  • The opposition accused the authorities of fraud.1
  • Moldovans were divided over identity: some saw themselves as Moldovans, others as Romanians.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Natalia Morar

    A 25-year-old journalist who organised the first flash mob.

  • Students and young people

    Who made up most of the protesters.

  • Valeriu Boboc

    A young man who died after being beaten, his family and human rights groups said, by police.

  • Vladimir Voronin

    President of Moldova, who called the riots an attempted coup.

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 6 April 2009, Natalia Morar and others organised a flash mob in Chișinău, using social media. The press called it a "Twitter revolution".1

  • On 7 April, more than 10,000 — by some estimates 30,000 — mostly young people gathered on Ștefan cel Mare Boulevard. They chanted "We want Europe" and "Down with Communism".1
  • The demonstration turned into a riot. Rioters attacked the parliament and the president's office, broke windows, set furniture on fire and stole property.1
  • Two teenagers raised the Romanian and EU flags on the buildings.1
  • Hundreds of people, both police and protesters, were injured.1
  • That night and in the following days, police detained hundreds of people. Many reported being beaten and tortured in custody.3
04

What happened next?

  • Deaths: at least three young people died in connection with the events, including Valeriu Boboc.
  • New elections: parliament could not elect a president, and a new election in July 2009 brought a pro-European coalition to power.
05

Why does it matter?

The protests were among the first said to be organised through social media, and they changed Moldova's government.

  • They also show how protest can turn into a riot — and how police abuse can follow.
  • At least three young people died in connection with the events, including Valeriu Boboc.
  • A new election in July 2009 brought a pro-European coalition to power.

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

President Voronin called the riots a coup attempt organised from Romania; the opposition said provocateurs had started the violence.1

Debates since

  • Violence on both sides: rioters set fire to parliament and injured police; police then beat, detained and tortured protesters.13
  • "Twitter revolution": some analysts argue that text messages and older networks mattered more than Twitter.
How do we know?The evidence, and a source from the time

Protesters in the centre of Chișinău on 7 April 2009, the day the protest turned into a riot. Many people at the protest photographed it and shared images online.

Reading it

Protesters in central Chișinău on the day the protest turned into a riot. Many people there photographed it and shared their pictures online, so the record is unusually large and unusually varied. Pictures from the same day can tell very different stories, depending on the moment and the person holding the camera.
