The Mokrani Revolt

After years of famine and disease, a third of Algeria's people rose against French colonial rule. When it was crushed, France seized nearly half a million hectares of land for settlers.

When
March 1871 – January 1872
Where
Medjana and Kabylie, Algeria
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

France had invaded Algeria in 1830, and crushed the resistance of Abd al-Qadir by 1847.1

  • Between 1866 and 1868, drought, cold winters, cholera and an earthquake killed more than 10% of the Kabyle population.1
  • In 1870, France gave Algeria a civilian government that favoured the European settlers.1
  • Cheikh Mokrani, a local leader who had guaranteed loans for the authorities during the famine, was left to repay them himself.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 16 March 1871, the revolt began. More than 250 tribes — about a third of Algeria's population — joined it.1

  • The Rahmaniyya Sufi order under Cheikh El Haddad brought many followers into the revolt.1
  • On 5 May, Cheikh Mokrani was killed in battle.1
  • French columns advanced into Kabylie, and El Haddad was captured in July.1
  • Boumezrag Mokrani was captured in January 1872.1

04

What happened next?

  • Deportation: rebels were deported to Cayenne and New Caledonia, where they became known as the "Algerians of the Pacific".1
  • Land: Kabylie was fined 36 million francs, and 450,000 hectares of land were confiscated and given to settlers, many from Alsace-Lorraine.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

French settlers blamed the revolt on the Crémieux Decree, which made Algerian Jews French citizens; many historians doubt this.1

Debates since

  • Causes: historians stress famine, debt, settler power and the loss of land and autonomy.12
  • Violence on both sides: about 100 European civilians died, along with an unknown number of Algerian civilians; the collective punishment of the population was severe.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • The revolt is remembered in Algeria as a key moment of resistance; descendants of the deportees still live in New Caledonia.
08

Why does it matter?

The Mokrani Revolt was the most important rising against France in Algeria between the conquest and the war of independence.1

  • Its defeat opened the way to massive settler land grabs.

Questions to think about

  3. How did religion help to spread the revolt?
