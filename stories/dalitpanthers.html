The Dalit Panthers

Young Dalit poets in Bombay, inspired by the American Black Panthers, called India's 25th Independence Day a 'Black Independence Day' and demanded an end to caste oppression.

When
1972 – 1977
Where
Worli and Bombay (Mumbai), India
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Dalits — formerly called "untouchables" — have faced centuries of caste discrimination in India. B. R. Ambedkar, a Dalit lawyer and the architect of India's constitution, led millions of Dalits to convert to Buddhism in 1956.1

  • Despite legal protections, atrocities against Dalits continued. In 1971, a government committee reported on violence against Dalits, and two Dalit women were forced to walk naked through a village in Maharashtra.1
  • The main Dalit party, the Republican Party of India, had split into factions and was seen by young Dalits as corrupt and ageing.1
  • A new Dalit literature was flourishing in Marathi, telling of caste oppression in the voices of Dalits themselves.1

03

What happened?

In 1972, the Mahar poet-writers J. V. Pawar, Namdeo Dhasal and Raja Dhale founded the Dalit Panthers in Bombay.1

  • Inspired by the Black Panther Party in the United States, they adopted its structure and drew on African American literature.1
  • They urged a boycott of the celebrations of India's 25th Independence Day, calling it a "Black Independence Day".1
  • Their 1973 manifesto fused the ideas of Ambedkar and Jyotirao Phule with Marxism, and called for a complete revolution.1 They openly defended the use of violence if necessary.1

The Panthers challenged the Shiv Sena, a Hindu nationalist party that had drawn many Dalit youths into its gangs.1 In January 1974, a Dalit Panther meeting in Worli was attacked with stones; police baton-charged the crowd and arrested Panthers. In the Worli riots that followed, a Panther, Bhagwat Jadhav, was killed by a grinding stone thrown from a building.1

04

What happened next?

  • Divisions: the founders soon disagreed — over Marxism versus Buddhism and Ambedkarism — and the movement split. Its heyday lasted until about 1977.1
  • Literature: Dalit literature flourished, and Namdeo Dhasal's poetry gained national recognition.
  • New movements: Dalit Panther groups were founded in other states, and in Tamil Nadu the Dalit Panthers of India became the Viduthalai Chiruthaigal Katchi, a political party.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The Shiv Sena accused the Panthers of dividing Maharashtrians by raising caste; the Panthers replied that the Sena represented only the upper castes.1

Historians' debates

  • Marx or Ambedkar? The movement was divided between those who emphasised class struggle and those who emphasised Buddhism and Ambedkar's ideas.1
  • Violence: the Panthers' rhetoric defended violence, but in practice they were mostly the victims of violence by rival groups and the police.
  • Legacy: some see the movement as short-lived and divided; others as the start of an autonomous Dalit politics.1

The evidence

07

How is it remembered?

  • The 50th anniversary of the Dalit Panthers in 2022 was widely marked in Maharashtra.
08

Why does it matter?

The Dalit Panthers gave a new, militant voice to India's most oppressed people. They showed how ideas of liberation travel across the world — from Black America to Bombay.

  • They transformed Indian literature and politics.
  • Caste discrimination and violence remain serious problems in India today.

What other events are like this?

  • 1927The Mahad Satyagraha — B. R. Ambedkar led thousands of Dalits to drink from a public water tank that caste Hindus had forbidden them to touch — a founding moment of the struggle against untouchability.
  • 1971The Israeli Black Panthers — Young Jews from Moroccan families in a poor Jerusalem neighbourhood took the name of the American Black Panthers to protest discrimination against Mizrahi Jews.
  • 1965Bloody Sunday at the Edmund Pettus Bridge — Around six hundred marchers set out from Selma to demand the right to vote. On the Edmund Pettus Bridge, state troopers attacked them with clubs and tear gas.
