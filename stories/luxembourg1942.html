The Luxembourg General Strike of 1942

When Nazi Germany announced that young Luxembourgers would be conscripted into its army, a whole country stopped work. Twenty-one strikers were shot, and their families deported.

When
31 August – 3 September 1942
Where
Wiltz, Esch-sur-Alzette and across Luxembourg
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Germany invaded Luxembourg in May 1940. In August 1940, a German civilian administration took over, under Gauleiter Gustav Simon.1

  • Luxembourgers were declared to be German, and German became the only permitted language.1
  • On 30 August 1942, Simon announced that all Luxembourg men born between 1920 and 1927 would be conscripted into the German army.1

03

What happened?

Within hours of the announcement, Luxembourgers began to organise a general strike. Leaflets were printed and distributed in secret.1

  • On 31 August 1942, the strike began in Wiltz, led by town officials Michel Worré and Nicolas Müller. Workers at the large tannery joined.1
  • It spread to the steel towns of the south, including Schifflange, Differdange and Esch-sur-Alzette.1
  • Administration, farming, industry and schools were paralysed. Teachers refused to teach and children stayed at home.1
  • The Germans set up special courts. Twenty-one strikers were sentenced to death and shot at the Hinzert concentration camp; the first, Worré and Müller, were shot on 2 September.1
  • Red posters across the country announced the names of those executed.1

04

What happened next?

  • Reprisals: the families of executed strikers, including children, were sent to camps in Germany and occupied Poland.1
  • Conscription: about 10,000 young Luxembourgers were forced into the German forces; around 3,000 died. Many deserted or went into hiding.
  • Liberation: Luxembourg was liberated in September 1944, though fighting returned in the Battle of the Bulge.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The German authorities called the strike an insurrection that endangered military work.1

Debates since

  • Non-violence: the strikers used no violence; the killings were judicial executions by the occupiers.1
  • Collaboration: historians also study the Luxembourgers who supported the German authorities.2

07

How is it remembered?

  • The national strike monument stands in Wiltz.
  • The strike is remembered each year on 31 August.
08

Why does it matter?

The 1942 strike was one of the largest acts of mass resistance in Nazi-occupied Western Europe.

  • It was a refusal by a small nation to be absorbed into Germany.

Questions to think about
