15 March 1848: Hungary's Revolution

Young radicals in a Pest café drew up twelve demands, printed them without the censor's permission and read a poem to the crowds. Within weeks, Hungary had abolished serfdom and won an elected parliament.

When
15 March 1848 – October 1849
Where

01

What is the background to this event?

Hungary was part of the Austrian Empire, ruled by the Habsburgs.

  • Most Hungarians were serfs, bound to work for noble landowners.
  • Reformers had drawn up a programme called the Twelve Points, demanding freedom of the press, an elected parliament and equality before the law.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 15 March 1848, radical young people met at the Pilvax café in Pest.1

  • Sándor Petőfi recited his new "National Song", and Mór Jókai read the Twelve Points to growing crowds of students.1
  • They printed the Twelve Points and the poem without the censor's permission — the first act of a free press.1
  • The April Laws that followed created an elected parliament and abolished serfdom.12
  • In April 1848, Hungary passed a law for democratic parliamentary elections.1

04

What happened next?

  • War: the Habsburgs, with Croatian troops under Josip Jelačić and later the Russian army, went to war against Hungary.1
  • Defeat: the Hungarian army surrendered in August 1849. Thirteen generals were executed at Arad, and Prime Minister Lajos Batthyány was shot in Pest.1
  • Minority rights: in July 1849, the revolutionary parliament passed some of the first laws on minority rights in Europe.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Hungarians saw a struggle for freedom; many Croats, Serbs and Romanians saw a Hungarian government that ignored their own national rights.1

Debates since

  • Whose freedom? The April Laws ignored Croatian autonomy, and conflict between nationalities shaped the war.1
  • Violence on all sides: the war saw brutal clashes between Hungarians and Romanians, and both Romanian and Hungarian civilians were massacred in Transylvania; and Austrian reprisals after 1849 included executions.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • Each 15 March, Hungarians wear cockades in the national colours and gather at the National Museum, where crowds met on the afternoon of 15 March 1848.1
08

Why does it matter?

15 March is one of Hungary's three national holidays and a cornerstone of Hungarian identity.1

  • The abolition of serfdom outlasted the defeat of the revolution.2
