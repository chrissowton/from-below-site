Charlemagne Péralte and the Cacos

When US Marines occupied Haiti, peasant guerrillas called Cacos fought back. Their leader was killed by Marines in disguise, and a photograph of his body, meant to frighten Haitians, made him a martyr.

When
1915 – 1920
Where
Hinche, Haiti
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The United States invaded Haiti in July 1915, during a period of political instability.1

  • The US wanted to protect American interests and control a route to the Panama Canal.1
  • US Marines defeated Caco rebels in 1915, and set up a Haitian Gendarmerie under Marine command.1
  • The occupation brought back forced labour (corvée) on road building, which caused great anger among peasants.

In their own words

03

What happened?

Péralte left government service when the occupation began.1

  • In 1917, he took part in a raid on a Gendarmerie payroll and was sentenced to five years in prison.1
  • He escaped in 1918, and began leading Cacos in raids, vowing to drive the invaders into the sea.1
  • By mid-1919, his force had grown to about 5,000.1
  • In October 1919, his attack on Port-au-Prince was beaten back.1
  • On 31 October 1919, Marines disguised as Cacos, guided by a paid informer, entered his camp and shot him dead.1

04

What happened next?

  • A martyr: US forces spread a photograph of his body tied to a door; its resemblance to a crucifixion made him a national hero.1
  • Defeat: the Cacos fought on under Benoît Batraville, who was killed in 1920.1
  • Occupation: US forces stayed in Haiti until 1934.
05

What do others say?

At the time

US officials called the Cacos bandits; Haitians saw patriots resisting occupation.2

Debates since

  • Racism and empire: historian Mary Renda shows how racist ideas shaped the occupation.2
  • Violence on both sides: the Cacos raided Gendarmerie posts and towns; Marines and Gendarmes killed thousands of Haitians, and a US Senate inquiry examined reports of killings of civilians and prisoners.2

The evidence

How is it remembered?

  • Péralte's image has appeared on Haitian coins and stamps.1
08

Why does it matter?

The Caco resistance was a peasant struggle against foreign occupation and forced labour.

  • It shows the violence of US interventions in the Caribbean.
