The Helot Revolt

Sparta's power rested on the forced labour of the helots, a conquered people. When an earthquake shattered Sparta, the helots of Messenia rose up and held out on a mountain fortress for years.

When
c. 464 – 455 BCE
Where
Mount Ithome, Messenia, Greece

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The helots made up the majority of the population of Laconia and Messenia, the lands ruled by Sparta. They worked the land to feed Spartan citizens.1

  • The Messenians had been conquered by Sparta in the eighth and seventh centuries BCE.1
  • According to Herodotus, there were seven helots for every Spartan soldier in 479 BCE.1
  • Helots were ritually humiliated. Each year Sparta declared war on them, so that young Spartans of the Crypteia could kill helots without religious penalty.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • The helots of Messenia

    Who rose and held Mount Ithome.

  • The Spartans

    Citizens whose way of life depended on helot labour.

  • Cimon

    An Athenian general who brought troops to help Sparta, and was sent home.

03

What happened?

In about 464 BCE, a great earthquake struck Sparta, killing many citizens. The helots, especially in Messenia, rose in revolt.2

  • The rebels fortified Mount Ithome, the ancient stronghold of Messenia.2
  • Sparta appealed to its allies, including Athens, which sent troops under Cimon. The Spartans, suspicious of the Athenians, sent them home.2
  • The rebels held out for years.2
  • Finally they surrendered on condition that they could leave the Peloponnese. Athens settled them at Naupactus, on the Gulf of Corinth.2

04

What happened next?

  • Athens and Sparta: the insult to Cimon helped turn Athens against Sparta, contributing to the Peloponnesian War.
  • Freedom: in 369 BCE, after Thebes defeated Sparta, the Theban general Epaminondas freed Messenia, and the city of Messene was founded at the foot of Ithome.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Spartans feared the helots above all; Greek writers disagreed about whether helots were slaves or something in between.1

Debates since

  • Violence: the revolt was a war, with killings on both sides; the Spartan system itself relied on routine violence against helots.13
  • Dates: the revolt's length is uncertain; ancient sources disagree.3
06

The evidence

  • Thucydides and later writers.2
  • Archaeology of Ithome and Messene.
07

How is it remembered?

  • The ruins of ancient Messene, the city of the freed Messenians, are among the finest in Greece.
08

Why does it matter?

The helots' revolt exposes the hidden foundation of Spartan power: a large, unfree population held down by terror.

  • The Messenians eventually won back their freedom.

Questions to think about

  2. Why did the earthquake give the helots a chance to rebel?
  3. Why did Sparta send the Athenians home?
  4. Why is Sparta often admired, despite the helots?

What other events are like this?

  • 73 BCESpartacus and the Slave Revolt — Seventy gladiators broke out of a training school armed with kitchen knives. Within two years, an army of some 120,000 enslaved people had defeated Rome's legions again and again.
  • 135 BCEEunus and the First Sicilian Slave War — Enslaved workers on Sicily's great estates rose up under a Syrian slave and prophet named Eunus. They took cities, crowned him king, and held off Roman armies for three years.
  • 869The Zanj Rebellion — Enslaved East Africans forced to dig salt-crusted marshland in southern Iraq rose against the Abbasid Caliphate, built their own state and held out for fourteen years.
