The Mirabal Sisters

Three sisters known as 'the Butterflies' joined the underground against the dictator Rafael Trujillo. Their murder helped to bring him down — and the day they died is now the world's day against violence towards women.

When
25 November 1960
Where
Salcedo and Puerto Plata, Dominican Republic
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Rafael Trujillo ruled the Dominican Republic from 1930 to 1961, through terror, a cult of personality and control of the economy.

  • The Mirabal family were farmers and landowners from Ojo de Agua, near Salcedo. They kept no portrait of Trujillo in the house, and were treated as dissidents.1
  • Minerva Mirabal rejected Trujillo's advances; her father was imprisoned and the family lost much of its fortune.1
  • Minerva studied law and was the first woman to graduate from law school in the country.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Minerva and her husband, Manolo Tavárez Justo, founded the 14 June Revolutionary Movement, an underground network that planned to overthrow Trujillo. Within it, the sisters were known as Las Mariposas — the Butterflies.1

  • The movement was discovered in January 1960. Minerva and María Teresa were imprisoned several times, in the La Victoria and La 40 prisons; their husbands were also jailed.1
  • The Catholic Church and foreign governments protested at the arrests.
  • On 25 November 1960, Patria, Minerva and María Teresa were returning from visiting their husbands in prison at Puerto Plata.2
  • Trujillo's agents stopped their car, beat the three sisters and their driver, Rufino de la Cruz, to death, and pushed the car off a mountain road to make it look like an accident.12

04

What happened next?

  • Outrage: few believed the story of an accident. The murders turned many Dominicans, and the United States, against Trujillo.
  • Trujillo was assassinated on 30 May 1961.
  • The killers were tried and convicted in 1962, though they later escaped during the country's civil conflict.
  • 25 November: in 1999, the UN General Assembly made the date the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women.13
05

What do others say?

At the time

The regime claimed the sisters died in a road accident.1

Debates since

  • Armed resistance: the 14 June Movement stored weapons and planned an armed rising; the sisters are sometimes remembered only as victims, not as revolutionaries.1
  • Heroines and others: some historians note that the focus on the sisters can overshadow the many other victims of Trujillo, including thousands of Haitians massacred in 1937.
06

The evidence

  • The 1962 trial of the killers.
  • Dedé Mirabal's memoir and the family's letters and belongings.2
07

How is it remembered?

  • The sisters' last home is a museum, and their province was renamed Hermanas Mirabal.1
  • Julia Alvarez's novel In the Time of the Butterflies (1994) made their story known worldwide.
08

Why does it matter?

The Mirabal sisters became symbols of both popular and feminist resistance.1

  • Their story shows the role of women in resisting dictatorship.
  • It links political violence to violence against women.
