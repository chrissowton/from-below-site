Disability rights

The Blind Marchers of 1920

Blind workers organised in their own trade union marched on London from three directions, demanding 'rights not charity'. Their march won the first disability law in Britain — and inspired the Jarrow March.

When
April 1920
Where
Leeds, Manchester and Newport to Trafalgar Square, London

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the early twentieth century, most blind people in Britain lived in poverty. Many depended on charity, or worked for low wages in workshops run by charities.1

  • The National League of the Blind was founded in 1893 and registered as a trade union in 1899 — one of the first organisations run by disabled people themselves.1
  • It affiliated to the Trades Union Congress in 1902 and organised its first strike in 1912.1
  • It opposed charity-based provision and campaigned for state support and decent employment.1

03

What happened?

In April 1920, the National League of the Blind organised a march on London. Marchers set out from Leeds, Manchester and Newport and converged on Trafalgar Square.1

  • They demanded state support for blind people, rather than dependence on charity.12
  • Along the way they held meetings and were supported by trade unionists and local people.2
  • The march helped secure the Blind Persons Act 1920, which lowered the pension age for blind people and required local authorities to support them.12

04

What happened next?

  • Jarrow: the march helped inspire the Jarrow March of 1936.1
  • 1936: the League marched again in 1936, contributing to a further Blind Persons Act in 1938.1
  • Later: in 1981 the League helped found the British Council of Organisations of Disabled People.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Charities argued they provided care and work; the League argued they kept blind people poor and dependent.2

Debates since

  • Sheltered workshops: the League campaigned for state workshops, which later disability activists criticised as segregation.12

How is it remembered?

  • The League continues as a section of the Community trade union.1
08

Why does it matter?

The blind marchers were among the first disabled people to organise as workers and demand rights rather than charity.

  • Their slogan — rights not charity — remains central to the disability movement.
  • They linked disability to the labour movement.

What other events are like this?

  • 1977The Section 504 Sit-in — More than a hundred disabled people occupied a federal building for 26 days until the government signed the first major civil rights regulations for disabled Americans.
  • 1993The Disabled People's Direct Action Network — Disabled activists chained their wheelchairs to buses, blocked traffic and picketed charity telethons, demanding 'rights not charity'. Their direct action helped force Britain to pass its first disability discrimination law.
  • 1888The Matchwomen's Strike — About 1,400 women and girls walked out of a match factory, won their demands in three weeks, and founded the largest union of women in Britain.
