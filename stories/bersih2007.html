The Bersih Rally

Tens of thousands of Malaysians, many dressed in yellow, marched through Kuala Lumpur to demand clean and fair elections. Police used water cannon and tear gas — and a year later, the ruling coalition suffered its worst result in decades.

When
10 November 2007
Where
Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

Malaysia had been ruled by the same coalition, the Barisan Nasional, since independence in 1957.1

  • Critics alleged that the election system was unfair and heavily favoured the ruling coalition.1
  • In 1998, the sacking of the deputy prime minister, Anwar Ibrahim, had sparked the Reformasi protests.1
  • BERSIH (the Coalition for Clean and Fair Elections) was formed by opposition parties and civil society groups.

In their own words

03

What happened?

Publicity for the rally spread mainly online, through blogs and alternative media.1

  • To avoid the police, the real gathering points were only announced the day before.1
  • Marchers met at four points and walked towards the palace to hand a memorandum to the King.1
  • Estimates of the crowd range from 10,000 to 40,000, and some claim up to 100,000.1
  • They demanded indelible ink to stop repeat voting, clean electoral rolls, an end to postal-vote abuse and fair access to the media.1
  • Police fired water cannon and tear gas at some marchers, and dozens were arrested.1

04

What happened next?

  • "Political tsunami": in the March 2008 election, the ruling coalition lost its two-thirds majority, as voters of different ethnic groups backed the opposition.1
  • More rallies: Bersih held further mass rallies in 2011, 2012, 2015 and 2016.
  • Change: in 2018, the coalition lost power for the first time since independence.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government called the rally illegal; organisers said it was a peaceful walk to petition the King.1

Debates since

  • Impact: many credit Bersih with changing voters' minds in 2008; others stress wider economic and ethnic politics.1
  • Violence: the marchers were peaceful; police used water cannon and tear gas.1

How is it remembered?

  • Yellow, Bersih's colour, became a symbol of Malaysia's reform movement.
08

Why does it matter?

Bersih showed how online organising and peaceful mass protest could challenge one-party dominance.

  • It brought together Malaysians across ethnic lines.

What other events are like this?

  • 1986The People Power Revolution — More than two million unarmed Filipinos filled a Manila highway to protect rebel soldiers and end Ferdinand Marcos's twenty-year rule.
  • 1973The Thai Student Uprising of 14 October 1973 — When thirteen activists were arrested for handing out leaflets calling for a constitution, half a million people filled the streets of Bangkok and brought down a military dictatorship.
  • 1955The Hock Lee Bus Strike — Bus workers sacked for joining a union went on strike, and Chinese-school students came to support them. A night of rioting left four people dead and became a defining moment in Singapore's history.
