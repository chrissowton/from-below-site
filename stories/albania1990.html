The Albanian Student Movement

In Europe's most isolated communist state, students began by protesting about power cuts in their dormitories. Within days they had won the right to form parties; within weeks they had toppled the statue of Enver Hoxha.

When
December 1990 – February 1991
Where
Student City, Tirana, Albania
Facts checked
2026-11-15

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Enver Hoxha ruled Albania from 1944 until his death in 1985. It was one of the most closed and repressive states in the world; religion was banned and tens of thousands were imprisoned.1

  • His successor, Ramiz Alia, introduced cautious reforms.1
  • Many Albanians did not know the Berlin Wall had fallen.1
  • In January 1990, people in Shkodër tried to pull down a statue of Stalin; protests spread to other towns.1
  • In July 1990, thousands of Albanians climbed into foreign embassies in Tirana, seeking to leave the country.

03

What happened?

In early December 1990, students at Tirana's Student City protested against power cuts and poor conditions. Their demands quickly became political.1

  • A student demonstration at the dormitories was crushed by the police.1
  • Ramiz Alia met a delegation of students to seek a compromise.1
  • On 11 December, the party allowed other political parties; the Democratic Party was founded the next day.1
  • In February 1991, students went on hunger strike, demanding that Enver Hoxha's name be removed from the university.
  • On 20 February 1991, a huge crowd pulled down Hoxha's statue in Skanderbeg Square.

04

What happened next?

  • Shkodër: on 2 April 1991, after disputed elections, police fired on protesters in Shkodër, killing four people.
  • Elections: the communists won the March 1991 election, but a general strike forced a coalition government. In 1992 the Democratic Party won, and Berisha became president.1
  • Exodus: tens of thousands of Albanians fled by boat to Italy in 1991.
05

What do others say?

At the time

State media first presented the students as hooligans; Alia then chose to negotiate.1

Debates since

  • Who led? Some argue that party insiders, such as Berisha, took over a movement started by students.1
  • Violence: the students' movement was mainly peaceful; police violence and later riots marked the transition.2
06

The evidence

  • Video footage of the protests and the toppling of the statue.1
  • Memoirs of student leaders.
07

How is it remembered?

  • 8 December is commemorated in Albania as the day of the student movement, and Albania issued stamps for its 20th anniversary.
08

Why does it matter?

Students broke the last communist regime in Europe outside the Soviet Union.1

  • Their movement began with everyday grievances and grew into a demand for democracy.

What other events are like this?

  • 1968Belgrade 1968: 'Down with the Red Bourgeoisie' — In socialist Yugoslavia, students occupied their university and accused the party elite of betraying socialism. Tito went on television and told the nation: 'The students are right.'
  • 1989Timișoara and the Romanian Revolution — It began with parishioners protecting their pastor from eviction in Timișoara. Within ten days, the most repressive regime in Eastern Europe had fallen — the only one in 1989 to fall in blood.
  • 1973The Thai Student Uprising of 14 October 1973 — When thirteen activists were arrested for handing out leaflets calling for a constitution, half a million people filled the streets of Bangkok and brought down a military dictatorship.
