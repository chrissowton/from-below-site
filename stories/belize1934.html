Antonio Soberanis and the Labourers of Belize

With mahogany exports collapsing and Belize City still in ruins after a hurricane, a barber stood up in Battlefield Park and demanded work and a minimum wage. 'I'd rather be a dead hero than a living coward,' he said.

When
1934 – 1935
Where
Belize City, British Honduras (now Belize)
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

British Honduras depended on the export of mahogany and other timber, controlled by a few British companies.1

  • The Great Depression cut demand for timber, and a hurricane in 1931 destroyed much of Belize City.1
  • By 1934, 1,800 people were registered as unemployed.1
  • An Unemployed Brigade marched for work. The governor offered 80 jobs and a feeding programme of rice porridge; many of its leaders gave up.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Soberanis called the leaders who gave up "cowards", and said he was not afraid to die.1

  • With others, he founded the Labourers and Unemployed Association (LUA) to demand relief work and a minimum wage.1
  • The LUA held mass meetings at Battlefield Park, in front of the courthouse, and organised boycotts, demonstrations and pickets of large merchants and companies.1
  • Soberanis travelled to Dangriga and Corozal to spread the movement.1
  • In October 1934, tensions boiled over: protesters clashed with police in Belize City, some shops were looted, and Soberanis was arrested. He was later charged with sedition.12

04

What happened next?

  • Prison: Soberanis was jailed for sedition in 1935.1
  • Reforms: the colonial government introduced relief work, and in 1941 trade unions were legalised.
  • Independence: the labour movement led to the People's United Party, founded in 1950, and to independence in 1981.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The colonial authorities saw Soberanis as a dangerous agitator; supporters saw a champion of the poor.2

Debates since

  • Violence: the movement mainly used meetings, boycotts and pickets, but the October 1934 clashes involved looting and fighting with police.12
  • Leadership: historians debate how much the movement depended on Soberanis himself.2

07

How is it remembered?

  • Soberanis is honoured in Belize as a pioneer of the labour movement.
08

Why does it matter?

The movement of 1934 is seen as the beginning of modern Belizean politics and the labour movement.

  • It was part of a wave of labour revolts across the British Caribbean in the 1930s.

What other events are like this?

  • 1937The Butler Riots: Trinidad's Oilfield Strike — Oil workers led by the preacher 'Buzz' Butler struck against low wages and racism. The violence that followed killed police and strikers alike — and the island's labour movement was born.
  • 1865The Morant Bay Rebellion — Thirty years after slavery ended, poor Black Jamaicans marched on a courthouse demanding justice and land. The governor's response — hundreds killed, flogged and hanged — scandalised Britain.
  • 1935The Copperbelt Strike of 1935 — When the colonial government raised the tax on African mineworkers, they walked out at three of the Copperbelt's great mines. Police shot six people dead — but the strike is remembered as the birth of Zambian nationalism.
