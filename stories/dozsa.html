György Dózsa's Peasant War

Peasants called up for a crusade against the Ottomans turned their weapons on the nobles instead. Led by a soldier and radical friars, they demanded the end of the nobility and the sharing of land.

When
April – July 1514
Where
Cegléd and the Great Hungarian Plain, Hungary
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1513, Pope Leo X called for a crusade against the Ottoman Empire, appealing directly to the common people.1

  • Hungarian peasants were heavily taxed and dominated by the landed nobility.1
  • Franciscan friars recruited the crusaders. Some had long preached radical ideas of social equality, influenced by Jan Hus.1
  • György Dózsa was a Székely soldier from Transylvania who had fought the Ottomans.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • György Dózsa

    Soldier and leader of the peasant army; executed in 1514.

  • Gergely Dózsa

    His younger brother, also executed.

  • Lőrinc Mészáros

    Franciscan friar and parish priest of Cegléd, a leader of the rising.

  • John Zápolya

    Transylvanian noble who defeated the peasants.

In their own words

03

What happened?

Within weeks, tens of thousands of peasants, wandering students, friars and priests gathered for the crusade.1

  • They were given no food or clothing. When landlords ordered them home for the harvest and attacked their families, they turned against the nobles.1
  • The crusade was cancelled, but Dózsa and the friars carried on, calling the nobles traitors.1
  • Their leaders called for the abolition of the nobility and for the lands of nobles and the Church to be shared among peasants.1
  • The rebels took Csanád, Arad and other fortresses.1
  • On 15 July 1514, they were defeated at Temesvár (Timișoara) by an army led by John Zápolya.1
  • Dózsa was executed by torture, seated on a heated iron throne.1
04

What happened next?

  • Repression: some 70,000 peasants are said to have been tortured.1
  • Serfdom: new laws drawn up by István Werbőczy (the Tripartitum) strengthened the nobility; serfdom lasted until 1848.1
  • Mohács: divisions within Hungary helped the Ottomans to defeat it in 1526.1
05

Why does it matter?

Dózsa's war was one of the largest peasant revolts in European history, with a radical programme of equality.

  • Its defeat tied Hungarian peasants to the land for more than three centuries.
  • The repression was terrible: some 70,000 peasants are said to have been tortured.1
  • New laws strengthened the nobility, and serfdom in Hungary lasted until 1848.1
  • Communist Hungary and Romania later honoured Dózsa as a hero, with streets, squares and statues in his name.

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

Noble writers portrayed the rebels as criminals; Dózsa's proclamations called the nobles traitors.1

Debates since

  • Programme: the radical aims come mainly from the research of Sándor Márki in 1913, and some historians question how far they were shared.1
  • Leadership: recent research suggests a mercenary captain, Menyhért, led the camp at first, and the two are often confused.1
  • Violence on both sides: the rebels impaled the bishop of Csanád and tortured the treasurer to death, though they often released nobles who surrendered; the nobles' repression was far more brutal.1
How do we know?The evidence, and a source from the time
  • Dózsa's proclamations and royal decrees.
  • Diplomatic reports and noble chronicles.1

Book illustration
Made by, and when
Stephanus Taurinus, 'Stauromachia', Vienna, 1519

A Latin poem on the 'servile war' written by a churchman five years after the rising, with an illustration of Dózsa's execution. It tells the story from the side of the victors.

Reading it

The poem was written by a churchman five years after the rising, from the side of the victors, and its illustration dwells on Dózsa's execution. The nobles wanted his punishment widely known, as a warning. The peasants left no account of their own — what happened to them afterwards is told in the laws that bound them to the land.
