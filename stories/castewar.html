The Caste War of Yucatán

Maya peasants losing their land to sugar and henequen estates rose against the ruling Yucatecos. They nearly took the whole peninsula, and an independent Maya state survived in the forest for half a century.

When
1847 – 1901
Where
Tepich and Chan Santa Cruz, Yucatán Peninsula, Mexico

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

After the Spanish conquest, Yucatán was ruled by people of Spanish descent, the Yucatecos, while most Maya were poor peasants.1

  • After Mexican independence in 1821, new laws helped private estates take over Maya communal lands for sugar and henequen (sisal).1
  • By the 1840s, many Maya were forced into debt labour on large estates (haciendas).1
  • Rival Yucateco factions armed Maya soldiers and promised to end their taxes, then broke their promises.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Cecilio Chi

    Maya leader of Tepich.

  • Jacinto Pat

    Maya batab (leader) who called for liberty.

  • Manuel Antonio Ay

    Maya leader executed in Valladolid in 1847.

  • Santiago Méndez

    Governor of Yucatán.

03

What happened?

In 1847, fearing a revolt, Governor Méndez had Manuel Antonio Ay executed, burned the town of Tepich and sacked other Maya towns.1

  • On 30 July 1847, Cecilio Chi and Jacinto Pat attacked Tepich, and the war began.1
  • By spring 1848, the Maya held most of Yucatán except the walled cities of Mérida and Campeche.1
  • Yucatán's governor asked Spain, Britain and the United States for help; none would intervene, and Yucatán rejoined Mexico.1
  • From 1850, the "Talking Cross" at Chan Santa Cruz inspired the Maya to fight on, and an independent Maya state grew up around it.1
  • Britain recognised and traded with the Maya state, and British Honduras supplied it with arms.1

04

What happened next?

  • End: in 1893, Britain recognised Mexican control and cut off trade. The Mexican army occupied Chan Santa Cruz in 1901.1
  • Reform: in 1915, Governor Salvador Alvarado brought reforms from the Mexican Revolution that ended some Maya grievances. Small settlements resisted until 1933.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Yucatecos presented it as a race war; Maya leaders wrote that they wanted liberty and an end to heavy taxes.1

Debates since

  • Caste or class? Some historians see it as an inter-ethnic conflict; not all Maya joined, and some fought against the rebels.1
  • Violence on both sides: after government forces massacred Maya at Tepich and elsewhere, Cecilio Chi ordered all non-Maya killed; government troops and militias in turn killed and enslaved Maya, and captured Maya were sold to Cuba as forced labourers.12
06

The evidence

  • Letters from Maya leaders to British Honduras.1
  • Yucatecan military records and press.
07

How is it remembered?

  • The town of Felipe Carrillo Puerto, once Chan Santa Cruz, keeps the Balam Na temple of the Talking Cross.
08

Why does it matter?

The Caste War was one of the most successful Indigenous revolts in the Americas in modern times.1

  • It was rooted in the defence of communal land against plantation agriculture.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did the loss of communal land drive the Maya to war?
  2. How did the Talking Cross help keep the rebellion alive?
  3. Why did Britain help the Maya state, and then stop?
  4. Is 'Caste War' a fair name for the conflict?
