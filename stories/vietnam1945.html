Viet Nam: From the August Revolution to Reunification

In August 1945, after a famine that killed hundreds of thousands, the Việt Minh took power and declared Viet Nam independent. It took thirty years of war — first against France, then against the United States and its South Vietnamese ally — before the country was reunited.

01

What is the background to this event?

France ruled Viet Nam as part of French Indochina. Risings such as the Yên Bái mutiny and the Nghệ-Tĩnh soviets of 1930 were crushed.12

  • During the Second World War, Japan occupied Indochina. In 1944–45, famine killed between 400,000 and 2 million people in the north.3
  • The Việt Minh, a communist-led independence front, built support in the countryside.4

03

What happened?

After Japan's surrender, the Việt Minh took control of Hanoi on 19 August 1945 and of the north within days. Emperor Bảo Đại abdicated on 30 August.4

  • Independence: on 2 September 1945, Hồ Chí Minh read a Declaration of Independence at Ba Đình, Hanoi.5
  • War with France: France fought to restore its rule. In November 1946, French warships shelled Haiphong, killing between 2,000 and 6,000 people. In 1954, the Việt Minh defeated the French at Điện Biên Phủ, and the Geneva Accords divided the country.67
  • War with the United States: the United States backed South Viet Nam against the communist North and the southern insurgency. The war brought massacres such as Mỹ Lai (by US troops) and Huế (by communist forces), and the use of Agent Orange.8
  • Reunification: North Vietnamese forces captured Saigon on 30 April 1975.9
04

What happened next?

  • The cost: a 1995 demographic study counted 791,000 to 1,141,000 Vietnamese war deaths in 1965–75 alone; the Vietnamese government estimated 1.1 million communist soldiers and 2 million civilians dead in 1955–75.8
  • Exile: after 1975, many South Vietnamese left the country.
05

Why does it matter?

Viet Nam's long struggle was one of the defining anti-colonial wars of the 20th century, and the war with the United States inspired protest movements around the world.8

  • A 1995 study counted between 791,000 and 1,141,000 Vietnamese war deaths in 1965–75 alone.8
  • For many who left the South after 1975, 30 April is remembered as a day of loss rather than reunification.
  • The war with the United States inspired protest movements around the world.8

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

The Việt Minh and their successors presented the wars as a single struggle for national independence; France, the United States and the South Vietnamese government presented them as a fight against communism.4

Debates since

  • The land reform: in the North in 1953–56, estimates of executions range from 800 to 200,000; the government itself admitted serious mistakes.10
  • The death toll: estimates of deaths vary widely depending on the years and places covered.8
How do we know?The evidence, and a source from the time
  • Government and military records on all sides, photographs, news reports, and the memories of survivors.8

Reading it

The crowd at Ba Đình square as Hồ Chí Minh read the Declaration of Independence. Its size was a statement in itself. He had asked American intelligence officers for ideas for the declaration, hoping for US support — a hope that would turn bitterly in the thirty years it took to make independence real.

How is it remembered?Memorials, anniversaries and arguments over memory
  • In Viet Nam, 2 September is National Day and 30 April is celebrated as Reunification Day; for many who left the South, 30 April is remembered as a day of loss.
