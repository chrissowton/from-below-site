The Candle Demonstration

Thousands of Slovak Catholics stood silently in a square holding candles to ask for religious freedom. The communist police turned water cannon and batons on them — and the protest became a first step towards the Velvet Revolution.

When
25 March 1988
Where
Hviezdoslav Square, Bratislava, Slovakia
Facts checked
2026-11-15

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Since the crushing of the Prague Spring in 1968, Czechoslovakia had been ruled by a hard-line communist regime.

  • Religious practice was heavily restricted, and there had been no mass demonstration against the regime since 1969.1
  • Catholic dissidents in Slovakia ran a "secret church" and samizdat (underground) publications.

03

What happened?

The demonstration was planned by Catholic dissidents to ask for religious freedom and civil rights.1

  • The authorities refused permission.1
  • News of it spread through Vatican Radio, Radio Free Europe and Voice of America.1
  • On 25 March 1988, about 5,000 people gathered in Hviezdoslav Square holding candles, with thousands more in nearby streets.1
  • Police used water cannon, then attacked the protesters with batons, while senior officials watched from a hotel.1
04

What happened next?

  • Revolution: the demonstration was the first major step towards the Velvet Revolution of November 1989.1
  • Resignation: the Slovak minister of culture, who disagreed with the police action, resigned that autumn.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The regime called the protest an illegal provocation; the protesters asked for basic rights.1

Debates since

  • Its importance: Slovaks see it as the start of the end of communism in Czechoslovakia.1
  • Violence: the protesters were peaceful; the police used water cannon and batons, and arrested many.1
06

The evidence

  • Photographs and video recorded secretly on the day, and broadcast abroad.
07

How is it remembered?

  • 25 March is commemorated in Slovakia as the Day of Struggle for Human Rights.1
08

Why does it matter?

The Candle Demonstration showed that silent, peaceful protest could challenge a police state.

  • It linked religious freedom to wider human rights.
