The Ådalen Shootings

Thousands of striking sawmill and pulp workers marched on the quarters of strike-breakers. Soldiers opened fire, killing five people. Sweden never again used its army against its own workers.

When
14 May 1931
Where
Lunde, Ådalen, Sweden
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1931, in the depths of the Depression, a long dispute over wage cuts at a pulp factory led workers at other plants in the Ådalen valley to strike in sympathy.1

  • The owner of the Graninge company hired about 60 strike-breakers, who arrived on 12 May.1
  • On 13 May, workers marched to the Sandviken plant and attacked some of the strike-breakers.1
  • The county authorities called in soldiers to protect the strike-breakers. They were met by protesters, allegedly throwing stones.1

03

What happened?

On 14 May 1931, a union rally voted for a general strike in the timber and pulp industries of Ådalen. Several thousand people then marched to the strike-breakers' quarters at Lunde.1

  • Mounted soldiers tried and failed to stop the march. In the confusion a rider fell, and another fired warning shots.1
  • Captain Mesterton said he believed the marchers were armed. At less than 100 metres, he ordered his men to fire.1
  • The soldiers aimed at the ground, but ricochets hit the crowd. A machine gun was then fired.1
  • Five people were killed and five injured.1
  • An inquiry found no evidence that the marchers had been armed.1

04

What happened next?

  • Protest: huge demonstrations followed across Sweden.
  • Trials: strike leaders, including Axel Nordström, received prison sentences; the officers received light punishments or were acquitted.
  • Politics: the Social Democrats won the 1932 election and governed for decades.
  • The army: the use of soldiers against civilians was ended, and in 1938 unions and employers signed the Saltsjöbaden Agreement to settle disputes peacefully.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Conservatives blamed Communist agitators; the labour movement blamed the authorities for sending soldiers. The Social Democrats criticised both the military and the Communists.2

Debates since

  • Violence: strikers had beaten strike-breakers the day before; the marchers on 14 May were unarmed.1
  • Memory: historians such as Roger Johansson have shown how left and right have told the story differently.2

07

How is it remembered?

  • A monument by Lenny Clarhäll stands at Lunde.
  • Bo Widerberg's film Ådalen 31 (1969) dramatised the events.
08

Why does it matter?

Ådalen is seen as a turning point in Swedish history, from open class conflict towards the "Swedish model" of negotiation.

  • It ended the use of the military in labour disputes.
