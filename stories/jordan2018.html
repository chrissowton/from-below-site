Jordan's Tax Protests

A general strike called by more than 30 trade unions, and night after night of protest during Ramadan, brought down a prime minister and stopped a tax law — almost without violence.

When
30 May – 7 June 2018
Where
The Fourth Circle, Amman, and across Jordan
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Jordan has few natural resources and hosts very large numbers of refugees from Palestine, Iraq and Syria. Its public debt rose from 60 per cent of national income in 2011 to 93 per cent in 2016.1

  • From 2016, the government of Hani Mulki adopted austerity measures backed by the International Monetary Fund.1
  • In May 2018, it sent parliament a new income tax law that would have brought many more people into the tax system.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • The professional associations and trade unions

    More than 30 unions that called the general strike.

  • Young protesters at the Fourth Circle

    From the middle and poorer classes, not led by the traditional opposition.

  • Hani Mulki

    Prime minister, who resigned on 4 June 2018.

  • Omar Razzaz

    The education minister who replaced him and withdrew the bill.

  • King Abdullah II

    Who froze the price rises and accepted Mulki's resignation.

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 30 May 2018, more than 30 trade unions held a general strike against the tax bill.1

  • The next day, the government raised fuel and electricity prices. That night, crowds gathered at the Fourth Circle in Amman, near the prime minister's office.1
  • Protests spread across the country in unusually large numbers.1
  • On 1 June, King Abdullah ordered the price rises frozen.1
  • The protests continued each night after the Ramadan fast. Some protesters burned tyres and blocked roads, but the protests were largely peaceful, and few casualties were reported.1
  • On 4 June, Mulki resigned. Protests ended only after the new prime minister, Omar Razzaz, promised to withdraw the bill.1

04

What happened next?

  • A revised law: a modified income tax law was passed later in 2018.
  • The teachers: in 2019, Jordan's teachers held a month-long strike and won a pay rise; in 2020 the government closed their union and arrested its leaders.
  • The system: governments changed, but power remained with the king.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government said the tax law was needed to control the debt; protesters said ordinary people were paying for corruption and mismanagement.1

Debates since

  • Safety valve? Some analysts argue that dismissing prime ministers lets the monarchy absorb protest without real change.2
  • Policing: the restraint shown by both protesters and security forces was widely noted.1

How is it remembered?

  • "The Fourth Circle" has become shorthand in Jordan for popular protest.
08

Why does it matter?

The protests showed that organised workers and a new generation could force a government to back down peacefully.

  • They revived a tradition going back to 1989, when riots over price rises led to the return of elections.
  • They show how international lenders' conditions can set off unrest.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did a tax law cause such anger?
  2. Why were trade unions able to lead the protests?
  3. Why did the protests stay largely peaceful?
  4. Did changing the prime minister change anything?

What other events are like this?

  • 2005Niger's Campaign Against the High Cost of Living — When the government of one of the world's poorest countries put VAT on flour, milk and sugar, a coalition of unions and associations brought tens of thousands into the streets — and forced a climbdown.
  • 2019Lebanon's 17 October Revolution — A tax on WhatsApp calls sparked a nationwide uprising against a political system built on sectarian division. 'All of them means all of them,' protesters chanted — demanding the removal of the entire political class.
  • 2010The Tunisian Revolution — A street vendor's desperate protest in a small town sparked a month of uprising that toppled a dictator of 23 years and began the Arab Spring.
