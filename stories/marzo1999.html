The Paraguayan March

After the vice-president was assassinated, young Paraguayans and farmers occupied the squares outside Congress for days. Snipers killed seven of them, and the president resigned.

When
23 – 28 March 1999
Where
Plaza de la Independencia, Asunción, Paraguay
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

Paraguay had been ruled by the dictator Alfredo Stroessner until 1989, and its young democracy was fragile.

  • President Raúl Cubas Grau had freed his ally, General Lino Oviedo, who was serving a sentence for a failed coup.1
  • Vice-President Luis María Argaña was a rival of Oviedo.1
  • On 23 March 1999, Argaña was assassinated; the opposition blamed Cubas and Oviedo.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Argaña's assassination led to demonstrations by opponents and supporters of the government.1

  • Thousands of young people, along with farmers who were already in the capital, gathered in the squares outside Congress to demand Cubas's removal.
  • Tanks took up positions nearby.1
  • On the evening of 26 March, snipers in nearby buildings opened fire, killing seven demonstrators.1
  • On 28 March, facing impeachment, Cubas resigned.1

04

What happened next?

  • New president: Luis González Macchi, head of the Senate, became president.1
  • Exile: Oviedo fled abroad; Cubas went into exile in Brazil.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Supporters of Oviedo claimed the opposition armed protesters; the protesters blamed Oviedo's supporters for the snipers.1

Debates since

  • Factions: the crisis was partly a struggle between rival factions of the ruling Colorado Party, but the young protesters acted independently.1
  • Violence: there were clashes between rival groups of demonstrators; snipers killed seven anti-government protesters.1

How is it remembered?

08

Why does it matter?

The Paraguayan March showed young people defending a fragile democracy in the streets, at great cost.
