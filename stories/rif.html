Abd el-Krim and the Republic of the Rif

Berber mountain tribes inflicted one of the worst defeats on a European army in colonial history and founded a republic. Spain and France needed hundreds of thousands of troops — and poison gas — to crush it.

When
1921 – 1926
Where
Ajdir and the Rif mountains, northern Morocco
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1912, Morocco was divided into French and Spanish protectorates. Spain took the north, including the Rif mountains, home to Amazigh (Berber) tribes who had long resisted outside control.1

  • From 1909, Rif tribes attacked Spanish workers building railways to iron mines near Melilla, leading to years of fighting.1
  • Muhammad ibn Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi — Abd el-Krim — had worked for the Spanish administration as a judge and journalist, but turned against Spanish rule.

In their own words

03

What happened?

In July 1921, Spanish forces under General Silvestre, spread thinly across 144 forts and posts, were attacked by Abd el-Krim's fighters at Annual.1

  • The Spanish army collapsed in what Spain called the "Disaster of Annual". Some 8,000 soldiers were reported killed or missing at Annual, and the total Spanish death toll in the rout was reported as 13,192.1
  • Many Spanish soldiers were killed after surrendering. At Monte Arruit, the Spanish found the bodies of 2,600 of the garrison when they retook the fort in 1922.1
  • In September 1921, Abd el-Krim proclaimed the Republic of the Rif, with its own government, taxes and courts.1

Unable to defeat the Rif, the Spanish military turned to chemical weapons, dropping mustard gas on villages, markets and fields.1 In 1925, after the Rif attacked the French zone, France joined the war. A combined force of hundreds of thousands of French and Spanish troops, with aircraft and tanks, and a large Spanish landing at Al Hoceima, wore down the Rif.1

In 1926, Abd el-Krim surrendered to the French and was exiled.1

04

What happened next?

  • Exile: Abd el-Krim was exiled to the island of Réunion. In 1947 he escaped in Egypt and helped inspire North African independence movements from Cairo.
  • Spain: the disaster at Annual helped bring down Spain's parliamentary government; General Primo de Rivera seized power in 1923. Officers who fought in Morocco, including Francisco Franco, later led the 1936 coup that began the Spanish Civil War.
  • Gas: studies suggest that the gas attacks may have caused long-term health effects in the Rif, and campaigners have called on Spain to acknowledge them.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Spain and France called the Rif rebels savages. Anti-colonial movements and left-wing parties in Europe — including the French Communist Party — supported the Rif.

Historians' debates

  • A modern state? Historians such as C. R. Pennell show that the Republic of the Rif built a functioning government, not just a tribal alliance.
  • Violence on both sides: Rif fighters killed many Spanish prisoners after Annual and Monte Arruit; Spanish and French forces used chemical weapons and bombed civilians.1
  • Memory in Morocco: the Rif's relationship with the Moroccan monarchy has been tense; the Rif rose again in 1958–59 and in the Hirak protests of 2016–17.

How is it remembered?

  • Abd el-Krim is a hero across North Africa and the anti-colonial world.
  • The Rif flag was carried by protesters in the Hirak movement of 2016–17.
08

Why does it matter?

The Rif War was one of the most important anti-colonial wars of the twentieth century. Abd el-Krim's guerrilla tactics influenced later liberation movements, from Vietnam to Algeria.

  • It shows how a mountain people could challenge European armies.
  • It was one of the first uses of chemical weapons from the air against civilians.
  • It shaped the history of Spain as well as Morocco.
