The Strike of the Century

Facing an austerity package after the loss of the Congo, 700,000 Belgian workers walked out in the depths of winter. In the industrial south, the strike lasted six weeks and changed the shape of the country.

When
14 December 1960 – January 1961
Where
Liège, Charleroi and across Wallonia, Belgium
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1960, Belgium lost its colony, the Congo. The government of Gaston Eyskens proposed a package of austerity measures, the "Unitary Law" (Loi unique), to cut the deficit.1

  • The law raised taxes and cut public spending, including on benefits.1
  • Wallonia, the French-speaking south, depended on coal and steel, which were already declining.1

03

What happened?

On 14 December 1960, the socialist union federation, the FGTB, began a strike against the Unitary Law.1

  • About 700,000 workers joined. Railways, ports, mines and factories stopped.1
  • In Flanders, most workers returned after a few days. In Wallonia the strike continued for weeks.1
  • There were clashes between strikers and the police, the gendarmerie and the army. On 6 January 1961, strikers ransacked the Liège-Guillemins railway station.1
  • Four strikers died.1
  • The strike gradually ended in late January, and the law was passed on 14 February 1961.1
04

What happened next?

  • Walloon movement: Renard founded the Walloon Popular Movement in 1961, linking trade unionism with demands for Walloon self-government.1
  • Federal Belgium: the strike deepened the divide between Flanders and Wallonia, which led to Belgium becoming a federal state.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government and Catholic unions called it a political strike against an elected parliament; the strikers said they were defending workers' living standards.2

Debates since

  • Violence: the strike included sabotage and attacks on property, and clashes with the gendarmerie and army; four strikers died.1
  • Class or nation? Historians debate whether it was mainly a workers' struggle or the birth of Walloon nationalism.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • The strike is remembered in Wallonia as the "Strike of the Century", and was the subject of the film Hiver 60 (1983).1
08

Why does it matter?

Called "one of the most serious class confrontations in Belgium's social history", the strike reshaped Belgian politics.1

  • It shows how economic struggle and regional identity can become linked.

Open questions

  1. Why did the loss of the Congo lead to austerity?
  2. Why did the strike last longer in Wallonia than in Flanders?
  3. Was the strike a failure, given that the law was passed?
  4. How did a strike help to turn Belgium into a federal state?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 1969Italy's Hot Autumn — Migrant workers from southern Italy, students and factory workers came together in a wave of strikes that shook Italy's car plants and tyre factories — and won a charter of workers' rights.
  • 1919The Winnipeg General Strike — For six weeks, 30,000 workers brought Canada's third-largest city to a halt to win the right to collective bargaining. It ended with 'Bloody Saturday' — and transformed Canadian politics.
  • 1913The Dublin Lockout — When 300 employers locked out some 20,000 workers to destroy a new union of the unskilled, Dublin's poorest families held out for five months, fed by food ships from British workers.
