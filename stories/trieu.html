Lady Triệu's Rising

Two centuries after the Trưng sisters, a young woman led the people of northern Vietnam against Chinese rule. She is remembered as riding into battle on an elephant.

When
248
Where
Jiuzhen (Thanh Hóa), Vietnam
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Northern Vietnam (Jiaozhi and Jiuzhen) was ruled by the Chinese kingdom of Eastern Wu.1

  • In 226, the Wu ruler Sun Quan sent troops to take direct control; they killed the local ruling family and, according to the sources, ten thousand people in Jiuzhen.1
  • In 231, Wu sent another general to "pacify" the Yue peoples.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In 248, the people of Jiaozhi and Jiuzhen rose against Wu.1

  • Lady Triệu led the rising in Jiuzhen, followed by a hundred chieftains and fifty thousand families.1
  • Wu sent Lu Yin against the rebels. After several months of war, she was killed.1
  • A later Vietnamese writer described her fighting on an elephant.1
04

What happened next?

  • Chinese rule: Chinese dynasties continued to rule northern Vietnam until the tenth century.
  • Memory: she became one of Vietnam's national heroines, alongside the Trưng sisters.
05

What do others say?

At the time

For the Wu authorities, the revolt was a rising of "barbarians" to be crushed.3

Debates since

  • The sources: Keith Taylor wrote that Chinese records did not mention her; Catherine Churchman has shown that the oldest and most detailed account comes from a Chinese chronicle of the Jin period.1
  • National story: modern Vietnamese history presents her as part of a long struggle for independence; some scholars question reading modern nationalism back into the third century.1
  • Violence: the revolt was suppressed by force after Wu's earlier massacre in Jiuzhen.1
06

The evidence

  • The Jiaozhou ji, quoted in later Chinese encyclopedias.1
  • Vietnamese chronicles and temple traditions.
07

How is it remembered?

  • Her temple in Hậu Lộc, Thanh Hóa, holds an annual festival, and streets across Vietnam bear her name.
08

Why does it matter?

Lady Triệu is a symbol of resistance to foreign rule and of women's leadership in Vietnamese history.

  • Her story also shows how heroes are reshaped by later writers.

What other events are like this?

  • 40 CEThe Trưng Sisters' Rebellion — Two sisters led the peoples of the Red River Delta in a revolt against Chinese rule, drove out the Han governor and ruled as queens for three years.
  • 60 CEBoudica's Revolt — When Roman officials seized the kingdom of the Iceni, flogged its queen and raped her daughters, Boudica led the peoples of eastern Britain in a revolt that burned three Roman towns — before ending in a massacre.
  • 18 CEThe Red Eyebrows — When the Yellow River flooded and Wang Mang's land reforms failed, starving farmers painted their eyebrows red to tell friend from foe. Their rebellion helped bring down an emperor — and was joined by a mother avenging her son.
