The Saya San Rebellion

When the Great Depression crashed rice prices, a healer and former monk proclaimed himself king and led Burmese peasants against British rule and their taxes.

When
December 1930 – 1932
Where
Tharrawaddy, Myanmar
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Britain annexed all of Burma in 1886, after the Third Anglo-Burmese War, and ruled it as a province of India.1

  • In 1930, the Great Depression caused rice prices to collapse. Rice was Burma's main export, and peasants depended on it.1
  • Land had become concentrated in fewer hands, and many rice farmers had become landless.1
  • Saya San, a healer and former monk, had investigated peasant conditions for a nationalist organisation in 1924.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Saya San

    Healer and former monk who led the rebellion; hanged in 1931.

  • The Galon peasants

    Rice farmers who joined his army.

  • Ba Maw

    Lawyer who defended Saya San at his trial.

In their own words

03

What happened?

In December 1930, Saya San was crowned king, and set up a royal city at Tharrawaddy.1

  • On 22 December 1930, the first outbreak of the rebellion took place in the Tharrawaddy district.1
  • His followers formed the "Galon Army", named after the mythical bird that defeats the serpent, and believed charms and tattoos would protect them.1
  • The rebellion spread across Lower Burma.
  • Saya San fled to the Shan Hills, but was captured in August 1931.1
04

What happened next?

  • Repression: by the end of 1932, more than 1,000 rebels had been killed and 9,000 captured or surrendered.1
  • Executions: Saya San and 125 others were hanged, and nearly 1,400 were imprisoned or transported.1
05

Why does it matter?

The Saya San Rebellion is regarded as a key anti-colonial movement in Southeast Asia.1

  • It was a peasant revolt driven by the global economic crisis.
  • By the end of 1932, more than 1,000 rebels had been killed and 9,000 captured or surrendered.1
  • Saya San is honoured as a national hero in Myanmar.

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

British officials blamed superstition; nationalists saw a revolt against foreign rule and economic misery.3

Debates since

  • Tradition or modern politics? Patricia Herbert and others argue the rebellion was rooted in modern nationalist politics, not just traditional beliefs.3
  • Violence on both sides: rebels attacked officials, police and others; British forces killed more than 1,000 rebels and hanged 126.1

Burma, 1920s

A portrait of Saya San, the healer and former monk who led the rebellion of 1930–32, taken before the rising. The British authorities later hanged him, and their official report blamed the rebellion on the 'superstition' of Burmese peasants rather than on the peasants' economic grievances.

Reading it

This portrait of Saya San, the healer and former monk, was taken before the rising. The British later hanged him, and their official report blamed the rebellion on peasant 'superstition' rather than economic grievance. That explanation suited the authorities: it put the problem in the peasants' heads, not in colonial policy.
