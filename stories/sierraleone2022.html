"Bio Must Go": Sierra Leone's Cost-of-Living Protests

With inflation at 28% and protest effectively banned, market women marched in Freetown. Weeks later, protests called through WhatsApp turned deadly, and the government called them an attempted overthrow.

When
July – August 2022
Where
Freetown, Makeni and Kamakwie, Sierra Leone
Facts checked
2026-11-17

01

What is the background to this event?

By June 2022, inflation in Sierra Leone had reached 28%, driven by the effects of the Covid-19 pandemic, the Russian invasion of Ukraine and a global food crisis.1

  • Many Sierra Leoneans felt the government was doing little to help and failing to explain the economic problems.1
  • The Public Order Act of 1965 required police permission to protest, which was usually refused.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In July 2022, women working in Freetown's markets led a peaceful protest. Dozens were arrested, and there were allegations of police brutality.1

  • The movement that followed had no clear leaders; participants called it "faceless". Calls to protest spread on WhatsApp.1
  • On 10 August 2022, violent protests broke out in Freetown and in the north. Protesters threw rocks, set fires and chanted "Bio must go". Police used tear gas and guns.1
  • The internet was shut down for several hours, and the vice-president declared a nationwide curfew.1
  • At least 27 civilians and 6 police officers died, according to Wikipedia's summary of reports.1
  • The president called the protests "terrorism" and blamed Sierra Leoneans living abroad. He said that the protesters' aim was not the cost of living but the violent overthrow of an elected government.1

04

What happened next?

  • Arrests: 515 people were arrested, including for the killings of police officers, arson and breaking the curfew.1
  • Unequal burials: police officers who died were given a state funeral and their families received 100 million leones. The bodies of civilians who died were released in October and buried publicly; their families were offered 20 million leones but were not allowed to bury them themselves.1
  • Accountability: Amnesty International called for the authorities to be held accountable.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government said the protests were an attempt to overthrow it, not a protest about prices; protesters and human rights groups pointed to hardship and to restrictions on the right to protest.1

Debates since

  • Violence: both protesters and police were killed. Wikipedia reports at least 27 civilians and 6 police officers dead; Amnesty International called for accountability for the killings.1
06

The evidence

  • Government statements, news reports, NetBlocks' monitoring of the internet shutdown, and reports by Amnesty International.1
07

How is it remembered?

  • The protests were described as unusual for a country that had been relatively stable for years.1
08

Why does it matter?

The protests showed how the global rise in prices in 2022 hit poorer countries hardest — and how, where protest is effectively banned, anger can erupt in violence that both sides then blame on the other.1

Questions to think about

  1. Why did events far away, such as the war in Ukraine, cause hardship in Sierra Leone?
  2. How might a law requiring police permission to protest affect the way protests happen?
  3. Why did the government blame Sierra Leoneans abroad?
  4. Why do you think the families of police officers and civilians were treated differently?

What other events are like this?

  • 1898The Hut Tax War — When the British imposed a tax on every house in the new Sierra Leone Protectorate, the Temne leader Bai Bureh led a guerrilla war against them, while a separate Mende rising killed traders and missionaries. Both were crushed.
  • 2018Jordan's Tax Protests — A general strike called by more than 30 trade unions, and night after night of protest during Ramadan, brought down a prime minister and stopped a tax law — almost without violence.
  • 2024Reject Finance Bill: Kenya's Gen Z Protests — Young Kenyans organised online against a bill that would have taxed bread and sanitary pads. They forced the president to withdraw it, but at least 60 people were killed in 2024, and dozens more when protests returned in 2025.
