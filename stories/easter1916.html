The Easter Rising

While Britain fought the First World War, a few hundred republicans — Volunteers, socialists of the Citizen Army and women of Cumann na mBan — seized the centre of Dublin and proclaimed an Irish Republic. They held out for six days. The executions that followed turned Ireland towards independence.

01

What is the background to this event?

For decades, many Irish people had sought self-government through the Home Rule movement, within the United Kingdom.1

  • On the outbreak of the First World War in 1914, a Home Rule law was passed, but it was postponed until the war was over.1
  • The Dublin Lockout of 1913 had led to the founding of the Irish Citizen Army, a workers' militia led by the socialist James Connolly.2
  • A secret seven-man Military Council of the Irish Republican Brotherhood planned a rising while Britain was at war.1

03

What happened?

On Easter Monday, 24 April 1916, members of the Irish Volunteers, the Irish Citizen Army and about 200 women of Cumann na mBan seized important buildings in Dublin and proclaimed an Irish Republic.1

  • They made the General Post Office on Sackville Street (now O'Connell Street) their headquarters and raised two republican flags.1
  • British forces brought in reinforcements and artillery. Much of the city centre was destroyed.1
  • On Saturday 29 April, Pearse agreed to an unconditional surrender.1
  • Of the 485 people killed, 260 were civilians, many killed by British artillery fire or mistaken for rebels; 143 were British soldiers and police; and 82 were rebels, including those executed afterwards.1
04

What happened next?

  • Executions: sixteen leaders of the Rising were executed after courts martial, starting in May 1916. About 3,500 people were taken prisoner, and 1,800 were sent to internment camps or prisons in Britain.1
  • A change of mood: the executions, and the political developments that followed, increased popular support for independence. In the 1918 election, Sinn Féin won 73 of Ireland's 105 seats.1
  • Independence and civil war: the War of Independence (1919–21), the Treaty and the Civil War (1922–23) followed.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The British authorities treated the Rising as a wartime rebellion and executed its leaders after courts martial; the executions, and what followed, increased popular support for independence.1

Debates since

  • A 'blood sacrifice'? Historians debate whether the leaders expected to win or meant to inspire others through their deaths.
  • Civilian deaths: more than half of those killed were civilians.1
06

The evidence

  • The Proclamation of the Republic, photographs of the ruined city, court-martial records, and the witness statements later collected from participants.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • The Rising is commemorated every Easter in Ireland, and the General Post Office remains its best-known symbol.
08

Why does it matter?

The Rising failed as a military action but transformed Irish politics. It also brought together nationalists, socialists and women, each with their own vision of what an Irish republic should be.1

Open questions

  1. Why did the rebels choose to rise while Britain was at war?
  2. Why did socialists like James Connolly join a nationalist rising?
  3. Why did the executions change public opinion?
  4. Was the Rising justified? How might different people at the time have answered?

What other events are like this?

  • 1913The Dublin Lockout — When 300 employers locked out some 20,000 workers to destroy a new union of the unskilled, Dublin's poorest families held out for five months, fed by food ships from British workers.
  • 1798The United Irishmen and the Rising of 1798 — Protestants, Catholics and Dissenters united to demand an Irish republic. The rising that followed was crushed with terrible violence — and violence was also committed in its name.
  • 1919Egypt's 1919 Revolution — When Britain exiled the nationalist leader Saad Zaghlul, Egyptians of every class and religion rose up in strikes and demonstrations — and women marched in public for the first time.
