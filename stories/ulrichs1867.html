Karl Heinrich Ulrichs Speaks Out

A German lawyer stood up before a congress of jurists to call for an end to laws against love between men. He was shouted down — but it was the first time anyone had publicly defended homosexuality in this way.

When
29 August 1867
Where
Munich, Bavaria (Germany)
Facts checked
2026-11-22

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In the 1860s, German laws punished love between men, and few dared to discuss it in public.1

  • Karl Heinrich Ulrichs, a lawyer from Hanover, had resigned from his post as a judge in 1854 rather than risk being blackmailed and exposed.1
  • In 1862, he told his family and friends that he was, in his own word, an 'Urning' — a man who loves men. He began writing pamphlets under a pen name, arguing that such love was natural.1
  • His books were confiscated and banned by police in Saxony in 1864, and later throughout Prussia.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In 1867, Ulrichs went to the congress of the Association of German Jurists in Munich, planning to propose that Germany's laws against homosexuality be reformed.1

  • The congress's committee banned his motion. On 29 August, the final day, he rose to protest against his exclusion.1
  • He became the first homosexual man to speak out publicly in defence of homosexuality. He was shouted down, though some in the audience appear to have been stirred to support him.1
  • He then published an account of the day under his own name and sent it to all the lawyers who had been there.1
04

What happened next?

  • Under his own name: from then on, Ulrichs published his pamphlets openly. He wrote twelve in all, the last in 1879.1
  • A new word: in 1868, Karl-Maria Kertbeny coined the word 'homosexual' in a letter to Ulrichs, and from the 1870s the subject began to be widely discussed.1
  • Exile: Ulrichs left Germany for Italy and died in L'Aquila in 1895. His gravestone reads exul et pauper, 'exile and pauper'.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The congress refused even to hear Ulrichs's motion; he argued that men like him were born that way and should not be punished.1

Debates since

  • The 'first gay man'? The sexologist Volkmar Sigusch called Ulrichs the "first gay man in world history"; others point out that many people before him loved others of the same sex, though few defended it in public.1
  • His theory: Ulrichs described a man who loves men as having a female soul in a male body — an idea that later campaigners left behind.1
06

The evidence

  • Ulrichs's own pamphlets, including his account of the Munich congress, and his papers, some of which were found in Prussian archives and published in 2004.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • Streets are named after him in Munich, Bremen, Hanover and Berlin, and his birthday is marked each year by a street party at Karl-Heinrich-Ulrichs-Platz in Munich. The International Lesbian and Gay Law Association gives an award in his name.1
08

Why does it matter?

Ulrichs was the first homosexual man known to have stood up in public to defend love between men. He is regarded today as a pioneer of sexology and of the modern gay rights movement.1
