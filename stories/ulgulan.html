Birsa Munda and the Ulgulan

A young Munda prophet called on his people to end the queen's rule and restore their own. His 'great tumult' against landlords, missionaries and the British ended with his death in jail at 24.

When
1895 – 1900
Where
Khunti, Jharkhand, India

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Under British rule, the Munda people of Chotanagpur were losing their traditional system of communal land.1

  • Outsiders took over Munda land with the support of colonial and local authorities, turning tribal farmers into tenants.1
  • Birsa Munda, born on 15 November 1875, was educated at a German mission school and briefly converted to Christianity.1
  • He then founded his own faith, and many Munda people joined him.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • Birsa Munda

    Munda religious and political leader; died in jail in 1900.

  • Munda and Oraon communities

    Who formed the movement.

  • The commissioner and deputy commissioner of Ranchi

    Colonial officials who led the suppression with a force of 150 men.

03

What happened?

Birsa's followers, the Birsaits, declared that their real enemies were the British and the Christian missionaries.1

  • His slogan called for the end of the queen's rule and the establishment of the Munda people's own rule.1
  • On Christmas Eve 1899, his followers tried to burn churches in Ranchi and Singhbhum.1
  • In January 1900, they killed police constables at Etkedih and attacked the Khunti police station.1
  • On 9 January 1900, police fired on Birsa's supporters at Sail Rakab hill; the death toll is unknown.1
  • Birsa was arrested on 3 February 1900, and died in jail on 9 June 1900.1

04

What happened next?

  • Trials: 460 people were charged; 63 were convicted, one was sentenced to death and 39 to transportation for life. Six died in prison, including Birsa.1
  • Land law: in 1908, the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act banned the transfer of tribal land to non-tribal people.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Colonial officials saw a dangerous religious fanatic; his followers saw him as a prophet.1

Debates since

  • Religion or politics? Historians such as K. S. Singh see a millenarian movement rooted in land grievances.2
  • Violence on both sides: Birsaits killed police and tried to burn churches; police fired on gatherings and the state imprisoned hundreds.1
06

The evidence

  • Colonial police and court records.1
  • Munda oral traditions and songs.
07

How is it remembered?

  • Since 2021, his birthday, 15 November, has been marked in India as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas.1
  • Mahasweta Devi's novel Aranyer Adhikar (Right to the Forest) is based on his life.1
08

Why does it matter?

Birsa Munda's movement was a struggle for land, faith and self-rule by an Indigenous people.

  • It led directly to legal protection for tribal land that still matters today.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did the loss of communal land matter so much to the Munda?
  2. Why did Birsa turn against the missionaries?
  3. How should we judge the attacks on churches and police?
  4. Why is Birsa Munda honoured across India today?
