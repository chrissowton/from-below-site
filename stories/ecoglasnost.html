Land & environment

Ecoglasnost

In a country with no open dissent for decades, environmentalists began petitioning against polluting projects. Their march on parliament helped bring down Bulgaria's communist leader.

When
1988 – 1989
Where
Sofia, Bulgaria
Facts checked
2026-11-15

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Bulgaria had been ruled by the communist leader Todor Zhivkov since 1954, with almost no open dissent.1

  • Industrial pollution was severe. In the late 1980s, the city of Ruse suffered chlorine pollution from a factory across the Danube in Romania, and mothers there began protesting.
  • In 1988, activists formed a committee to protect Ruse's environment, the first independent organisation of its kind.
02

Who were the key people?

  • Members of Ecoglasnost

    Environmentalists, scientists and intellectuals.

  • Dimitrina Petrova

    Member of Ecoglasnost's steering committee.

  • Todor Zhivkov

    Bulgaria's communist leader, removed in November 1989.

03

What happened?

Ecoglasnost was founded on 11 April 1989.1

  • It organised petitions and demonstrations against plans to divert the Struma and Mesta rivers and to build the Belene nuclear power plant.1
  • Its reports circulated as samizdat and were given to foreign media.1
  • During an international environmental conference in Sofia in October–November 1989, it held the first unofficial demonstrations allowed under communism.1
  • On 3 November 1989, at least 10,000 people marched to parliament chanting "democracy".1
  • A week later, on 10 November, Zhivkov was removed from power.1

Sources

  1. Wikipedia, 'Ecoglasnost', on its founding, campaigns, the Sofia demonstrations and its role in the fall of Zhivkov. Link ↗
04

What happened next?

  • Opposition: Ecoglasnost became a founding member of the Union of Democratic Forces in December 1989.1
  • Green politics: it gave rise to the Green Party of Bulgaria.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The regime first harassed Ecoglasnost, then allowed its demonstrations while foreign delegates were in Sofia.1

Debates since

  • Who toppled Zhivkov? His removal was carried out by reformers inside the Communist Party; historians debate how much the street protests forced their hand.1
  • Violence: Ecoglasnost was peaceful; police harassed and beat activists at an earlier gathering in Sofia in October 1989.
06

The evidence

  • Ecoglasnost's samizdat reports and "Charter '89".1
07

How is it remembered?

  • Ecoglasnost is remembered as the first mass opposition movement in communist Bulgaria.
08

Why does it matter?

Ecoglasnost shows how environmental protest could open the door to political change under communism.

Questions to think about

  1. Why was environmental protest possible when other protest was not?
  2. How did an international conference give protesters an opportunity?
  3. Why did the march on parliament matter so much?
  4. How do environmental and democratic movements connect?

What other events are like this?

  • 1988Estonia's Singing Revolution — Night after night in June 1988, crowds gathered at Tallinn's Song Festival Grounds to sing forbidden songs. The artist Heinz Valk gave the movement its name: the Singing Revolution.
  • 1988The Candle Demonstration — Thousands of Slovak Catholics stood silently in a square holding candles to ask for religious freedom. The communist police turned water cannon and batons on them — and the protest became a first step towards the Velvet Revolution.
  • 1989Nevada–Semipalatinsk — After 40 years of Soviet nuclear tests near their homes, Kazakhs formed a mass movement led by a poet. Within two years, the test site was closed forever.
