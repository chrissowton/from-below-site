The Nghệ-Tĩnh Soviets

Peasants and workers in central Vietnam rose against French taxes, landlords and mandarins, and set up their own village councils. The French bombed them from the air.

When
1930 – 1931
Where
Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh provinces, Vietnam
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh, in central Vietnam, were poor provinces with a long tradition of rebellion. Under French colonial rule, people paid heavy taxes and suffered from monopolies on salt, alcohol and opium.1

  • Communal land had been taken by rich landowners, and peasants were forced to do unpaid labour (corvée).1
  • The Great Depression caused rice prices to collapse, and harvests failed.
  • Communist groups, which united in 1930 as the Indochinese Communist Party, had organised peasant associations, unions and women's and youth groups in the area.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

The movement began in March 1930 with strikes in Vinh and Bến Thủy.1 Protests spread to the countryside, where peasants demanded a halt to personal taxes, an end to forced labour, and the return of communal lands.1

  • On May Day 1930, French-led native guards fired on demonstrations, killing 27 men, women and children.1
  • Protesters attacked district offices and depots of the hated French alcohol monopoly.1
  • On 12 September 1930, a huge demonstration marched at Hưng Nguyên, near Vinh. French aircraft dropped bombs on the marchers, killing 140 to 200 people.1

Where officials fled, villagers set up their own councils — "soviets" — which cut rents, redistributed communal land, abolished taxes and ran local affairs. Some mandarins and village notables who did not flee were beaten, killed or executed.1

From early 1931, the French redeployed troops and built dozens of military posts. With famine and repression, the movement collapsed by the second half of 1931.1

04

What happened next?

  • Repression: thousands were killed, imprisoned or exiled. Many communist leaders were arrested, and the party was nearly destroyed.
  • Lessons: the Vietnamese communists later drew lessons from the defeat, building a broader national front that eventually led the revolution of 1945.
  • French reform: the uprising led to some debate in France about colonial policy, and the use of bombing against unarmed demonstrators was banned.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The French blamed communist agitators. The communists saw the soviets as the first experiment in revolutionary power.

Historians' debates

  • Peasant or party? Historians debate how far the movement was organised by the Communist Party and how far it was driven by peasants' own grievances.
  • Violence: rebels killed and beat local officials and notables; French and native forces killed far more, including by aerial bombing.1
  • Official memory: Vietnamese official history celebrates the soviets; some historians note that party leaders at the time criticised the movement as premature.

Primary source: Newspaper proclamation

What is it?
Newspaper proclamation
Made by, and when
Thanh-Nghệ-Tĩnh News, Vinh, 1930

A proclamation to the people of Nghệ-Tĩnh by a Vietnamese mandarin serving the French, printed in a local newspaper during the rising. It praises France as a source of learning and progress, and urges the young to follow its guidance.

Questions to ask of this source

  1. Why would the colonial authorities ask a Vietnamese mandarin, not a Frenchman, to address the people?
  2. Why does the proclamation talk about 'civilisation' and 'progress' rather than taxes and land?
  3. How might peasants who had set up their own councils have reacted to it?
07

How is it remembered?

  • The Nghệ-Tĩnh Soviets Museum in Vinh commemorates the uprising.
  • 12 September is commemorated in Vietnam, and streets in many cities are named after the soviets.
08

Why does it matter?

The Nghệ-Tĩnh soviets were one of the most important peasant uprisings in colonial Asia. Villagers briefly governed themselves, redistributing land and abolishing taxes.

  • It shows how colonial taxes, monopolies and landlessness could drive peasants to revolt.
  • It is remembered in Vietnam as a founding moment of the revolution.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did peasants in Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh rise up in 1930?
  2. What did the village soviets do?
  3. How should we judge violence against local officials?
  4. Why did the French use aircraft against demonstrators?
