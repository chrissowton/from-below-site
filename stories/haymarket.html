The Haymarket Affair

At a rally for the eight-hour day, someone threw a bomb at the police. Eight anarchists were convicted without evidence that any of them threw it, and four were hanged. Their deaths gave the world May Day.

When
4 May 1886
Where
Haymarket Square, Chicago, United States

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

In 1884, American unions set 1 May 1886 as the date by which the eight-hour day should become standard.1

  • On 1 May 1886, huge strikes began across the United States, with Chicago at the centre.
  • Chicago had a strong anarchist movement among mostly immigrant workers, around the German-language newspaper Arbeiter-Zeitung.1
  • On 3 May, police fired on strikers at the McCormick Harvesting Machine plant, killing two.1
02

Who were the key people?

  • August Spies

    Editor of the Arbeiter-Zeitung; hanged in 1887.

  • Albert Parsons

    Labour organiser; hanged in 1887.

  • Lucy Parsons

    Albert's wife, who campaigned for the defendants for decades.

  • John Peter Altgeld

    Illinois governor who pardoned the surviving defendants.

03

What happened?

On 4 May, a peaceful rally was held in Haymarket Square to protest the McCormick killings.1

  • As police moved to break up the meeting, an unknown person threw a dynamite bomb at them.1
  • The bomb and the police gunfire that followed killed seven police officers and at least four civilians; dozens were wounded.1
  • Eight anarchists were convicted of conspiracy. The evidence suggested none of them threw the bomb, and only two were present.1
  • Seven were sentenced to death. Two sentences were commuted, one man killed himself in jail, and four were hanged on 11 November 1887.1

04

What happened next?

  • Pardon: in 1893, Governor Altgeld pardoned the surviving defendants and criticised the trial.1
  • May Day: 1 May became International Workers' Day across the world in memory of the Haymarket martyrs.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Much of the press blamed "foreign anarchists"; workers across the world saw the defendants as martyrs.1

Debates since

  • Who threw the bomb? The bomber has never been identified.12
  • Violence on both sides: some Chicago anarchists openly advocated armed action and made bombs; police had killed strikers the day before, and many of the civilian victims may have been hit by police gunfire.12
06

The evidence

  • Trial records and the speeches of the defendants.2
07

How is it remembered?

  • A monument stands at the defendants' grave in Forest Park, Illinois, and a sculpture was placed at the Haymarket site in 2004.1
08

Why does it matter?

Haymarket is a story of the fight for the eight-hour day, of police violence, and of an unfair trial.

  • It is the origin of May Day, celebrated by workers worldwide.1

Questions to think about

  1. Why was the eight-hour day such a powerful demand?
  2. Why were the anarchists convicted without proof that they threw the bomb?
  3. How should we judge the use of violence by some anarchists and by the police?
  4. Why is May Day a holiday in many countries but not the United States?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 1912The Bread and Roses Strike — More than twenty thousand migrant textile workers, speaking dozens of languages, shut down the mills of Lawrence and won.
  • 1914The Ludlow Massacre and the Colorado Coalfield War — Striking coal miners and their families were living in a tent colony when National Guard soldiers and company guards attacked. Two women and eleven children died in a pit beneath a burning tent. The armed miners' revenge turned a strike into a war.
  • 1919The Winnipeg General Strike — For six weeks, 30,000 workers brought Canada's third-largest city to a halt to win the right to collective bargaining. It ended with 'Bloody Saturday' — and transformed Canadian politics.
