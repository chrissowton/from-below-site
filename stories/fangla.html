The Fang La Rebellion

When the Song emperor's demand for rare plants and rocks added to crushing taxes, a lacquer grower led the people of the south-east in revolt. Within months they held dozens of counties.

When
1120 – 1121
Where
Shezhou and Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

Under Emperor Huizong of the Song dynasty, the people of the south-east faced heavy taxes and the concentration of land in a few hands.1

  • The emperor's agents collected rare plants and rocks for his gardens, seizing them from local people.
  • Fang La owned a lacquer plantation in Qingxi county, Shezhou.1

03

What happened?

In late 1120, Fang La rose in Shezhou against the Song government.1

  • The rebels quickly took Hangzhou, one of the richest cities in China.1
  • At their height, they controlled six prefectures and 52 counties.1
  • The Song sent a large army; the rebels were defeated.1
  • Fang La was captured and executed in Kaifeng in 1121.1

04

What happened next?

  • The Song weakened: in 1127, the Jurchen Jin dynasty captured Kaifeng and Emperor Huizong.
  • In fiction: the novel Water Margin makes the outlaws of Liangshan fight against Fang La.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Song officials described Fang La as the leader of a heretical sect.

Debates since

  • Religion? Some historians link the rebels to Manichaean beliefs; others doubt it.2
  • Violence on both sides: the rebels killed officials and others, and government forces massacred civilians. Sources claim up to two million deaths, which is probably exaggerated.1
06

The evidence

  • Song histories and official reports.
07

How is it remembered?

  • Fang La is best known in China through the novel Water Margin.
08

Why does it matter?

The Fang La rebellion shows how the luxury of a ruler and the poverty of a region could produce a sudden, massive revolt.

  • It also shows the scale of violence in suppression.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did a plantation owner lead a peasant revolt?
  2. How did the emperor's love of gardens contribute to it?
  3. Why did the revolt spread so quickly, and collapse so quickly?
  4. How should we treat the huge casualty figures in the sources?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 880The Huang Chao Rebellion — A salt smuggler who had failed the imperial examinations led an army of the poor across China and captured the Tang capital. The rebellion was crushed, but the Tang dynasty never recovered.
  • 1351The Red Turban Rebellions — Labourers conscripted to repair the Yellow River's banks rose against the Mongol Yuan dynasty, inspired by the promise of a coming Buddha. From their ranks rose a former beggar-monk who founded the Ming dynasty.
  • 209 BCEThe Dazexiang Uprising — Nine hundred conscripts held up by floods faced execution for being late. Their leaders asked: 'Are kings, generals and ministers born to their station?' The first great peasant revolt in Chinese history helped bring down the Qin empire.
