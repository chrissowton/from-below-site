'Fifty Years Is Too Long': Togo's Protests against a Dynasty

One family had ruled Togo for half a century, father then son. In 2017, protests spread even to the family's northern heartland. The government cut the internet, and the president clung on by promising term limits that would not count his past terms.

What is the background to this event?

Gnassingbé Eyadéma ruled Togo until his death in 2005, when his son, Faure Gnassingbé, was installed in power with the army's support.12

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 19 August 2017, thousands protested, mostly in Sokodé, demanding a return to the 1992 constitution. Some chanted "Fifty years is too long".1

  • Security forces shot two civilians dead. Protesters burned security vehicles and killed seven security men. About 27 people were arrested, and some later said they were tortured.1
  • From then on, the opposition held protests almost every week. Amnesty International estimated that about 100,000 people protested on 6 September.1
  • The government repeatedly cut off or slowed the internet. A photograph of a cow killed by soldiers in a village spread online and became a symbol of the protests.1
  • Amnesty International reported that security forces killed a 9-year-old boy in Mango; the security minister blamed the protesters.1
  • Parliament proposed a limit of two presidential terms, but not counting past terms. The opposition rejected this and boycotted the vote.1
04

What happened next?

  • Term limits — from now on: in May 2019, parliament passed the two-term limit, but it did not count Gnassingbé's earlier terms. He won again in 2020.12
  • A new system: in 2024, a new constitution moved most of the president's power to a "president of the Council of Ministers". Gnassingbé took the post in May 2025.2
  • New protests: from June 2025, protests against Gnassingbé grew again.2
05

Why does it matter?

Togo's protests show how a long-ruling family can survive mass opposition by changing the rules — and how protesters used social media even when the internet was cut off.12

  • In 2019 parliament passed a two-term limit that did not count the president's earlier terms; he won again in 2020.12
  • A new constitution moved most power to a new post, which Gnassingbé took in 2025.2
  • From June 2025, protests grew again.2

What do others say?How it was seen at the time, and where historians disagree

At the time

The government called the opposition's general strike "the campaign of terror, intimidation and threats"; protesters said they were demanding the constitution they had voted for in 1992.1

Debates since

  • Violence: at least 16 people were killed, including two soldiers, according to Wikipedia's summary; seven security men were killed in August 2017. The government and the opposition disputed several deaths.1
