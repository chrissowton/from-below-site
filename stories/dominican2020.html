The Suspended Election: Protests at the Plaza de la Bandera

When electronic voting failed and municipal elections were suspended on polling day, young Dominicans called a protest on social media. It became one of the largest demonstrations in the country's history.

When
16 February – 12 March 2020
Where
Plaza de la Bandera, Santo Domingo, and cities across the Dominican Republic
Facts checked

01

What is the background to this event?

The Dominican Liberation Party (PLD) had governed the Dominican Republic since 2004.2

  • Municipal elections were due on 16 February 2020. In 18 municipalities, which held about 62% of the votes, voting was to be done electronically.1
  • The night before, opposition parties complained that technicians from the Central Electoral Board had worked on the voting machines without party delegates present.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On the morning of 16 February 2020, the Central Electoral Board halted the municipal elections because of failures in the electronic voting system — the first time in the country's history that elections had been suspended.1

  • That night, people gathered outside the Board's headquarters in Santo Domingo. The next day, the Board rescheduled the elections for 15 March, and protesters dressed in black demanded that the Board resign.1
  • Young people used social media to call Dominicans to the Plaza de la Bandera (Flag Square). Protests spread to Santiago and other cities, and to Dominicans abroad.1
  • Two people were shot dead in incidents connected to the elections, one on each side of the main party divide.1
  • On 27 February, Independence Day, thousands gathered at the Plaza. Opposition members of Congress walked out before the president's annual address.1
  • Protesters rejected a dialogue run by a government body and held their own national dialogue. Banging on pots and pans (cacerolazos) disrupted the official talks.1
  • The rescheduled municipal elections took place on 15 March 2020, with turnout of about 45%.3

04

What happened next?

  • Change of government: the general election, postponed by the Covid-19 pandemic, was held on 5 July 2020. Luis Abinader of the opposition Modern Revolutionary Party won in the first round, ending sixteen years of PLD rule.2
  • A peaceful handover: the governing party's candidate, Gonzalo Castillo, conceded, and Abinader was sworn in on 16 August 2020.2
05

What do others say?

At the time

Opposition parties suspected sabotage or fraud; arrests were made on charges of sabotaging the election. The Board blamed technical errors.1

Debates since

  • Electronic voting: the crisis raised the question of whether voting machines can be trusted without close oversight by all parties.
  • Violence: the protests themselves were largely peaceful; the two deaths reported were shootings between party supporters around polling day.1

How is it remembered?

  • The Plaza de la Bandera became a symbol of the protests of February 2020.1
08

Why does it matter?

The Plaza de la Bandera protests showed how quickly trust in an election can collapse — and how a movement led by young people on social media could turn that anger into peaceful pressure before a national vote.12
