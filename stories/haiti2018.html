"Where Did the PetroCaribe Money Go?": Haiti's Anti-Corruption Protests

A rise in fuel prices and a Senate report on missing oil money brought Haitians onto the streets. A cardboard sign asking where the money had gone became a national slogan.

When
July 2018 – 2019
Where
Port-au-Prince, Les Cayes, Jacmel, Hinche and towns across Haiti
Facts checked
2026-11-17

01

What is the background to this event?

PetroCaribe was an oil agreement between Venezuela and Caribbean states, presented as a way to fund development in the region. Haiti received its first oil shipment under it in 2008.2

  • A Haitian Senate investigation, released in November 2017, found that significant corruption under the governments of René Préval and Michel Martelly (2008–2016) had been funded with Venezuelan loans through PetroCaribe.1
  • President Jovenel Moïse had come to power in a 2016 election in which only about 21% of voters took part.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 6 July 2018, the government announced increases in fuel prices of up to 51%. Protests and rioting followed.1

  • The government suspended the price rises, and on 14 July Prime Minister Jack Guy Lafontant resigned.1
  • In mid-August 2018, Gilbert Mirambeau Jr. posted a photograph of himself, blindfolded, holding a cardboard sign that asked in Haitian Creole where the PetroCaribe money had gone. The hashtag #PetroCaribeChallenge spread on social media, and Haitian media carried the message offline.1
  • Protests grew through the autumn, with clashes in Les Cayes, Jacmel and Saint-Marc. A week of protests in November 2018 left 10 people dead.1
  • In February 2019, after a court report on the Senate investigation, protests broke out again. The opposition, led by Jean-Charles Moïse, refused offers of dialogue and called a general strike to force the president to resign.1
  • The opposition also accused the government of involvement in a massacre in La Saline, a district of Port-au-Prince, and took its case to the UN Secretary-General.1

04

What happened next?

  • Deadlock: protests continued through 2019 and into 2021, when protesters argued that Moïse's term had ended. Between 2017 and 2021, with the government deadlocked, gangs gained power through links to politicians and business.1
  • Assassination: on 7 July 2021, President Moïse was assassinated. A group of foreign mercenaries was accused of the killing.1
  • Gangs: in 2022, gangs blockaded Haiti's largest fuel depot. In March 2024, acting Prime Minister Ariel Henry announced his resignation under pressure from protesters, gangs and foreign governments.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

President Moïse called for dialogue, arguing that the country's problems were social and economic as well as political; the opposition refused to talk until he resigned.1

Debates since

  • Who was the campaign for? Shearon Roberts, cited by Wikipedia, argues that the online campaign also signalled to the outside world that a push for a change of government was under way.1
  • Violence: deaths were counted protest by protest — for example, 10 in a week in November 2018 — and no overall official figure is given in our sources.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • The question on Mirambeau's sign, "Kot kòb PetwoKaribe a?", became the slogan of the movement, carried from social media onto the streets by Haitian media.1
08

Why does it matter?

The PetroCaribe protests showed how a single question — where did the money go? — could unite people against corruption. Their failure to bring accountability is part of the story of how Haiti's state collapsed.1

Questions to think about

  1. Why did a simple question on a cardboard sign spread so quickly?

  3. Why did the protests not lead to anyone being held to account?
