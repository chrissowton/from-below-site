The Kaivarta Revolt

Led by a chieftain named Divya, the Kaivartas — a community of fishermen, boatmen and farmers treated as low caste — rose against the Pala king of Bengal, killed him and ruled their own state for about half a century.

When
Late 11th century
Where
Varendra, northern Bengal (now Bangladesh and West Bengal)
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The Pala dynasty ruled Bengal from the eighth to the twelfth century, and was known as a great patron of Buddhism.1

  • In early sources, the Kaivartas were classed as low, associated with fishing and boating, but in Varendra they also included farmers and landholders.1
  • By the eleventh century, the Pala state was weakening, and King Mahipala II was unpopular.1

03

What happened?

Divya, a Kaivarta chieftain who held high office, possibly as commander of the royal army, called on the people of Varendra to rise.1

  • The rebels quickly captured Varendra.1
  • King Mahipala II was killed, and the Pala armies were forced to retreat.1
  • Divya declared Varendra a separate state.1
  • Divya, Rudok and Bhima ruled Varendra for about half a century.1
  • Eventually, Ramapala, with the help of many local chiefs, defeated and killed Bhima and retook Varendra.
04

What happened next?

  • Pala restoration: the Palas regained Varendra, but the dynasty declined and was replaced by the Senas in the twelfth century.
  • Remembrance: the stone pillar at Dibar Dighi is traditionally linked to the Kaivarta kings.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Our main source, the Ramacharitam, was written by a poet at the Pala court and presents the Kaivartas as usurpers.1

Debates since

  • Peasant revolt? Some historians call it the first peasant revolt in Indian history; others argue it began as a rebellion of feudal lords who mobilised the peasants, and only later became a mass uprising.1
  • Violence: the rebels killed the king; the Palas later killed Bhima and reconquered the region.1
06

The evidence

  • The Ramacharitam of Sandhyakar Nandi and copper-plate inscriptions.1
  • The Dibar pillar.
07

How is it remembered?

  • In Bangladesh, the Kaivarta revolt is remembered as an early popular uprising.
08

Why does it matter?

The Kaivarta revolt has been called the first peasant revolt in Indian history, and shows a community treated as low caste taking power.1

09

What other events are like this?

  • 209 BCEThe Dazexiang Uprising — Nine hundred conscripts held up by floods faced execution for being late. Their leaders asked: 'Are kings, generals and ministers born to their station?' The first great peasant revolt in Chinese history helped bring down the Qin empire.
  • 1927The Mahad Satyagraha — B. R. Ambedkar led thousands of Dalits to drink from a public water tank that caste Hindus had forbidden them to touch — a founding moment of the struggle against untouchability.
  • 1381The Peasants' Revolt — Tens of thousands of villagers and townspeople marched on London to demand an end to serfdom. For a few days, the government of England was at their mercy.
