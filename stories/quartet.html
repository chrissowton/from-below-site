The Tunisian National Dialogue Quartet

When Tunisia's revolution seemed about to collapse into violence, a trade union, employers, human rights campaigners and lawyers brought the rival parties to the table. They won the Nobel Peace Prize.

When
2013 – 2014
Where
Tunis, Tunisia

01

What is the background to this event?

In December 2010, the self-immolation of Mohamed Bouazizi sparked the revolution that overthrew President Ben Ali in January 2011.1

  • The Islamist party Ennahda led the new government.
  • In 2013, the assassinations of two left-wing politicians, Chokri Belaïd and Mohamed Brahmi, led to a political crisis, and work on a new constitution stopped.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In the summer of 2013, four civil society organisations formed the National Dialogue Quartet.1

  • They brought the Ennahda-led government and the opposition into negotiations.1
  • The government agreed to hand power to a caretaker administration.
  • In January 2014, Tunisia adopted a new democratic constitution.1

04

What happened next?

  • Nobel Prize: on 9 October 2015, the Quartet was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.1
  • Reversal: in 2021, President Kais Saied suspended parliament and later replaced the 2014 constitution.
05

What do others say?

At the time

Some in Ennahda feared the dialogue was a way to force them from power; the opposition feared Islamist rule.1

Debates since

  • The UGTT: the union's long history of protest, including the 1978 general strike, gave it the authority to act as a mediator.1
  • Violence: the crisis was triggered by political assassinations; the Quartet's work was entirely peaceful.1

07

How is it remembered?

  • The Nobel Prize made the Quartet a symbol of the hopes of the Arab Spring.
08

Why does it matter?

The Quartet showed how civil society — including a trade union — could rescue a democratic transition.

  • Its achievements also show that democracy needs constant defence.
