The March First Movement

Koreans read out a declaration of independence and poured into the streets in peaceful protests against Japanese rule. Up to two million people took part, and thousands were killed.

When
1 March 1919 – 1921
Where
Tapgol Park, Seoul, South Korea

01

What is the background to this event?

Japan annexed Korea in 1910.1

  • Japan took control of Korea's economy and began forcing Koreans to assimilate.1
  • Land was confiscated from Koreans and given to Japanese people.1
  • Resistance was violently suppressed, and speech and the press were tightly controlled.1
  • After the First World War, US President Woodrow Wilson's call for national self-determination inspired Koreans.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

On 1 March 1919, the Korean Declaration of Independence was read out in Seoul, at the T'aehwagwan restaurant and in Tapgol Park.1

  • The movement spread rapidly: there were around 1,500 to 1,800 protests, with 0.8 to 2 million participants out of a population of about 16–17 million.1
  • The protests were largely peaceful, but were often violently suppressed.1
  • At Jeamni, Japanese authorities were seen destroying evidence of a massacre.1
  • Yu Gwan-sun took part in protests in Seoul and was arrested, and became a national symbol.1

04

What happened next?

  • Repression: one Korean estimate in 1920 claimed 7,509 deaths and 46,948 arrests; Japanese figures were much lower.1
  • Provisional Government: the movement led to the creation of the Korean Provisional Government.1
  • "Cultural rule": Japan granted limited cultural freedoms.1
  • Inspiration: the movement inspired China's May Fourth Movement and Indian satyagraha.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Japan ran a global disinformation campaign, denying the protests or calling them Bolshevik uprisings.1

Debates since

  • Memory: South Korea celebrates 1 March as a national holiday; North Korea now stresses the role of the Kim family.1
  • Violence: the protests were largely peaceful; the Japanese authorities killed and arrested thousands, and the numbers remain disputed.1

The evidence

33 national representatives, 1 March 1919

The Declaration of Independence read out on 1 March 1919. Copies were printed in secret and spread across Korea, so that protests broke out in many places at once.

07

How is it remembered?

  • 1 March is a national holiday in South Korea.1
08

Why does it matter?

The March First Movement is a landmark in Korean history and one of the largest non-violent movements against colonial rule.1

Questions to think about

  1. Why did Koreans choose peaceful protest?
  2. How did Wilson's ideas inspire colonised peoples?
  3. Why did Japan try to hide the repression?
  4. Why is Yu Gwan-sun remembered as a symbol?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 1919The May Fourth Movement — When the peace conference at Versailles handed German territory in China to Japan, Beijing students took to the streets. Workers and merchants joined them in strikes, and China refused to sign the treaty.
  • 1980The Gwangju Uprising — Students and citizens in Gwangju rose against a military coup. Paratroopers killed hundreds, but the uprising became the foundation of South Korean democracy.
  • 1947The February 28 Incident — A dispute over contraband cigarettes sparked an island-wide uprising against a corrupt new government. The crackdown that followed killed tens of thousands and was not spoken of publicly for decades.
