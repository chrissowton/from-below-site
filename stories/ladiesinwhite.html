The Ladies in White

After 75 dissidents were jailed in Cuba's 'Black Spring', their wives, mothers and daughters began walking silently through Havana every Sunday, dressed in white.

When

Where

01

What is the background to this event?

In March 2003, during what became known as the "Black Spring", the Cuban government arrested 75 human rights activists, independent journalists and librarians.1

  • They were tried quickly and sentenced to up to 28 years in prison.1
  • The government accused them of working for the United States; human rights groups said they were jailed for peaceful expression.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Two weeks after the arrests, relatives of the prisoners, led by Laura Pollán, began gathering at St Rita's church in Havana on Sundays.1

  • After Mass, they walked silently through the streets, dressed in white as a symbol of peace.1
  • Each woman wore a badge with a photo of her imprisoned relative and the length of his sentence.1
  • Their tactics echoed the Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo in Argentina.1
  • The government called them a subversive group backed by the United States, and organised counter-protests.1

04

What happened next?

  • Recognition: in 2005, the group received the European Parliament's Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought.1
  • Releases: by 2011, after negotiations involving the Catholic Church and Spain, the remaining Black Spring prisoners had been freed, most into exile.2
  • Continuing protest: the group continued to march for other political prisoners, and members were often detained.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The Cuban government said the prisoners and their supporters served US interests; international human rights groups called them prisoners of conscience.12

Debates since

  • Foreign links: supporters of the Cuban government point to US funding of some dissident groups; human rights organisations say peaceful expression should never be punished.1
  • Violence: the Ladies in White have been non-violent; they have faced harassment, beatings by government supporters and frequent short detentions.2

How is it remembered?

08

Why does it matter?

The Ladies in White show the power of silent, persistent protest by women on behalf of prisoners.

What other events are like this?

  • 1977The Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo — The mothers of Argentina's 'disappeared' walked in silent circles in front of the presidential palace, in open defiance of a military dictatorship.
  • 1995The Saturday Mothers — Every Saturday at noon, mothers of people who disappeared in state custody sit silently in an Istanbul square holding their photographs. They have kept coming for decades, despite bans and arrests.
  • 1995The Genocide Widows of Rwanda — After the genocide against the Tutsi in 1994, fifty widows founded an association to help each other survive. Survivors' groups led by women went on to demand justice, health care and recognition of sexual violence as a crime of genocide.
