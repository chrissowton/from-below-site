Disability rights

The League of the Physically Handicapped

During the Great Depression, disabled New Yorkers were stamped 'unemployable' and refused government jobs. They occupied the relief offices for nine days — decades before the modern disability rights movement.

When
May 1935 – 1938
Where
New York City, United States

01

What is the background to this event?

During the Great Depression, the Works Progress Administration (WPA) created millions of jobs for the unemployed.1

  • New York City's Home Relief Bureau stamped disabled applicants' forms "PH" — physically handicapped.1
  • They were marked as "unemployable" and denied WPA jobs.1

03

What happened?

In May 1935, disabled New Yorkers formed the League of the Physically Handicapped to protest discrimination by the WPA.1

  • On 29 May 1935, members began a sit-in at the Home Relief Bureau that lasted nine days.1
  • In June 1935, they held a weekend sit-in at the WPA headquarters in New York.1
  • They demanded jobs, not charity.

04

What happened next?

  • Jobs: the protests led to the creation of 1,500 jobs for physically disabled workers in New York City in 1936.1
  • The end: the League had dissolved by 1938.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Officials saw disabled people as needing relief, not work; the League argued they had the right to work.2

Debates since

  • New disability history: Paul Longmore and David Goldberger used the League to show that disabled people organised politically long before the 1970s.2
  • Non-violence: the League used peaceful sit-ins and pickets.1
06

The evidence

  • Newspaper reports and the League's own records, studied by Longmore and Goldberger.2

07

How is it remembered?

  • The League is now seen as a forerunner of the sit-ins of Section 504 and the Capitol Crawl.
08

Why does it matter?

The League was one of the first disability rights protests in the United States.

  • It challenged the idea that disabled people were "unemployable" and should depend on charity.2

Questions to think about

  1. Why did the 'PH' stamp matter so much?
  2. Why did the League use sit-ins?
  3. Why did they demand jobs rather than charity?
  4. Why was the League forgotten until historians rediscovered it?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 1977The Section 504 Sit-in — More than a hundred disabled people occupied a federal building for 26 days until the government signed the first major civil rights regulations for disabled Americans.
  • 1920The Blind Marchers of 1920 — Blind workers organised in their own trade union marched on London from three directions, demanding 'rights not charity'. Their march won the first disability law in Britain — and inspired the Jarrow March.
  • 1993The Disabled People's Direct Action Network — Disabled activists chained their wheelchairs to buses, blocked traffic and picketed charity telethons, demanding 'rights not charity'. Their direct action helped force Britain to pass its first disability discrimination law.
