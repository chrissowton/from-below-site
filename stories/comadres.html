CoMadres

Mothers of the disappeared and murdered in El Salvador formed a committee to demand the truth. Death squads bombed their office and tortured their members, but they kept occupying offices and marching.

01

What is the background to this event?

In the 1970s, political violence grew in El Salvador between left-wing guerrillas and right-wing death squads allied with the military.1

  • Many people were imprisoned, disappeared or killed for political reasons.1
  • Women whose husbands and children were taken became heads of their families and began to organise.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

CoMadres — the Committee of Mothers and Relatives of Political Prisoners, Disappeared and Assassinated of El Salvador — was founded in 1977.1

  • It began with about nine members and grew to more than 500 by 1993.1
  • Members occupied government offices and the United Nations office in San Salvador, and held sit-ins at the Ministry of Justice and in churches.1
  • They used television, radio and newspapers to denounce disappearances.1
  • Members themselves were imprisoned, tortured and raped.1

04

What happened next?

  • Award: in 1984, CoMadres received the Robert F. Kennedy Human Rights Award.1
  • Peace: the civil war ended with peace agreements in 1992; a UN Truth Commission found that most abuses were committed by the state and its allies.
  • Continuing work: CoMadres went on campaigning for justice after the war.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government and death squads labelled the mothers as subversives; supporters saw them as defenders of human rights.1

Debates since

  • Not a feminist start: historians such as Lynn Stephen note that CoMadres grew from urgent need rather than feminist ideas, but came to address women's rights too.12
  • Violence on both sides: the civil war involved killings by guerrillas as well as state forces; CoMadres itself was non-violent and was a target of state terror.1

How is it remembered?

  • The Monument to Memory and Truth in San Salvador lists tens of thousands of civilian victims of the war.
08

Why does it matter?

CoMadres shows the courage of women who demanded the truth from a state that was killing its own people.
