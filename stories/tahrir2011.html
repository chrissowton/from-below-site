Tahrir Square and the Egyptian Revolution

Weeks after Tunisia's president fell, young Egyptians called a protest on National Police Day. Millions followed, Tahrir Square became a city within a city, and after eighteen days Hosni Mubarak resigned after thirty years in power.

When
25 January – 11 February 2011
Where

01

What is the background to this event?

Hosni Mubarak had ruled Egypt since 1981 under emergency laws.

  • Police brutality, corruption, unemployment and rising food prices fuelled anger.1
  • In 2010, the death of Khaled Said after a police beating sparked a campaign, "We are all Khaled Said".1
  • Opposition groups studied non-violent action and worked with leaders of Serbia's Otpor movement.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

Youth groups chose 25 January 2011, National Police Day, for a protest against police brutality.1

  • Millions joined demonstrations, occupations, strikes and civil disobedience across Egypt, demanding Mubarak's removal.1
  • Tahrir Square in Cairo was occupied by protesters.1
  • On 2 February, Mubarak supporters rode camels and horses into the square; three people were killed and 600 injured.1
  • On 11 February, Mubarak resigned and handed power to the army.1

04

What happened next?

  • Elections: Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood was elected president in 2012.1
  • Coup: after mass protests, the army removed Morsi in July 2013; General Abdel Fattah el-Sisi became president in 2014.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

The government blamed foreign plots; protesters called for "bread, freedom and social justice".

Debates since

  • A revolution betrayed? Many argue the army used the revolution to keep power.1
  • Violence on both sides: at least 846 people were killed and over 6,000 injured, overwhelmingly by the security forces; protesters burned more than 90 police stations.1

How is it remembered?

08

Why does it matter?

Tahrir Square became a worldwide symbol of the Arab Spring — and of how hard it is to turn a revolution into democracy.

What other events are like this?

  • 2010The Tunisian Revolution — A street vendor's desperate protest in a small town sparked a month of uprising that toppled a dictator of 23 years and began the Arab Spring.
  • 1919Egypt's 1919 Revolution — When Britain exiled the nationalist leader Saad Zaghlul, Egyptians of every class and religion rose up in strikes and demonstrations — and women marched in public for the first time.
  • 2008Mahalla and the April 6 Strike — Women textile workers walked out of Egypt's largest factory, starting a wave of strikes. Their call for a general strike on 6 April 2008 spread through Facebook — and helped create the movement that toppled Mubarak three years later.
