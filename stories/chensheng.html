The Dazexiang Uprising

Nine hundred conscripts held up by floods faced execution for being late. Their leaders asked: 'Are kings, generals and ministers born to their station?' The first great peasant revolt in Chinese history helped bring down the Qin empire.

When
209 – 208 BCE
Where
Dazexiang (Big Swamp Village), Anhui, China
Facts checked
2026-11-16

See it on the map →

01

What is the background to this event?

The Qin dynasty united China in 221 BCE under its first emperor, Qin Shi Huang. It ruled through harsh laws, heavy taxes and forced labour on huge projects.1

  • After the first emperor died in 210 BCE, his son Huhai took the throne, having had his elder brother Fusu killed.1
  • Qin law ordered execution for those who arrived late for government service, whatever the reason.1

In their own words

03

What happened?

In 209 BCE, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang were leading about 900 conscripts north to Yuyang when floods stopped them at Dazexiang.1

  • Facing execution for being late, they decided to rebel.1
  • To win support, they placed a silk message saying "Chen Sheng will be king" inside a fish, and imitated fox cries at night.1
  • The rebels killed their officers and rose in the name of the murdered prince Fusu and of the old kingdom of Chu.1
  • Within months, risings broke out across China, and their forces grew to tens of thousands, mostly peasants.1
  • They could not match the Qin's professional armies. Within a year, both Chen and Wu were killed by their own followers.1

04

What happened next?

  • The fall of Qin: other rebels, including Liu Bang and Xiang Yu, followed their example. The Qin dynasty fell in 206 BCE.1
  • The Han: Liu Bang, himself of humble origins, founded the Han dynasty in 202 BCE.
05

What do others say?

At the time

The historian Sima Qian, writing under the Han, gave Chen Sheng an honoured place among China's great families.2

Debates since

  • Peasant revolution? Twentieth-century Chinese historians hailed it as the first peasant revolution; others note that its leaders soon behaved like kings.
  • Violence: the rebels killed officials and fought a war; they were in turn killed by Qin armies and by rivals within the revolt.1
06

The evidence

  • Sima Qian's Shiji, written about a century later.2
07

How is it remembered?

  • Chen Sheng and Wu Guang appear in Chinese school textbooks, and a memorial stands at Dazexiang.
08

Why does it matter?

The Dazexiang uprising was the first great peasant revolt in Chinese history, and showed that the people could overthrow an empire.

  • Chen Sheng's question — "Are kings and nobles born to it?" — became one of China's most famous radical sayings.

Questions to think about

  1. Why did harsh laws make rebellion more likely?
  2. Why did the rebels use omens and signs?
  3. Why did the uprising fail, yet bring down the Qin?
  4. Why did later Chinese governments celebrate Chen Sheng?
09

What other events are like this?

  • 184 CEThe Yellow Turban Rebellion — Inspired by a Daoist healer who promised a new age of 'Great Peace', hundreds of thousands of peasants rose against the Han dynasty — a revolt that helped to bring the empire down.
  • 18 CEThe Red Eyebrows — When the Yellow River flooded and Wang Mang's land reforms failed, starving farmers painted their eyebrows red to tell friend from foe. Their rebellion helped bring down an emperor — and was joined by a mother avenging her son.
  • 1381The Peasants' Revolt — Tens of thousands of villagers and townspeople marched on London to demand an end to serfdom. For a few days, the government of England was at their mercy.
