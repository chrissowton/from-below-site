Land & environment

You Stink

When rubbish piled up in Beirut's streets because the politicians could not agree on a new landfill, young Lebanese of every sect took to the streets with a simple message for their leaders: 'You Stink'.

When
July 2015 – 2016
Where
Martyrs' Square and Riad Al Solh Square, Beirut, Lebanon

01

What is the background to this event?

Since 1997, waste from Beirut and Mount Lebanon had gone to a single landfill at Naameh, south of Beirut.1

  • Built for 2 million tons, it had received over 15 million tons by 2015.1
  • In July 2015, after local protests, it closed without any alternative, and rubbish collection stopped.1
  • Thousands of tons of waste piled up in the streets.1

03

What happened?

The rubbish crisis sparked protests that quickly became a broader movement.1

  • Protesters demanded political reform, accountability and an end to Lebanon's sectarian power-sharing system.1
  • The movement was non-sectarian and decentralised, organised through social media.1
  • On 22 and 23 August 2015, security forces used tear gas, water cannon, rubber bullets and batons against protesters in central Beirut.1
  • Human rights organisations criticised the excessive use of force.1

04

What happened next?

  • Beirut Madinati: the movement inspired the Beirut Madinati campaign in the 2016 municipal elections.1
  • 2019: it is seen as a forerunner of the October 2019 uprising.1
05

What do others say?

At the time

Politicians blamed each other or outside agitators; protesters blamed the whole political class.1

Debates since

  • Success? The protests did not bring institutional reform, but strengthened Lebanese civil society.1
  • Violence: most protesters were peaceful, though some clashed with security forces; police used force against crowds.1

How is it remembered?

  • The slogan "You Stink" returned during the 2019 uprising.
08

Why does it matter?

You Stink showed that an environmental crisis could unite people across Lebanon's sectarian divides against their political class.

What other events are like this?

  • 2019Lebanon's 17 October Revolution — A tax on WhatsApp calls sparked a nationwide uprising against a political system built on sectarian division. 'All of them means all of them,' protesters chanted — demanding the removal of the entire political class.
  • 2009The Pots and Pans Revolution — When Iceland's banks collapsed, a singer stood alone in front of parliament with a microphone. Within months, thousands were banging pots and pans, and the government fell.
  • 2015Electric Yerevan — When electricity prices rose again, thousands of young Armenians sat down in the main avenue of Yerevan under the slogan 'No to Robbery'. Police water cannon only made the protest bigger, and the price rise was reversed.
